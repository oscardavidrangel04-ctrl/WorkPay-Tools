Balance Transfer vs Personal Loan

A balance transfer moves eligible revolving debt to a card; a personal loan creates an installment repayment obligation.

Costs that can change the comparison

Review transfer fees, promotional end dates, purchase APR, loan origination fees and the total repayment schedule.

Read the actual card and loan offers

Compare the current statement, agreement or offer before acting. Focus on the amount owed, APR, fees, required payment, due date and any promotional or service terms that apply to your specific accounts.

Test whether you can finish before a promotion ends

For an illustrative $5,000 transfer with a 3% fee added to the balance, the starting amount becomes $5,150. If a 0% promotion covers that whole amount for 15 monthly payments, paying it off within that window requires about $343.34 per month, with a small final adjustment. The issuer's minimum may be much smaller. Verify the actual offer, fee treatment and dates.

Compare loan proceeds with the debts being replaced

A hypothetical $5,000 loan with a $200 fee deducted delivers only $4,800. It would leave a $200 gap against a $5,000 payoff. If the fee is financed instead, compare the amount borrowed and repayment schedule rather than subtracting it from proceeds.

Check the transfer fee calculation and the written loan disclosure. Do not treat a promotional rate as a promise that you will qualify, receive a sufficient limit or finish repayment before it expires.

Balance Transfer vs Personal Loan FAQ

Is a balance transfer the same as a consolidation loan?

No. A transfer moves eligible debt onto a card. A consolidation loan creates a new loan obligation. Their fees, payment schedules and terms can differ.

Does a 0% transfer offer mean there is no cost?

Not necessarily. A transfer fee or other charges may apply, and a remaining balance can become subject to the applicable post-promotion rate.
