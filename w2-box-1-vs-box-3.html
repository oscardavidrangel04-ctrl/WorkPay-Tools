W-2 Box 1 vs Box 3

W-2 Box 1 reports federal income-tax wages, while Box 3 reports Social Security wages. The amounts can differ because the two boxes follow different inclusion rules and Box 3 is also subject to the annual Social Security wage base.

Reviewed September 15, 2026

Box 1 vs Box 3 at a glance

W-2 boxWhat it generally reportsCommon reason it differs
Box 1Wages, tips and other compensation subject to federal income taxEligible pre-tax salary deferrals can reduce this amount
Box 3Wages subject to Social Security taxSome amounts excluded from Box 1 remain included here; the annual wage base also applies
Box 5Wages subject to Medicare taxUnlike Box 3, Medicare wages do not use the Social Security wage base

Why are Box 1 and Box 3 different on a W-2?

A common reason is a traditional pre-tax 401(k) contribution. The IRS says eligible elective deferrals generally are not included in Box 1 wages for federal income-tax purposes, but they remain included in wages subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes. Other benefits, payroll adjustments and the Social Security wage base can also create differences.

Do not assume the difference always equals your retirement contribution. Compare the W-2 with your year-end pay statement and the codes in Box 12.

Simple W-2 Box 1 and Box 3 example

Suppose an employee earns $60,000 and makes $6,000 in eligible traditional 401(k) elective deferrals. Ignoring other adjustments and annual limits, Box 1 might reflect $54,000 while Box 3 could still reflect $60,000. This example isolates one common reason for a difference; actual reporting can include additional items.

Traditional 401(k) vs Roth 401(k) treatment

Traditional elective deferrals generally reduce current federal income-tax wages in Box 1. Designated Roth 401(k) contributions are made after tax and generally remain included in Box 1. Both types are generally included in Social Security and Medicare wages. See how 401(k) contributions affect taxable income for a fuller comparison.

What to check when the boxes differ

  • Box 12 codes and retirement-plan deferrals
  • Year-end pay-stub taxable-wage totals
  • Employer-provided benefit and payroll explanations
  • Whether annual Social Security wage limits affected Box 3
  • Whether the W-2 may need correction

If an amount appears incorrect, contact the employer or payroll department. WorkPay Tools cannot determine the correct reporting for an individual W-2.

W-2 Box 1 vs Box 3 FAQ

Why are Box 1 and Box 3 different on my W-2?

They use different wage definitions. Traditional pre-tax 401(k) deferrals are one common reason Box 1 may be lower than Box 3.

Is Box 3 my gross pay?

Not necessarily. It is Social Security wages under specific reporting rules, not a universal gross-pay total.

Can Box 1 be higher than Box 3?

It can happen, including when the Social Security wage base limits Box 3. Other reporting differences may also apply.

Does Box 3 equal Box 5?

Not always. Social Security wages have an annual wage base; Medicare wages generally do not use that cap.
