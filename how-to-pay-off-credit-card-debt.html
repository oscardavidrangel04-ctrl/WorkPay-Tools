Debt and credit planning

How to Pay Off Credit Card Debt

List every balance, APR and required payment; keep all accounts current; then send available extra money to one target balance at a time. The statement remains the source for your account terms.

Build a payoff plan before choosing a method

  1. Record each card’s statement balance, purchase APR, minimum payment and due date.
  2. Choose a realistic monthly amount above the combined minimums.
  3. Put that extra amount toward one target card; when it is paid off, roll that freed payment into the next target.

Use the Credit Card Payoff Calculator to compare an estimated balance, APR and payment.

Debt avalanche vs. debt snowball

The avalanche method sends extra money to the highest APR first and usually reduces interest cost. The snowball method sends it to the smallest balance first and can create faster visible progress. Both keep the required payment on every other card current. Compare them in Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball.

Credit card debt payoff checklist

  • Record the current balance and purchase APR from every statement.
  • Confirm the required payment and due date before allocating extra money.
  • Check whether a promotional rate, balance-transfer fee or deferred-interest term applies.
  • Use a debt payoff plan to choose an order and the payoff calculator to model one balance.

When a balance transfer or consolidation may fit

A balance-transfer card or consolidation loan can change the rate or payment structure, but it does not erase the balance. Compare the promotional period, transfer or origination fee, regular APR, monthly payment and what happens after the offer ends.

If you cannot make the minimum payment

Contact the issuer before the due date when possible and ask what hardship or payment options it offers. Review fees, risks and written terms before enrolling in a debt-relief service. Read CFPB guidance.

Credit card debt payoff FAQ

Should I pay more than the minimum?

If it fits your budget and you keep required payments current, an amount above the minimum generally pays down the balance sooner and can reduce interest over time.

Will paying off a card affect my credit score?

Results vary. Lower reported balances can change utilization, while closing an account can affect credit available to you. See whether closing a credit card can hurt your score before deciding to close one.
