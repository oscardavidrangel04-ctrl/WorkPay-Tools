Debt Payoff vs Saving: How to Compare Priorities

The choice depends on required payments, available cash, emergency needs, interest costs and the purpose of the savings.

The short answer

Separate an emergency reserve from a planned purchase fund, then compare the debt terms and the consequences of missing a payment.

Distinguish emergency cash from long-term saving

Cash reserved for an unexpected bill serves a different purpose from money invested for a distant goal. Using every available dollar for debt can leave no cash for the next urgent expense; keeping costly debt indefinitely also has a price.

Use a simple monthly decision worksheet

  1. List essential bills and required debt payments first.
  2. Record accessible cash and likely near-term expenses.
  3. Identify each debt's APR and any changing promotional terms.
  4. Decide how much of the remaining budget goes to a cash buffer and how much to extra debt payments.
  5. Revisit the split after an expense, income change or debt payoff.

For example, a $200 monthly surplus could be divided between the two goals. The appropriate split depends on your circumstances; this illustration is not a recommended allocation. Keep the savings target separate from the required debt payment so neither is accidentally omitted.
