W-2 vs 1099: What’s the Difference?

In the United States, a Form W-2 generally reports wages paid to an employee, while Form 1099-NEC is commonly used to report qualifying nonemployee compensation. The forms reflect different work relationships; they are not simply two interchangeable ways to pay the same worker.

Why the same dollar rate may not be equivalent

A $40 employee hourly rate and a $40 contractor rate are not automatically equal in economic value. A contractor may have unpaid nonbillable time, business expenses and benefits they fund themselves. An employee package may include employer-paid benefits or other compensation in addition to base wages.

Worker classification is not determined by the form alone

The IRS explains that worker status depends on the facts and circumstances, including the degree of control and independence in the relationship. Calling someone a contractor or issuing a particular form does not by itself settle classification.

Official references: IRS: Independent Contractor or Employee? and IRS: Form 1099-NEC and independent contractors.

How to compare an employee offer with contract work

Start with annualized cash pay using realistic working hours. Then identify employer-paid benefits and variable compensation on the employee side. On the contractor side, identify expected billable time and business costs. Keep taxes separate unless you are using a tax tool designed for your specific circumstances.

W-2 vs 1099 FAQ

What is the main difference between W-2 and 1099 pay?

W-2 generally reports employee wages, while Form 1099-NEC is commonly used to report qualifying nonemployee compensation. Worker classification depends on the actual relationship, not just the form name.

Is a 1099 contractor the same as an employee?

No. Employees and independent contractors are different worker classifications, and the correct classification depends on facts and circumstances.

Should a contractor hourly rate match an employee hourly rate?

Not necessarily. A contractor may need to account for unpaid time, business expenses and benefits that an employer might otherwise provide.

Can an employer choose 1099 status just to avoid payroll obligations?

Worker classification is governed by applicable rules and the facts of the relationship; the label alone does not determine status.
