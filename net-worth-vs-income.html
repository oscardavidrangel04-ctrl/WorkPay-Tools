Net Worth vs Income: What Is the Difference?

Understand the difference between income and net worth and why both measurements answer different financial questions.

The short answer

Income is money received over a period, such as wages, business income, interest or other payments. Net worth is the value of assets minus liabilities at a point in time. A higher income does not automatically mean higher net worth, and a net-worth figure does not tell you how much cash is available for a bill today.

What to check

Assets can include cash, investments or property; liabilities can include loans and credit-card balances. Valuation, taxes, selling costs and access restrictions can make a simple net-worth list incomplete. Use consistent dates and reasonable estimates when tracking change over time.

Use the right information

Keep gross income, net pay, account balances and debt balances separate. That avoids using a pay figure as if it were an asset or treating a credit limit as wealth.

Official reference

The CFPB’s budgeting and debt resources distinguish income, debt obligations and savings needs as separate parts of financial planning. Read the source.

Frequently asked questions

What should I use for an account-specific answer?

Use the current account agreement, statement or official plan document. General terms do not override those records.
