Balance transfers

How Long Does a Balance Transfer Take?

A balance transfer can take several business days or longer; it is not instant. Keep making required payments on the old account until the old issuer shows that the transferred amount was received and credited.

Typical balance transfer timeline

Issuers publish different timelines. Some transfers complete within several business days, while a new account, verification step or transfer between institutions can make the process take weeks. The estimate in your approved offer or account dashboard is more useful than a general timeline.

A new card may need to be opened, activated or cleared for transfers before the new issuer sends payment to the old creditor. The transfer is complete only when the old account reflects the credited payment.

What can delay a balance transfer?

  • New-account processing: a newly opened card can have an additional review or waiting period.
  • Incorrect details: an account number, creditor name or payment address error can delay or reject the request.
  • Transfer limit: the requested amount plus any transfer fee may exceed the available amount under the offer.
  • Multiple creditors: each requested transfer can have its own processing path and timing.
  • Payment method: electronic payments, checks and other disbursement methods can move at different speeds.

What to do while a transfer is pending

  1. Keep paying at least the required minimum on the original card by its due date.
  2. Monitor both the old and new account; a pending request does not suspend the old account's due date.
  3. Do not assume a promotional APR applies to new purchases. Review the offer and transaction categories.
  4. Save the confirmation number and the requested transfer amount.

A pending transfer does not stop interest that is already accruing on the old balance. For payment timing, read when a credit card payment is considered late.

How to track your balance transfer

Start with the receiving issuer, since it initiated the transfer. Check the status in the account dashboard or call the number on the card. Then confirm that the old creditor shows a payment or a reduced balance. A new card showing the transferred balance does not by itself prove the old account is fully paid.

Compare the final amount with the amount you requested. The difference can reflect an available-credit limit, a fee, payments you made while waiting or an amount the issuer did not approve for transfer.

What to do if the transfer is delayed

If the transfer has exceeded the timeline stated by the issuer, contact the new issuer and ask whether it needs corrected information or has a status update. Continue required payments to the old creditor until it confirms the payment. Do not rely on a transfer request as a replacement for an upcoming due-date payment.

If a transfer is denied or only partly approved, review the reason and your remaining old-card balance before making another credit application.

After the transfer appears complete

Check for a remaining balance, pending purchases, residual interest or fees on the old account. Record the new card's balance-transfer fee, promotional end date, regular APR after the promotion and minimum-payment schedule. A transfer can move debt, but it does not automatically pay it off.

Balance Transfer Timing FAQ

How long does a balance transfer take?

Several business days or longer. The exact time depends on the issuer, account status, verification and method.

Does a pending transfer count as a payment to the old card?

No, not until the old issuer receives and credits it. Keep making required payments until then.

What should I do if a transfer is delayed?

Check with the new issuer, verify details and keep the old account current. Contact the issuer if it exceeds the stated timeline.

Can a transfer fee use up available credit?

Yes. The fee and transfer amount can count toward the amount available under the offer.
