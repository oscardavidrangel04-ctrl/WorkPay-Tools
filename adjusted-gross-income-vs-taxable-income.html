Adjusted Gross Income vs Taxable Income

Adjusted gross income (AGI) and taxable income are different steps in a U.S. federal income tax return. AGI is total income minus qualifying adjustments; taxable income generally comes after subtracting the standard deduction or allowable itemized deductions and any other applicable deductions.

Line numbers can change in future revisions, so confirm them on the return for the tax year you are filing.

How adjusted gross income is calculated

AGI = total income − eligible adjustments to income

The IRS explains that total income can include wages, tips, interest, dividends, capital gains, business income and retirement income. Adjustments may include qualifying items reported on Schedule 1, such as certain deductible IRA or HSA contributions and student loan interest, when the applicable requirements are met.

AGI is not simply annual salary. Your annual income may include multiple sources, while AGI follows federal tax-return definitions.

How taxable income is determined

Taxable income = AGI − applicable deductions after AGI

In a simplified individual return, the standard deduction or allowable itemized deductions reduce AGI to help determine taxable income. Other return-specific deductions can also apply. Taxable income is then used with the federal tax rules; it is not the same thing as tax owed, withholding or net pay.

Adjusted gross income vs taxable income example

Suppose a filer reports $70,000 of total income and has $2,000 of eligible adjustments. AGI would be $68,000. If $15,000 of deductions applied at the next stage, the simplified taxable-income amount would be $53,000. This illustration explains the sequence only; it does not determine eligibility or reproduce a complete tax return.

Why the difference matters

  • AGI can affect eligibility or limits for certain deductions and credits.
  • Taxable income is the amount used at the later rate-calculation stage.
  • Payroll gross pay, W-2 wages, AGI and taxable income may all differ.
  • A refund or balance due also depends on withholding, estimated payments and credits.

AGI and taxable income FAQ

Is adjusted gross income the same as taxable income?

No. Taxable income is generally calculated after AGI and after additional applicable deductions.

Is taxable income after deductions?

Yes, but deductions occur at different stages. Adjustments help produce AGI; later deductions help produce taxable income.

Is AGI before or after taxes?

AGI is an income measurement used on the return, not an after-tax cash amount.

Where can I find these amounts?

On the current Form 1040 structure, AGI appears on line 11 and taxable income on line 15. Verify the form for your filing year.
