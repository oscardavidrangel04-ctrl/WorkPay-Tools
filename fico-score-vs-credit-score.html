The short answer

Every FICO Score is a credit score, but not every credit score is a FICO Score. Lenders and consumer services may use FICO models, VantageScore models or other risk models.

Why two legitimate scores can differ

Scores can use different credit-bureau data, model versions, industry variants and calculation dates. Compare the model name, version, bureau and dateâ€”not only the number.

What a lender may use

A score shown in an app is educational unless the provider says otherwise. A lender may obtain another score version for a mortgage, auto loan, card or other decision.

How to compare scores correctly

Record the score source, range, model, bureau and date. Then review the underlying reports for differences in balances, account status, inquiries or missing accounts.

Frequently asked questions

Is a FICO Score the same as a credit score?

A FICO Score is one type of credit score. The phrase credit score also includes scores produced by other models.

Why is my app score different from a lender score?

The model, version, bureau data, industry use and calculation date may differ.

Which score is most accurate?

A score is accurate for the model and data used to calculate it. The relevant score depends on what model a particular lender uses.
