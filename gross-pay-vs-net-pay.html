Gross Pay vs Net Pay: What Is the Difference?

See how gross pay and net pay differ, which paycheck items may reduce take-home pay, and why each pay period can vary.

The short answer

Gross pay is compensation before taxes and other deductions. Net pay, often called take-home pay, is the amount remaining after the deductions and withholdings shown on the paycheck. The difference is not automatically an error: it can reflect federal, state or local tax withholding, FICA taxes for Social Security and Medicare, benefit premiums, retirement contributions or other authorized items.

Reconcile the lines between gross and net

Read the individual lines rather than comparing only gross and net totals. An overtime payment, a bonus, a new benefit election or an adjustment can make one pay period look different from the prior one. Some deductions are pre-tax and some are after-tax; the treatment depends on the benefit and applicable rules.

Use the right record

This page explains labels, not a tax calculation. For a personal withholding review, use the current IRS resources or a qualified professional, especially after a major income or household change.

Official reference

The IRS describes gross income per pay period as the total paid before taxes and deductions. Read the source.

Gross-to-net example without estimating taxes

Suppose a pay stub lists $2,000 gross pay, $300 in taxes withheld, $100 in benefit deductions and a $100 employee retirement deduction. The displayed deductions total $500, leaving $1,500 net pay.

These are invented line-item amounts, not tax rates. This subtraction does not tell you how payroll calculated withholding or whether a deduction was pre-tax. Compare the categories on your own pay stub before using the example.

Source: IRS Publication 4268: payroll records.

Frequently asked questions

What is the basic gross-to-net formula?

For a simple pay stub, subtract the employee taxes withheld and other employee deductions from gross pay. Reimbursements and adjustments may require separate reconciliation.

Is net pay the same as taxable wages?

No. Net pay is the amount left after payroll deductions. Taxable wages are amounts used for particular tax calculations and may differ by tax.

Why did my net pay change if gross pay stayed the same?

Compare tax withholding, benefit elections, retirement deductions and adjustments between the two pay periods. A change in one of those lines can change net pay.
