Gross Pay vs Net Pay: What Is the Difference?

See how gross pay and net pay differ, which paycheck items may reduce take-home pay, and why each pay period can vary.

The short answer

Gross pay is compensation before taxes and other deductions. Net pay, often called take-home pay, is the amount remaining after the deductions and withholdings shown on the paycheck. The difference is not automatically an error: it can reflect federal, state or local tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare taxes, benefit premiums, retirement contributions or other authorized items.

What to check

Read the individual lines rather than comparing only gross and net totals. An overtime payment, a bonus, a new benefit election or an adjustment can make one pay period look different from the prior one. Some deductions are pre-tax and some are after-tax; the treatment depends on the benefit and applicable rules.

Use the right record

This page explains labels, not a tax calculation. For a personal withholding review, use the current IRS resources or a qualified professional, especially after a major income or household change.

Official reference

The IRS describes gross income per pay period as the total paid before taxes and deductions. Read the source.
