How to Calculate a Debt Snowball Payment

Learn the payment-allocation idea behind a debt snowball plan.

The basic method

A debt snowball plan generally directs extra money to the smallest selected balance while required minimums continue on other debts. Actual interest, fees and payment timing affect payoff results.

Calculate the extra amount available each month

This page works through payment allocation. For choosing the payoff order and understanding its trade-offs, start with the debt snowball method.

Extra amount = monthly debt-payment budget minus all required minimum payments.

Suppose you can allocate $300 this month. Three debts have minimums of $30, $50 and $70, totaling $150. That leaves $150 extra. If the debt with the $30 minimum has the smallest balance, its planned payment is $180; the other two receive $50 and $70. The total remains $300.

Check the allocation before paying

  1. Use current balances to identify the smallest debt and current statements for each minimum.
  2. Subtract all minimums from the amount you can afford for debt payments.
  3. Add the available extra amount to the target debt, without paying more than its actual payoff amount.
  4. After a payoff, update the balances and minimums before allocating the next payment.

If the budget is below the total minimums, there is no extra amount to allocate. Contact creditors about available assistance rather than treating a negative result as a payment plan.

This is a monthly allocation example, not a payoff-date or interest estimate. Interest, fees and new activity can change the required amounts. Try the debt snowball payment calculator with your own figures.

Method reference: CFPB guidance on debt-reduction methods.

Practical sequence

List each balance and its minimum payment, keep all minimums current, and apply the extra amount to the selected smallest balance. Recalculate after each payoff because balances and required payments change.

Question to check

Is snowball always the lowest-cost method?

Not necessarily. It prioritizes balance order; an avalanche method prioritizes APR.
