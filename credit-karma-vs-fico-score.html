Credit Karma vs FICO Score: Why Scores Differ

Credit Karma generally provides educational VantageScore information, while many lenders use one of several FICO Score versions. They are not interchangeable measurements.

Credit Karma and FICO use different models

Credit Karma and a lender can start with credit-bureau information but calculate scores using different model rules. A numerical difference does not by itself show an error.

The bureau and date also matter

Check whether each score uses Equifax, TransUnion or Experian data and when it was updated. Account balances and report changes may reach bureaus on different dates.

Use each score for its stated purpose

An educational score can help monitor direction and report changes. It does not promise the score or decision a lender will use.

Investigate a surprising difference

Compare the model label, range, bureau and date, then review the reports for missing or inconsistent information. Dispute only information you believe is inaccurate.

Official reference

Review current information from Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This guide is educational, not legal, lending or individualized financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is Credit Karma a FICO Score?

Credit Karma generally labels the VantageScore model and bureau associated with the score it displays; it should not be assumed to be a FICO Score.

Why can the scores be far apart?

Different models, bureau files, versions and calculation dates can produce different numbers.

Which score will a lender use?

That depends on the lender, product and model selected for the decision.
