Does Closing a Credit Card Hurt Your Credit Score?

Closing a card can affect a score, but the effect depends on your full credit profile. One immediate reason is that removing the card limit can increase your revolving utilization.

Why a score could fall

If other cards carry balances, losing the closed card limit can increase your balance-to-limit ratio. For example, $1,000 owed across $5,000 of available limits is 20%; if a card with a $2,000 limit closes and balances stay the same, the ratio becomes about 33%.

Does closing an old card erase its history?

Not automatically. An account in good standing may remain on a credit report after closure. See how long closed accounts may stay on a credit report. Model treatment and eventual removal vary, so do not assume the age disappears on the closing date.

Reasons to close despite a possible score effect

An annual fee, poor terms, fraud concerns or difficulty controlling spending can matter more than a short-term score change. Review the agreement, rewards and any remaining balance before deciding.

Before you close

Check total limits and balances, redeem rewards, move recurring payments, ask about a no-fee product change if relevant, and confirm the account status afterward. Use the credit utilization calculator to test the arithmetic, not to predict a score.

Official reference

Check current guidance from Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This page is educational and cannot determine an individual credit score or legal outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Will closing a credit card hurt my credit?

It can, especially if the lost limit raises revolving utilization, but the outcome varies by credit profile and model.

Does canceling a card immediately shorten credit age?

Not necessarily. A closed account in good standing may remain on a report after closure.

Should I keep a card with an annual fee only for my score?

Not automatically. Compare the fee and account terms with any possible score effects.
