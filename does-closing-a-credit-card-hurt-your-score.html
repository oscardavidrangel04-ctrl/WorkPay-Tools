Does Closing a Credit Card Hurt Your Credit Score?

Closing a card can affect a score, but the effect depends on your full credit profile. One immediate reason is that removing the card limit can increase your revolving utilization.

Why a score could fall

A card can have a zero balance and still contribute to the total credit limit used in a utilization calculation. Removing that limit can raise the ratio on balances you owe elsewhere.

Closing a paid-off card while another card has a balance
AccountBalanceLimit before closureLimit remaining after closure
Card A, kept open$1,000$3,000$3,000
Card B, closed with no balance$0$2,000$0
Combined$1,000$5,000$3,000

Before closure, $1,000 ÷ $5,000 is 20%. After removing Card B's limit, $1,000 ÷ $3,000 is about 33.3%, with no additional borrowing. This example concerns a card closed at zero balance; cards closed with unpaid balances can receive different model treatment.

What if every card balance is zero?

If you still have open cards with positive limits and all included balances are zero, this particular ratio remains 0%. That does not guarantee an unchanged score: account mix, history and the scoring model still matter. If you close your only card, the calculation is not simply zero divided by a remaining limit.

Does closing an old card erase its history?

Not automatically. An account in good standing may remain on a credit report after closure. See how long closed accounts may stay on a credit report. Model treatment and eventual removal vary, so do not assume the age disappears on the closing date.

Reasons to close despite a possible score effect

An annual fee, poor terms, fraud concerns or difficulty controlling spending can matter more than a short-term score change. Review the agreement, rewards and any remaining balance before deciding.

Before you close

  1. Check the balance, pending activity, rewards and any annual-fee refund terms.
  2. Move subscriptions and recurring charges to another payment method.
  3. Ask about a no-fee product change if the annual fee is the reason for closure.
  4. Request confirmation of closure and keep the account records.
  5. Review the following statement and credit report for remaining charges or an incorrect status.

Closing a card does not cancel an unpaid balance. Continue required payments if money remains due. Use the credit utilization calculator to compare limits and balances before deciding; it cannot forecast the score change.

How to compare closing a card with keeping it open

Start with the reason you want to close it. If the problem is an annual fee, check when the annual fee is charged and which refund questions to ask, then ask whether a no-fee product change is available and what it would change. If the concern is overspending or account security, consider those needs alongside a possible score effect.

  1. Calculate total card balances and limits before and after the proposed closure.
  2. Check rewards, recurring payments and any remaining amount owed.
  3. Ask the issuer how it will handle a product change or closure; do not assume the options have identical terms.
  4. Save the confirmation and review later statements for remaining charges or interest.

The utilization calculator can compare ratios. It cannot predict how many points a score will move, and the fact that an account remains on a report does not preserve its available spending limit.

Frequently asked questions

Will closing a credit card hurt my credit?

It can, especially if the lost limit raises revolving utilization, but the outcome varies by credit profile and model.

Does canceling a card immediately shorten credit age?

Not necessarily. A closed account in good standing may remain on a report after closure.

Should I keep a card with an annual fee only for my score?

Not automatically. Compare the fee and account terms with any possible score effects.
