Authorized User vs Joint Account Holder

Compare an authorized user with a joint account holder at a high level and verify liability under the actual account agreement.

Permission to spend vs contractual responsibility

An authorized user is a person the account holder allows to use a credit card account. A joint account holder generally has a different contractual relationship with the issuer and may share responsibility, but product terms and availability vary by issuer and state. Never assume the labels create the same liability.

Account controls and credit-report information

Adding a person can affect access, account controls and credit-report information. The primary account holder should understand that charges made by an authorized user may be treated as authorized charges under the agreement. An authorized user should not assume they are legally responsible merely from the card label.

Read the application and account agreement

Read the account agreement and application terms before adding anyone. For a debt-collection or liability question, seek qualified legal help rather than relying on general terminology.

Clarify responsibilities before sharing a card

Two cards bearing different names do not necessarily mean two people are joint borrowers. Ask the issuer what relationship was created: authorized use, a joint account or another arrangement. The application and agreement matter more than the number of cards issued.

Discuss these points in advance

  • Who is responsible for paying the issuer?
  • Who can change settings, request a limit change or remove access?
  • How will spending limits and reimbursement between people work?
  • Will the issuer report the relationship, and to which bureaus?

If an authorized user makes a $200 purchase, an informal promise to reimburse the primary holder does not itself change the issuer's agreement. Keep that personal arrangement separate from responsibility to the card company. Check the reported account details rather than assuming a shared card will produce a particular score result.

Official reference

The CFPB defines an authorized user and explains that an authorized user generally is not obligated to repay the debt solely because of that status. Read the source.

Frequently asked questions

Does having my name on a card prove I am a joint borrower?

No. An authorized user can also have a card in their name. Ask the issuer and review the account agreement to identify the relationship.

Does removing access settle charges already made?

Do not assume so. Removing permission to spend and resolving existing charges are separate matters under the account terms.
