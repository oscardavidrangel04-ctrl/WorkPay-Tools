Do 401k Contributions Reduce Taxable Income?

Traditional pre-tax 401(k) contributions generally reduce current federal income-tax wages, while Roth 401(k) contributions generally do not. Employee deferrals typically remain subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes.

Reviewed October 22, 2026

Key takeaways

  1. Traditional pre-tax 401(k) deferrals generally lower current federal income-tax wages.
  2. Roth 401(k) contributions generally do not lower current federal taxable income.
  3. Traditional employee deferrals generally remain subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes.

A traditional 401(k) is usually handled through payroll. It is not normally a separate deduction that a regular employee enters on Form 1040.

Are 401(k) contributions subject to FICA?

Yes. Traditional and Roth employee 401(k) deferrals generally remain in the wages used for Social Security and Medicare taxes, together called FICA. A traditional pre-tax deferral can lower federal income-tax wages without lowering those payroll-tax wages.

For example, with $2,500 in gross pay and a $125 traditional employee deferral, federal income-tax wages may be $2,375 while Social Security and Medicare wages generally remain $2,500, before other adjustments and applicable wage limits. Do not subtract the deferral again when checking FICA wages.

The IRS explains the payroll treatment of elective deferrals. See what FICA tax covers to distinguish the payroll taxes from federal income-tax withholding. This treatment concerns employee salary deferrals, not every type of employer contribution or retirement distribution.

How a traditional 401(k) contribution affects taxable wages

Assume an employee earns $60,000 and elects to defer $6,000 into a traditional 401(k). In a simplified example, current federal income-tax wages may be reduced to $54,000. The $6,000 deferral generally remains part of Social Security and Medicare wages.

This is why W-2 Box 1 and Box 3 can be different. The example does not calculate tax savings, because actual tax depends on the complete return, marginal rates, credits, deductions and other income.

Does a 401(k) reduce gross income or taxable income?

For a regular employee, a traditional 401(k) deferral generally does not change the gross pay shown before deductions. Instead, it can reduce the federal income-tax wages reported through payroll. That is why gross pay and taxable wages can be different on a pay stub or Form W-2.

Labels vary by payroll provider. Check the description of each wage line rather than assuming every amount called “gross” is used for every tax calculation.

How much tax could a pre-tax contribution save?

A contribution reduces eligible income, not tax dollar-for-dollar. For a simplified federal example, assume an additional $1,000 traditional pre-tax contribution reduces income that would otherwise all be taxed at a 22% marginal rate. The federal income-tax reduction would be $1,000 × 22% = $220, leaving a $780 after-tax cost for the $1,000 saved.

This illustration holds other factors constant and excludes state taxes, credits, benefit interactions and future withdrawal taxes. It does not predict the change on a particular paycheck. Federal withholding can respond differently from the final tax calculation, and employee FICA treatment generally stays the same.

Does contributing more to a 401(k) reduce taxes?

An additional eligible traditional pre-tax deferral can reduce current federal income-tax wages, subject to plan rules and annual limits. That does not mean the contribution produces a dollar-for-dollar reduction in tax, and it does not generally reduce employee Social Security or Medicare taxes. Tax is usually deferred until taxable distributions are taken later.

For a broader comparison of the two contribution types, see traditional vs Roth 401(k).

Roth contributions use a different timing approach: they generally do not reduce current federal taxable income, but qualified distributions can be tax-free when the applicable requirements are satisfied.

Is a traditional 401(k) contribution a tax deduction?

Usually, no separate deduction is claimed on Form 1040 for a regular employee’s traditional 401(k) salary deferral. Payroll generally applies the contribution before federal income-tax wages are reported, so the eligible amount is already excluded from Box 1 of Form W-2.

That distinction matters: a $1,000 traditional deferral can reduce current federal taxable wages by $1,000, but it does not reduce the final tax bill by $1,000. The eventual savings depend on the full return, including filing status, marginal rate, credits, deductions and other income.

Can you claim 401(k) contributions on your taxes?

For regular employee salary deferrals, use the federal wages reported on your W-2. Do not subtract the traditional contribution a second time as a separate deduction: payroll has generally already excluded it from Box 1. A Roth employee contribution does not qualify for that exclusion.

Check these items before filing

  1. Confirm whether your contribution was traditional pre-tax, Roth or another after-tax contribution.
  2. Compare your final pay stub with W-2 Boxes 1 and 12. Ask payroll about a suspected error instead of changing reported wages yourself.
  3. Keep a 401(k) separate from any IRA contribution. Different reporting and deduction rules can apply.

See IRS Topic 424 for employee deferrals. Self-employed plan contributions require a different calculation; this example does not cover them.

What appears on Form W-2?

  • Traditional pre-tax elective deferrals generally are excluded from Box 1.
  • Traditional and Roth employee deferrals generally remain included in Boxes 3 and 5.
  • Box 12 reports deferrals using the applicable code.
  • Box 13 may indicate participation in a retirement plan.

Other deductions and benefits can also affect W-2 wages, so a difference between gross salary and Box 1 should not automatically be attributed only to the 401(k).

401(k) contributions, AGI and taxable income

Traditional 401(k) salary deferrals generally reduce the federal wages that flow into the return, which can reduce total income and AGI compared with receiving the same amount as currently taxable wages. Taxable income is calculated later after other applicable adjustments and deductions. Read adjusted gross income vs taxable income for that sequence.

401(k) taxable income FAQ

Do 401(k) contributions lower taxable income?

Traditional pre-tax employee deferrals generally lower current federal income-tax wages. Roth 401(k) contributions generally do not.

Does a 401(k) reduce gross income or taxable income?

A traditional 401(k) deferral generally reduces current federal income-tax wages through payroll rather than the gross pay shown before deductions. Payroll labels and tax treatment can vary, so review the pay stub and plan details.

Do they reduce Social Security and Medicare taxes?

Employee elective deferrals generally remain subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes.

Is a 401(k) contribution a deduction on my tax return?

For a regular employee’s traditional 401(k) salary deferral, the eligible amount is generally excluded through payroll from federal income-tax wages instead of being claimed as a separate Form 1040 deduction. Roth contributions do not receive that current exclusion.

Does an employer match reduce my taxable income?

A traditional employer match generally is not included in current employee taxable income when contributed and is not a personal wage deduction. A designated Roth employer match, if offered and elected under the plan, is included in income and reported on Form 1099-R.

Are withdrawals taxable later?

Traditional 401(k) distributions are generally included in taxable income when distributed, while qualified Roth distributions generally are not.
