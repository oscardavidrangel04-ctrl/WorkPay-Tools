How to Stop Debt Collector Calls

Understand how to request fewer or no collector calls, what written notice changes, and why it does not cancel the debt.

The short answer

You can tell a debt collector that certain times or places are inconvenient. You can also request in writing that it stop contacting you, but that request does not make the debt disappear.

Start with a specific contact restriction

Tell the collector not to call at work, at a particular time or through a particular channel. Keep a dated copy of your instructions and any later calls.

Request that contact stop

For a request to end routine contact, send the collector a written notice and retain proof of delivery. If it offers an electronic submission method, you can use that method and save the confirmation. The CFPB stop-contact instructions explain the process and limited notices that may still follow.

Keep the request easy to identify

Include the date, your name and mailing address, the collector’s name and the account reference from its notice. State clearly that you want contact to stop. Keep a copy of exactly what you sent. Use a verified address or submission route; a suspicious incoming caller is not a reliable source of payment or identity-verification instructions.

If you also dispute the debt, keep that issue explicit and follow the validation notice’s dispute instructions. A stop-contact request is not a substitute for an account dispute.

Separate contact from the underlying debt

A collector may still pursue permitted collection steps even after routine contact stops. If you dispute the debt or amount, use the validation process and keep that request distinct from a stop-contact notice.

Frequently asked questions

Is blocking a phone number the same as sending a stop-contact request?

No. Blocking affects that phone number on your device. It does not communicate a written request to the company or resolve the debt.

Can a collector contact me after receiving the request?

Limited notices can still be permitted, including confirmation that contact will end or notice of lawful action. Routine collection contact is different from those exceptions.

Should I keep a call log after sending the request?

Yes. Record later calls and messages alongside your request and delivery evidence, so the sequence is clear if you need to raise a complaint.

How often can a debt collector call you?

Federal rules create a presumption of a violation when a collector calls about a particular debt more than seven times within seven days, or calls within seven days after a telephone conversation about that debt. This is not permission for seven harassing calls: the pattern and circumstances still matter. Keep a dated call log. Read the CFPB call-frequency guidance.

Are debt collectors allowed to call on Sundays?

Sunday is not automatically prohibited under the general federal rule. Calls generally must avoid times or places the collector knows are inconvenient to you, and generally cannot occur before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. Tell the collector if Sunday calls are inconvenient, document the request, and check whether your state provides additional protection.

Calls to other people raise different issues; see whether collectors can call family members.

Do debt collectors leave voicemails?

Yes, but the words and frequency matter. Read the voicemail and limited-content message guide, and save the recording if you need to document repeated or revealing messages.
