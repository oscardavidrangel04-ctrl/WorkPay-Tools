How to Remove a Late Payment from a Credit Report

You can dispute a late-payment entry when it is inaccurate, incomplete or too old. An accurate, current late-payment mark generally cannot be removed simply by requesting it.

Check whether the mark is wrong

Compare the due date, payment receipt, posted date, account statements and report status. Look for an account that is not yours, a payment applied incorrectly or a late-payment month that does not match records.

Dispute a specific error

Identify the account and month, explain the exact discrepancy and attach copies of supporting documents. Submit the dispute to the credit-reporting company and the information furnisher, then keep confirmation and investigation results.

If the late payment is accurate

Neither a dispute template nor a credit-repair service can guarantee removal of correct information. You may ask the creditor to review its records or consider a courtesy adjustment, but it has no obligation to delete an accurate record.

What happens over time

Most negative payment history can generally appear for up to seven years. Continue making payments on time, check all three reports and correct errors rather than repeatedly filing unsupported disputes.

Official reference

Check current guidance from Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This page is educational and cannot determine an individual credit score or legal outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Can you get late payments removed from a credit report?

You can dispute entries that are inaccurate, incomplete or too old. Accurate late payments generally cannot be forced off early.

Will paying the account remove an old late payment?

No. Paying may update the current balance or status but does not automatically erase accurate historical late-payment marks.

What evidence helps a dispute?

Payment confirmations, bank records, statements and creditor correspondence that show the precise month or amount is wrong.
