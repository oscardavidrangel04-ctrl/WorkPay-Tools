Should You Pay the Collection Agency or Original Creditor?

Find out why the right payee depends on who owns the debt, whether a collector was hired, and the written payment terms.

The short answer

There is no universal payee. The original creditor may still own the debt and hire a collector, or may have sold the account. Confirm who is authorized to accept payment before sending money.

Find out who currently owns the debt

Compare the collector’s validation notice with original account records. Ask for the current creditor and the amount claimed if either is unclear. The name on a credit report alone is not sufficient proof of who should receive payment.

Get payment terms in writing

Before paying either party, request written terms stating the account, amount accepted, whether the payment resolves the obligation, and where to send it. Retain the agreement, receipt and later account statements.

If you already paid the original creditor

Do not pay the same amount again without checking records. Send copies of the payment evidence to the collector and dispute an inaccurate reported balance if necessary. Payment does not automatically remove an otherwise accurate collection entry.

Frequently asked questions

Does this page guarantee a deletion or score increase?

No. The outcome depends on the facts, the reporting and applicable rules.

Should I keep copies of my records?

Yes. Keep notices, correspondence, payment evidence and responses related to the account.

I paid the original creditor, not the collection agency—what next?

Match the receipt to the collector's account number and the period covered. Ask the original creditor to confirm whether it still owned the debt and whether the payment was forwarded or credited after any transfer. Send copies of payment proof to the collector if it continues to claim the same amount. Do not assume that paying one company automatically updated the other's system or erased a credit-report entry.
