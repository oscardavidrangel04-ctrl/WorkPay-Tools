Debt and credit planning

What Is a Debt Management Plan?

A debt management plan is a repayment arrangement commonly administered through a credit-counseling organization.

Questions to ask a credit counselor

Ask about fees, which debts are included, interest-rate changes, payment handling, creditor participation and the consequences of leaving the plan.

Check how the monthly payment reaches creditors

A debt management plan through a credit counselor can combine payments for distribution to creditors. It is not a new consolidation loan and does not erase the debts.

Build a payment checklist

  1. List participating accounts and any accounts excluded from the plan.
  2. Obtain the payment amount, service fees and expected schedule in writing.
  3. Confirm creditor acceptance and the start date before changing existing payments.
  4. Review creditor statements to confirm payments arrive as expected.
  5. Ask whom to contact if income changes or a payment is missed.

For example, distinguish a $300 creditor-payment allocation from a separately charged service fee. Your household budget needs the full amount leaving your account. Keep a copy of the plan and a record of payments so you can reconcile the service's summary with each creditor's statement.

Reference: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau guidance.

Check affordability beyond the plan payment

A plan payment does not necessarily include every debt or household expense. Suppose your budget has room for $450 after essential living costs. A $350 plan payment plus a $25 service fee and an excluded $100 loan payment would require $475. The plan would exceed that available amount by $25.

Ask what happens when income changes

Before enrolling, ask who you contact about a payment problem, which fees still apply and what creditors may do if the plan stops. Do not assume a counselor can change every creditor's terms. Continue to review creditor statements and resolve missing payments promptly.

A consolidation loan is different: it creates new borrowing. The CFPB's credit counseling overview explains the role of counselors and repayment plans.

Credit scores during a debt management plan

A repayment plan is not a guaranteed score increase. Its practical value is an affordable, correctly administered repayment arrangement. Making payments on time and reducing balances can support credit health, while missed payments, account changes and the rest of the credit file still matter.

Keep a record of what creditors actually receive

  1. Confirm the plan's first distribution date and each creditor's participation.
  2. Check creditor statements for the payment amount and posting date.
  3. Ask how enrolled accounts will be treated and whether they will close.
  4. Review your reports for accurate balances and payment status.

If the service confirms a debit but a creditor shows no payment, contact the counselor and creditor with the records. A payment to the service and receipt by the creditor are separate events. Debt Management Plan FAQ

Does a debt management plan require a new loan?

A counselor-administered repayment plan is not itself a new consolidation loan. Ask exactly how money is collected and distributed.

Are all of my creditors automatically included?

No. Confirm participation account by account and keep excluded payments in your household budget.

How quickly will a debt management plan improve my score?

There is no fixed timetable or guaranteed increase. Monitor payment delivery, balances and report accuracy rather than relying on a promised point gain.
