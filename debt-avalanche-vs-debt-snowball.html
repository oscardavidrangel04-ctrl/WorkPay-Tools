Debt Avalanche vs Debt Snowball: Which Debt Should You Pay First?

Compare the debt avalanche and debt snowball approaches, including the tradeoff between interest cost and visible progress.

The short answer

The debt avalanche approach directs extra payment toward the highest-interest debt while keeping required payments current on other debts. The debt snowball approach directs extra payment toward the smallest balance first. Both require a realistic plan for minimum payments, due dates and available cash.

What to check

The avalanche can reduce interest cost when all other facts are equal. The snowball can create faster account payoffs that some people find motivating. Neither approach changes the contract rate, stops collection activity or eliminates the need to make required payments.

Debt snowball vs avalanche: a simple example

MethodExtra payment goes toPrimary tradeoff
SnowballThe smallest remaining balanceMay create a paid-off account sooner; does not necessarily minimize interest.
AvalancheThe highest APR balanceCan reduce interest when other facts are equal; the first paid-off account may take longer.

For either method, keep required payments current on every debt and direct only the extra amount to the selected target. Use current statements; a promotional APR or fee can change the comparison.

Use the right information

List balances, interest rates, minimum payments and due dates from current statements before choosing a method. If payments are unaffordable, contact creditors or a qualified nonprofit counselor rather than taking a new loan without comparing total cost.

Official reference

The CFPB describes both the highest-interest-rate method and snowball method, noting their different tradeoffs. Read the source.
