W-2 Box 1 vs Box 5

Box 1 reports wages subject to federal income tax, while Box 5 reports Medicare wages and tips. The amounts can differ because some pre-tax deductions affect federal taxable wages differently from Medicare wages.

Reviewed September 15, 2026

Box 1 vs Box 5 at a glance

W-2 boxWhat it generally reportsCommon difference
Box 1Federal income-tax wages, tips and other compensationEligible pre-tax deductions can reduce the amount
Box 5Medicare wages and tipsTraditional 401(k) deferrals generally remain included

Why is Box 5 higher than Box 1?

A common reason is an eligible traditional 401(k) elective deferral. It generally reduces current federal income-tax wages reported in Box 1 but remains subject to Medicare tax and included in Box 5. Other benefit elections and payroll adjustments can also affect the relationship.

Do not assume the difference always equals retirement contributions. Compare the W-2 with the year-end pay statement and Box 12 codes, and contact payroll if the amounts appear incorrect.

W-2 Box 1 and Box 5 example

Suppose an employee earns $60,000 and contributes $6,000 to a traditional 401(k). Ignoring other adjustments, Box 1 might show $54,000 while Box 5 could show $60,000. This simplified example isolates one common difference and is not a complete W-2 calculation.

Box 3 vs Box 5

Box 3 reports Social Security wages and is subject to the applicable Social Security wage base. Box 5 reports Medicare wages and does not use that same wage cap. See the separate W-2 Box 1 vs Box 3 guide for the Social Security comparison.

W-2 Box 1 vs Box 5 FAQ

Why is W-2 Box 5 higher than Box 1?

Traditional 401(k) contributions are one common reason: eligible deferrals may reduce Box 1 while remaining included in Medicare wages in Box 5.

What does Box 5 report?

It reports Medicare wages and tips under applicable W-2 reporting rules.

Can Box 1 and Box 5 be equal?

Yes. They can be equal when there are no compensation items or deductions that create a difference between the two wage definitions.

Does Box 5 have the Social Security wage cap?

No. Medicare wages do not use the Social Security wage base that applies to Box 3.
