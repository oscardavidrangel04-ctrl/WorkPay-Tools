Credit Card Billing Period: Dates and Transactions

A billing period is the span of account activity included in one statement. Its closing date ends the cycle; the payment due date is a later deadline.

What belongs in one period

Posted purchases, credits, payments, fees and interest are assigned according to the issuer’s posting and cycle rules. Pending transactions may not be part of the statement if they have not posted by closing.

Closing date is not the due date

The closing date determines the statement snapshot. The due date tells you when the required payment must reach the issuer. The CFPB explains that statements must be delivered at least 21 days before the payment due date under applicable rules.

Why period length matters

A cycle can have a different number of days from another month. When interest accrues daily, the days and changing balances affect the charge. That is why a simple monthly APR ÷ 12 estimate may not match the statement.

Check the actual account terms

Credit card rates, fees, posting rules and promotional terms can vary by issuer and transaction. Read your current statement and agreement for the account-specific answer. Primary reference: consumer guidance from the CFPB or card issuer.

Frequently asked questions

Is a billing period always 30 days?

No. Cycle length can vary by calendar and issuer.

Does a purchase on closing day appear?

It depends on when the transaction posts under issuer processing rules.

Does the due date end the billing period?

No. The cycle closes first, and the payment deadline comes later.
