How Long Does a Repossession Stay on Your Credit Report?

A vehicle repossession can generally remain on a U.S. credit report for up to seven years, commonly measured from the delinquency that led to the repossessionâ€”not from a later payment or collection date.

Repossession reporting timeline

The credit-report entry may include the original auto account, repossession status, remaining balance and later collection activity. Related entries should still be checked for correct dates and ownership.

Voluntary surrender vs repossession

A voluntary surrender may reduce logistical costs, but it can still be reported as an adverse account event. Review the lender documents for sale proceeds, fees and any deficiency balance.

Check the date and account details

Compare the date of first delinquency, account status, balance and collector information with notices and statements. A later sale or transfer should not restart the federal credit-reporting period.

What to do if information is inaccurate

Gather the report, loan records, sale notice and payment documents. Dispute the specific inaccurate field through the current bureau process and keep the response.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a repossession stay on a credit report?

It can generally be reported for up to seven years, commonly tied to the delinquency that led to the repossession.

Does paying a deficiency remove the repossession?

Payment may update the balance or status but does not automatically remove accurate historical reporting.

Can a repossession be disputed?

A specific item can be disputed when you believe it is inaccurate or incomplete; accurate negative information is not removed merely because it is disputed.
