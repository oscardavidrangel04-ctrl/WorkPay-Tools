Paycheck terminology

What Is Net Pay?

Net pay is the amount remaining after payroll taxes and other deductions are subtracted from gross pay. It is commonly called take-home pay, although payment timing and adjustments may affect the amount received.

Reviewed September 13, 2026

Net pay formula

Net pay = gross pay − taxes − benefit deductions − other deductions

If gross pay is $2,000 and total deductions are $460, the resulting net-pay example is $1,540. This is an illustration, not a tax estimate.

Common paycheck deductions

  • Federal, state or local taxes that apply
  • Social Security and Medicare taxes where applicable
  • Health, dental or other benefit contributions
  • Retirement-plan contributions
  • Other authorized or legally required deductions

Not every deduction applies to every worker, and some may be pre-tax while others are after-tax. A pay statement identifies the items used for that payroll.

Gross pay and net pay are not interchangeable

WorkPay Tools calculators generally report gross estimates because exact take-home pay depends on personal and payroll details. Start with gross pay, then use an official withholding estimator or payroll statement when you need a personalized deduction estimate.

A raise may increase gross pay by a clear dollar amount while the change in take-home pay is smaller. Use the Salary Increase Calculator to measure the gross change and keep the distinction visible.

Why net pay changes between checks

Hours, overtime, bonuses, benefit elections, withholding settings and one-time deductions can change. A biweekly schedule can also create 26 paychecks in a typical year, while semimonthly payroll normally uses 24. Compare the pay-period structure with the Biweekly Pay Calculator.

Net pay FAQ

Is net pay the same as take-home pay?

The terms are commonly used for the amount left after taxes and deductions.

How do I calculate it?

Start with gross pay and subtract all taxes, benefits and other deductions shown for the paycheck.

Why can it change?

Changes in earnings, withholding, benefits or other payroll adjustments can change the amount.
