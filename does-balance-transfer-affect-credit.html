Does a Balance Transfer Affect Your Credit?

A transfer can change where a balance is reported and, if you open a new card, can add an application inquiry. No exact score change can be predicted.

Utilization can move between accounts

Moving debt changes balances on the old and new cards. Depending on limits and reporting dates, utilization on each card and across cards can change. Issuer reporting schedules and scoring models differ.

A new account is a separate event

Applying for a new balance-transfer card may lead to a credit inquiry and a newly reported account. A transfer offered on an existing account does not necessarily involve a new application.

Payments still matter

Keep making required payments on the old card until the transfer is complete. A processing delay does not erase the old account’s due date. After completion, verify the residual balance and pay the new account as required. Avoid promises that a transfer always helps or hurts a score.

Check the actual account terms

Credit card rates, fees, posting rules and promotional terms can vary by issuer and transaction. Read your current statement and agreement for the account-specific answer. Primary reference: consumer guidance from the CFPB or card issuer.

Frequently asked questions

Will a balance transfer automatically raise my score?

No. The result depends on reported balances, limits, inquiries and the scoring model.

Does a transfer close my old card?

Not necessarily. Check the issuer’s account status after the transfer.

Should I stop paying the old card immediately?

No. Continue required payments until the transfer is confirmed.
