What Is a Pay Stub? How to Read Your Paycheck

Learn what a pay stub shows, how pay period and pay date differ, and where to find gross pay, deductions and net pay.

What a pay stub shows

A pay stub is the record that accompanies a paycheck. It normally identifies the pay period, pay date, gross earnings, deductions, taxes and take-home amount. Its labels vary by employer and payroll provider, so use the labels and year-to-date columns in your own statement rather than assuming every stub is identical.

Identify the covered period and current amounts

Start with the pay period: the work dates covered by the payment. The pay date is when the check is issued. Gross pay is compensation before withholdings and deductions. Net pay is what remains after the listed items are taken out. Year-to-date figures are cumulative for the calendar year and are not the amount earned in just this check.

For the dates on the record, see pay period vs pay date rather than assuming the deposit date identifies the days worked.

How to read a year-end pay stub

The final pay stub of a calendar year can help you review total earnings and deductions. Use the year-to-date column for the annual running total and the current column for that single paycheck. Do not add a year-to-date total to the current amount when the total already includes that check.

Suppose the current check shows $2,000 gross pay and year-to-date gross pay is $52,000. The annual running total is $52,000, not $54,000. A $300 current deduction should likewise be distinguished from its cumulative total.

A year-end stub is useful for reconciliation but is not a substitute for Form W-2. Taxable wage definitions and later corrections can cause differences. Read why W-2 wage boxes can differ before comparing every box directly with gross pay.

Check earnings and deductions against your records

Compare the hours, rate, overtime, bonus or commission entries with your records. Then separate required tax withholding from benefit elections, retirement contributions, wage garnishments or other deductions. Social Security and Medicare may appear as separate lines; see what FICA tax means on a paycheck before combining or questioning those amounts. Ask payroll about a label you cannot identify before treating it as an error.

The method used to deliver net pay is a separate question; compare direct deposit and payroll cards if those are the options offered.

How to get your most recent pay stubs

  1. Open your employer’s payroll portal and look for pay statements or earnings statements.
  2. Select the latest completed pay dates required by the person requesting the documents. A bank transaction is not the same record.
  3. Download the original statement so the employer name, pay period and totals remain together.
  4. If access is unavailable, ask payroll or HR for the specific dates. For a former job, contact the former employer rather than recreating a statement.

If someone requests “two months of pay stubs,” confirm the exact date range: two monthly statements and two biweekly statements cover different lengths of time. See pay-frequency differences before choosing the documents.

Official reference

The IRS explains that gross income per pay period is the amount paid before taxes and deductions and notes that a pay stub can show both the pay period and pay date. Read the source.

A simple pay-stub reading order

  1. Identify the employer, employee and pay-period dates.
  2. Match the listed hours and pay rate to your records.
  3. Read gross earnings for this period, not the year-to-date total.
  4. Review each employee deduction and withholding line.
  5. Reconcile net pay with the payment or split deposits shown.

Save the record before asking payroll about a discrepancy so you can identify the exact line and period.

Source: IRS Publication 4268: payroll records.

Frequently asked questions

Is a pay stub the same as a bank deposit?

No. The pay stub explains earnings and deductions. A bank deposit shows money received and may be only part of a split payment.

What does YTD mean on a pay stub?

YTD means year to date. It is a cumulative amount, not the amount earned or deducted only in the current pay period.

What should I send payroll if a pay stub looks wrong?

Identify the pay period, the line in question and the amount you expected. Keep relevant time records, pay-rate information or benefit elections to support the comparison.
