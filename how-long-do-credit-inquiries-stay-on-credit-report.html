How Long Do Hard Inquiries Stay on Your Credit Report?

Hard inquiries generally stay on your credit report for up to two years. FICO Scores generally consider inquiries from the most recent 12 months, so an inquiry can remain visible after it stops affecting a FICO Score.

Reviewed October 25, 2026 · Editorial standards and methodology

Hard inquiry timeline at a glance

Time from inquiryWhat generally happens
Application dateThe lender's hard credit check can be added to one or more credit reports.
First 12 monthsThe inquiry can be considered by FICO Scores. Its practical effect may lessen before the full year.
After 12 monthsFICO Scores generally stop considering it, although it can still be displayed on the report.
About 24 monthsThe hard inquiry generally falls off the credit report.

Use the date of the inquiry recorded in the inquiry section, not the date you downloaded the report, an account-update date or an approval date. Bureau display conventions and non-FICO scoring models can differ.

What is a hard credit inquiry?

A hard inquiry, also called a hard pull or hard credit check, usually occurs when a lender reviews a credit file after a person applies for a credit card, mortgage, auto loan, student loan or another form of credit. It shows that new credit was requested and may be considered by credit-scoring models.

A hard inquiry is not the same as a new account. An application can create an inquiry even when the lender denies the application or the applicant decides not to accept the offer. If a new account is opened, the inquiry and the account can affect different parts of a credit profile.

Checking your own credit report does not create a hard inquiry. A prequalification may use a soft inquiry, but a later formal application can still result in a hard pull. Read the disclosure before authorizing a credit check.

How long does a hard inquiry affect your credit score?

Visibility and score impact are separate. FICO states that its scores consider inquiries from the previous 12 months even though the inquiries remain on reports for two years. Other scoring models and lender decisions may treat recent applications differently.

There is no universal number of points lost. The effect of one inquiry is often small, but it depends on the scoring model and the rest of the file. Several recent applications can matter more, especially when the credit history is short. Payment history, balances and credit utilization normally provide more context than an inquiry alone. How rate shopping changes the way inquiries are counted

When someone compares rates for one mortgage, auto loan or student loan, several hard inquiries may appear on the reports. FICO scoring versions can group eligible inquiries made within a shopping window and count them as one event for scoring purposes.

Eligible inquiries may be grouped within 14 days by older FICO versions or 45 days by newer versions. A separate FICO rule ignores mortgage, auto and student-loan inquiries made during the 30 days before scoring. The 30-day buffer and the grouping window serve different purposes; neither deletes inquiries from the report.

Do not apply this treatment to separate credit-card applications. The lender selects the scoring version. The FICO inquiry explanation describes the two timing rules.

When do credit inquiries fall off your credit report?

A hard inquiry should generally be removed about two years after its recorded date. Review the inquiry section of each bureau report because a lender may check one bureau, two bureaus or all three. The same application can therefore appear differently across reports.

  1. Find the inquiry name and date on the report.
  2. Match it to a credit application, rental or financing request.
  3. Check whether the report provides an estimated removal date.
  4. Review all three reports if the inquiry is unfamiliar or appears overdue for removal.

Use how to read a credit report to locate inquiry details and distinguish them from accounts.

Example: the inquiry is visible after its FICO impact ends

Suppose a hard inquiry is dated October 17, 2026. The ordinary two-year reporting window points to around October 2028. FICO's 12-month consideration window is shorter, so the inquiry can still be visible after October 2027 without being included in that part of a FICO Score calculation.

This is a timeline illustration, not a promise of an exact deletion day or score increase. Bureau processing and scoring models can differ. Compare the inquiry date on each report rather than using the date you received the card or paid off the account.

If the inquiry appears older than the normal window, save the report and ask the bureau to investigate the date. For an unfamiliar inquiry, first check the creditor name and application records. The FICO inquiry guide distinguishes report retention from scoring.

Can you remove a hard inquiry from your credit report?

An accurate, authorized hard inquiry normally cannot be removed early just because it lowered a score or the application was denied. Paying off or closing the resulting account also does not erase the inquiry.

How to review an inquiry you do not recognize

  1. Save the report showing the company, inquiry date and bureau.
  2. Compare the name with recent applications and the lender's parent or financing company.
  3. Contact the listed business through independently verified contact details if you cannot match it.
  4. If the inquiry is inaccurate or unauthorized, identify that specific entry when disputing it with each bureau showing it.

Keep copies of the records and explain what is wrong. Do not claim that every unfamiliar company name proves identity theft, or describe an application you actually authorized as unauthorized.

Follow how to dispute a credit report error for evidence and submission steps. If identity theft may be involved, compare a credit freeze and fraud alert. A promise to delete every legitimate inquiry is not the same as correcting a reporting error.

How to reduce the effect of future inquiries

  • Ask whether a prequalification uses a soft or hard inquiry before continuing.
  • Limit formal applications that are not necessary.
  • Complete eligible loan rate shopping within a compact window.
  • Review reports before a major application so an error does not surprise you.
  • Continue paying on time and managing balances; an inquiry is only one part of a score.

Waiting for an inquiry to age does not guarantee a particular score increase. Scores can change at the same time because balances, payment status, accounts and the model itself also change.

Credit inquiry FAQ

How long do hard inquiries stay on your credit report?

Hard inquiries generally remain on a credit report for up to two years. FICO Scores generally consider inquiries from the most recent 12 months, so report visibility and score impact do not last for the same period.

When do credit inquiries fall off your credit report?

A hard inquiry should generally fall off about two years after the inquiry date. Check the date and expected removal information shown by the credit bureau.

How long does a hard inquiry affect your credit score?

FICO Scores generally consider hard inquiries from the previous 12 months. The practical effect may fade sooner and depends on the rest of the credit file and the scoring model.

How long do soft inquiries stay on your credit report?

How long a soft inquiry remains visible can vary by credit bureau and inquiry type. Soft inquiries do not affect credit scores and are generally shown only on the consumer-facing report.

Is a hard pull the same as a hard inquiry?

Yes. Hard pull, hard credit check and hard inquiry commonly describe a lender request made after an application for credit.

Can you remove a hard inquiry early?

An accurate, authorized inquiry normally cannot be removed simply because it affected a score. If an inquiry is unauthorized or inaccurate, contact the listed company and dispute it with each bureau reporting it.

Do multiple loan inquiries count as one?

Some scoring models group mortgage, auto-loan and student-loan inquiries made within a rate-shopping window. The window and eligible loan types depend on the scoring model; separate credit-card applications are generally not grouped this way.
