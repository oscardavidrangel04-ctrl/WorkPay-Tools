How Long Do Credit Inquiries Stay on a Credit Report?

Hard inquiries generally remain on a credit report for up to two years. A score model may weigh them for less time, and checking your own report is generally a soft inquiry.

Hard versus soft inquiries

A hard inquiry usually follows an application for credit. A soft inquiry can arise from reviewing your own report or certain account and promotional checks. Soft inquiries are not the same as applications for new credit.

How long does an inquiry appear?

Hard inquiries can typically be listed for up to two years. How a particular score model treats recent inquiries is a separate question, so do not assume the full reporting period equals the scoring impact.

If you do not recognize an inquiry

Check the company name and date against applications or financing requests. If it is unfamiliar, contact the company and dispute inaccurate information with the bureau, especially if identity theft is possible.

Avoid duplicate assumptions

Multiple inquiries for rate shopping can be treated differently by scoring models depending on loan type and time window. Do not assume every listed inquiry has the same score effect.

Official reference

Check current guidance from Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This page is educational and cannot determine an individual credit score or legal outcome.

Frequently asked questions

How long do hard inquiries stay on a credit report?

Hard inquiries generally remain visible for up to two years.

Does checking my own credit report hurt my score?

No. Reviewing your own report is generally a soft inquiry.

Does an inquiry affect a score for two years?

Not necessarily. Report visibility and a scoring model impact are different.
