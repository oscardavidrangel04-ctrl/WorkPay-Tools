Savings Rate: How to Calculate It

Learn a simple savings-rate formula, why the income definition matters, and how to use the result as a personal trend.

The short answer

A savings rate compares money saved during a period with income during the same period. A simple formula is savings divided by income, expressed as a percentage. The definition of savings and income must stay consistent; otherwise two months may not be comparable.

What to check

For example, someone may count transfers to savings, retirement contributions or debt principal differently depending on the purpose of the calculation. Refunds, investment changes and one-time windfalls can also change the result. A single rate does not determine whether a household is financially secure.

Use the right information

Choose a consistent period, record the income type used and make a separate note for unusual events. Use the trend to start a conversation about goals, cash flow and expenses rather than as a rigid rule.

Official reference
