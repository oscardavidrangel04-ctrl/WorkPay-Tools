Debt Relief vs Debt Consolidation

Debt relief is a broad term that can include several services; consolidation generally refers to combining or restructuring balances.

Check the service behind the debt relief label

Read contracts, fees, payment handling, credit consequences and claims about guaranteed results before sharing account information.

Look beyond the advertisement label

Debt relief is a broad label; consolidation is a specific method of combining debts through replacement borrowing. Ask what action a provider will actually take before comparing its advertised payment with your current bills.

Write down these five answers

  1. Am I applying for a loan or paying for a service?
  2. Which accounts are covered?
  3. Who gets the money and on what dates?
  4. Which amounts are guaranteed by a contract and which are estimates?
  5. What costs or remaining balances are excluded from the advertisement?

A single advertised monthly number is not enough to compare two options. Use a worksheet with separate columns for payment, fees, duration and unpaid accounts. Keep copies of the offer and agreement; if the written terms do not match the explanation, resolve that difference before proceeding.

Reference: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau guidance.

Use the payment destination to identify the arrangement

Ask where your next payment goes. A payment on a new loan goes toward that borrowing. A debt management plan through credit counseling may distribute money to participating creditors. A settlement arrangement may involve setting money aside while negotiations continue. Those are different processes even when advertisements use the same phrase.

Compare written obligations, not promised savings

Create one row for each existing debt. Mark whether the offer pays it off, includes it in a repayment plan, proposes a negotiation or leaves it outside the arrangement. Then total the payments and fees you would still have to cover.

For the distinction between the two most easily confused options, read consolidation vs settlement. CFPB guidance on consolidation advertisements warns that the advertised label may not describe the actual service.

Debt Relief vs Debt Consolidation FAQ

Is every debt relief company a lender?

No. The label can describe a service rather than a loan. Ask whether you are borrowing money, paying for counseling or paying for negotiations.

What should I compare if two offers quote the same monthly payment?

Compare duration, fees, included debts, payment destinations and what happens to balances that are not resolved. The same monthly amount can buy very different arrangements.
