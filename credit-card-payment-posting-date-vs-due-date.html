Credit Card Payment Posting Date vs Due Date

Compare a credit card payment posting date vs due date, including receipt, cutoff times and processing differences.

The short answer

The due date is the deadline shown on the statement. The posting date is when the issuer records a payment on the account. A payment can be submitted, received, credited and displayed as posted at different times.

Receipt and cutoff time matter

Follow the payment instructions, stated time zone and cutoff time. Sending a payment on the due date does not always mean the issuer receives it by the required deadline.

Why a payment can remain pending

Online banking, issuer processing and payment holds can affect when a payment appears as posted or restores available credit. Keep the confirmation number and review the account after submission.

Weekend and holiday timing

Payment-method and issuer rules can differ. Do not assume every electronic, phone or mailed payment receives identical weekend treatment.

Official reference

Review current guidance from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the terms of the specific account.

Frequently asked questions

Is a pending payment considered on time?

The issuer process and receipt time control. Keep confirmation details and contact the issuer if the account shows a problem.

Does posting date change the statement closing date?

No. Payment processing and the statement-cycle closing date are separate concepts.

Can available credit update after the payment posts?

Yes. Available-credit restoration can occur on a different timetable.
