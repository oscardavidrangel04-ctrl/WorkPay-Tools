How Long Does a Credit Card Payment Take to Post?

There is no single posting time for every issuer or payment method. The effective date an issuer credits a payment can differ from when the transaction appears online; check the cutoff and time zone on your statement if the due date is close.

Payment posting date vs. due date

The due date is the payment deadline on your statement. The posting date is when the issuer displays the payment in account activity. A payment can be submitted, received, credited and displayed at different times, so keep the confirmation and follow the issuer’s stated cutoff.

Receipt and cutoff time matter

Follow the statement’s payment instructions, time zone and cutoff. For U.S. consumer credit cards, the CFPB says a payment generally cannot be treated as late if the issuer receives it by 5 p.m. on the due date in the time zone shown on the statement. If the due date falls on a Sunday or holiday, the next business day generally applies. Issuers can set reasonable cutoffs for online or phone payments, so check the exact method you use.

The date an item appears in your online activity can differ from the date used to determine whether the payment was timely. If a due-date issue appears, contact the issuer with your confirmation and ask which receipt or credit date it recorded.

Why a payment can remain pending

Payment method, submission after a cutoff, weekends, holidays, bank processing or an account verification hold can delay when a payment appears as posted or restores available credit. A pending display by itself does not tell you whether the issuer counted the payment as timely. Keep the confirmation number and review the account after submission.

Weekend and holiday timing

Payment-method and issuer rules can differ. Do not assume every electronic, phone, bank bill-pay or mailed payment receives identical weekend treatment. For a mailed payment, use the payment address and instructions on the statement and allow enough time for delivery and processing.

Official reference

Review the CFPB guidance on when a credit card payment is late and the payment-crediting rule. Your statement and the issuer’s payment instructions determine the relevant time zone, cutoff and accepted methods.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a credit card payment take to post?

There is no single posting time for every issuer or payment method. Check the issuer’s payment history for the effective credit date; a pending display may update later.

Does posting date change the statement closing date?

No. Payment processing and the statement-cycle closing date are separate concepts.

Can available credit update after the payment posts?

Yes. Available-credit restoration can occur on a different timetable.

When is a credit card payment considered received?

Check the issuer’s cutoff for your payment method; a Sunday or holiday due date generally moves the deadline to the next business day.
