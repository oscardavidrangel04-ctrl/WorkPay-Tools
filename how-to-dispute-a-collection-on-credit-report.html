How to Dispute a Collection on a Credit Report

Learn how to dispute inaccurate collection information on a credit report, what evidence to gather and what happens after a dispute.

The short answer

Dispute a collection only when you believe information is inaccurate or incomplete. Identify the exact account detail at issue, gather records supporting your position and submit a clear dispute to each credit reporting company showing the item.

Step 1: identify the specific error

Compare the collector name, original creditor, account number, balance, dates and account status. A general statement that an account is unfair is less useful than identifying the precise field you believe is wrong and why.

Step 2: gather and send supporting records

Useful records can include statements, payment confirmations, identity-theft documentation or correspondence. Send copies rather than irreplaceable originals, follow the bureau current submission instructions and keep proof of what you sent.

Step 3: review the investigation result

The credit reporting company generally forwards relevant information to the furnisher and sends the result after its investigation. Review the updated report carefully. A dispute does not guarantee deletion of information that is verified as accurate.

Disputing with the furnisher

You may also dispute directly with the company that furnished the information. Use the address or process specified for disputes, state the error clearly and retain the response.

Official reference

Review the current guidance from Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This page is educational and does not provide legal advice or predict a credit score.

What is the best dispute reason for a collection?

The strongest reason is the truthful, specific one supported by your records: the account is not yours, the balance or dates are wrong, the same debt appears twice, or the collector cannot verify the reported information. Do not select a generic reason merely because it sounds more likely to produce deletion.

Identify the precise field you believe is inaccurate and attach relevant records. Use the credit dispute letter template to organize the request.

Frequently asked questions

Can I dispute a collection that belongs to me?

You can dispute information you believe is inaccurate or incomplete. A dispute is not a method for removing accurate information solely because it is negative.

Should I use a template dispute reason?

Describe the actual error and provide relevant evidence. Do not claim identity theft, payment or another fact that is not true.

Will a dispute increase my credit score?

Not necessarily. The outcome depends on the investigation, any report change and the scoring model used.
