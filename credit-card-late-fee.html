Credit Card Late Fee: When It Applies and How to Request a Waiver

A late fee can apply when the issuer does not receive at least the required minimum by the applicable due date and cutoff.

Late fee is not the same as interest

A late fee is a penalty charge for a missed required payment. Interest is a borrowing cost and may accrue for different reasons. Paying only the minimum can avoid a late-payment fee while leaving a balance that accrues interest.

Why the amount can vary

Fee rules, account terms and applicable law affect the amount. Do not rely on a universal dollar figure: check the current agreement and statement. Repeated late payments can be treated differently from an initial late payment.

If the charge looks wrong

Compare the payment amount, confirmation time, time zone and issuer receipt with the statement. The CFPB says to contact the issuer if you believe you paid on time and ask it to review or waive the charge.

How to ask for a credit card late fee waiver

  1. Find the statement showing the fee and the required minimum.
  2. Gather the payment confirmation, amount, receipt time and any returned-payment notice.
  3. Contact the issuer using the number on your card or statement.
  4. Explain the facts and ask for a review or a courtesy waiver.
  5. Keep the response and check the next statement for any adjustment.

A courtesy waiver is not guaranteed. If the fee was charged in error, describe the payment evidence rather than only requesting goodwill. Ask whether any remaining payment or interest is still due.

A simple short-payment example

If the statement requires $40 and you pay $30 by the deadline, the payment may still fail to meet the minimum requirement. Paying something is different from paying the full required minimum. See minimum payment vs statement balance.

Prevent a fee without confusing it with interest

Track the required payment separately from the balance needed to avoid interest. Reminders and autopay can help, but verify the linked account has funds and the payment succeeded. A returned payment may leave the amount unpaid.

For receipt deadlines and method-specific cutoffs, read when a card payment is considered late. A fee on a statement is not itself proof that a 30-day delinquency was reported to a bureau.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ask for a first-time late fee to be waived?

Yes. Ask the issuer to review the charge or consider a courtesy waiver, but approval is not guaranteed.

Can paying less than the minimum trigger a late fee?

Yes. A payment below the required minimum can fail to satisfy the payment obligation even if received before the deadline.

Will waiving a fee also remove interest?

Not necessarily. Ask the issuer which charges it adjusted; fees, interest and payment status are separate.

Check the actual account terms

Credit card rates, fees, posting rules and promotional terms can vary by issuer and transaction. Read your current statement and agreement for the account-specific answer. Primary reference: consumer guidance from the CFPB or card issuer.
