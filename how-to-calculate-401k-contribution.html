How to Calculate a 401(k) Contribution

Understand how a salary percentage becomes a planned 401(k) contribution.

The basic method

A contribution estimate multiplies eligible compensation by the elected contribution percentage. Plan rules and current legal limits can affect the real payroll amount.

Calculate an election per paycheck

Assume eligible annual pay of $60,000 and an employee election of 5%. Multiply $60,000 by 0.05 to get $3,000 for a full year. With 26 equal paychecks, that is about $115.38 per paycheck before rounding adjustments.

Inputs that can change the result

  • Use eligible compensation under the plan, not automatically every payment you receive.
  • For an election starting midyear, calculate from the remaining eligible paychecks.
  • Keep the employer match separate from your employee contribution.
  • Check the applicable annual limit and contributions already made before increasing an election.

This example calculates a contribution, not take-home pay or tax savings. A percentage election and a fixed-dollar election can produce different totals when pay varies.

How to adjust a 401(k) contribution during the year

Start with the amount you still plan to contribute, not the full annual target. Suppose your employee contribution target is $6,000, you have already contributed $2,000, and eight eligible paychecks remain after the new election takes effect.

  1. Subtract contributions already made: $6,000 − $2,000 = $4,000 remaining.
  2. Divide by the remaining paychecks: $4,000 ÷ 8 = $500 per paycheck.
  3. If each remaining paycheck has $2,500 of eligible pay, the percentage election is $500 ÷ $2,500 × 100 = 20%.

Check the target before changing the election

The $6,000 target is an example, not an IRS limit or a recommendation. Confirm the current IRS contribution limits, any lower plan restriction, and relevant contributions made through another employer. Ask payroll which paycheck will first reflect the change.

If eligible pay varies, estimate the total remaining eligible pay and divide the remaining target by that total to find a starting percentage. Recheck actual contributions after payroll runs. Keep the employer match out of your employee-contribution target; use the 401(k) match calculator to estimate it separately.

Question to check

Does a contribution equal an employer match?

No. Your elected contribution and an employer match are separate amounts.
