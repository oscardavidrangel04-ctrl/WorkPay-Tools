Merit Increase vs COLA: What Is the Difference?

Learn how a merit increase differs from a cost-of-living adjustment and how to compare each to your current pay.

The short answer

A merit increase is generally tied to performance, skills, role scope or an employer’s compensation process. A cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) is generally intended to address changes in the cost of living. Employers may use the terms differently, and neither is guaranteed unless a policy, contract or plan says so.

What to check

Compare the percentage, effective date and resulting annual pay. A small percentage can have a different dollar effect depending on base salary. A raise can also change future pay calculations, but it does not automatically offset every household expense or inflation measure.

Use the right record

Use the Pay Raise Calculator for simple gross pay comparisons and the Salary Growth Calculator for recurring percentage scenarios. The results are estimates, not a prediction of an employer’s decision or inflation.

Official reference

The Department of Labor’s salary-negotiation materials describe salary ranges and their potential relationship to pay raises; employer policy controls the actual increase. Read the source.

Compare the purpose of a merit increase and a COLA

A merit increase normally recognizes performance or development under an employer’s process. A cost-of-living adjustment aims to address living-cost changes. Neither label guarantees a specific percentage or full inflation protection. Ask which measure, review period and eligible pay base the employer uses.

Worked example: combined vs sequential increases

On a $60,000 salary, 3% COLA is $1,800 and 2% merit is $1,200. If both apply to the original salary, the new base is $63,000. If 2% applies after the 3% adjustment, the result is $60,000 × 1.03 × 1.02 = $63,036. Confirm the method rather than assuming percentages simply add. Use the pay raise calculator to compare the final amounts.

Nominal pay growth vs purchasing power

An illustrative 3% raise with 4% inflation changes purchasing power by about −0.96%: 1.03 ÷ 1.04 − 1. A raise can increase dollars without keeping pace with prices. See salary increase vs inflation for this distinction. The example does not use a live inflation estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Can an employer award both merit and COLA?

Yes. The policy determines whether they are separate, combined or applied sequentially.

Is an employer COLA the same as a Social Security COLA?

No. They refer to different programs and rules; an employer adjustment does not automatically follow Social Security.

Does COLA guarantee that pay matches inflation?

No. Check the selected measure, period and employer formula.

Does a merit increase automatically follow a good review?

Not necessarily. Eligibility, budget and policy can affect the outcome.
