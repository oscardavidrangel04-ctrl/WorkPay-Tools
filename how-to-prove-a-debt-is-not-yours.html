How to Prove a Debt Is Not Yours

Learn how to check a debt you do not recognize, gather supporting evidence, and dispute a collector claim or inaccurate credit-report entry.

The short answer

To show a debt may not be yours, identify the exact mismatch first: a mixed file, a paid account, identity theft, or incorrect balance or dates. A new collector name alone does not prove the debt belongs to someone else; compare the notice and account details with your records.

Request details about the debt

Read the validation notice and ask for the creditor, account and amount if unclear. Compare them with your own statements and reports; a new collector name alone does not prove the account is someone else’s.

What evidence can show a debt is not yours?

The useful records depend on the mismatch. Gather copies of records that support the specific facts, such as:

  • Statements, payment confirmations, or a settlement letter if the debt was paid or the balance is wrong.
  • Account documents or correspondence showing the account belongs to someone else or uses details that do not match yours.
  • The validation notice and itemization, including the current creditor, original creditor, amount, and dates.
  • An identity-theft report or related account records if you suspect someone opened the account using your information.

Do not send irreplaceable originals. Keep copies of everything and use the collector or bureau current secure submission instructions.

Debt validation and credit-report disputes are separate

If you receive validation information from a debt collector covered by federal collection rules, the notice explains how to dispute. A written dispute generally made within 30 days of receiving that information requires the collector to pause collection of the disputed amount until it sends verification. These validation-notice rights may not apply in the same way to an original creditor; check the notice and current CFPB debt-validation guidance.

If the collection also appears inaccurately on your credit report, that is a separate process: dispute the entry with each reporting company that shows it and, when appropriate, with the furnisher. A collector dispute alone does not automatically correct a credit report. See the credit-report error steps and keep proof of each submission and response.

Frequently asked questions

Does this page guarantee a deletion or score increase?

No. The outcome depends on the facts, the reporting and applicable rules.

Should I keep copies of my records?

Yes. Keep notices, correspondence, payment evidence and responses related to the account.

How long do I have to dispute a debt validation notice?

You generally have 30 days after receiving validation information to dispute in writing. A timely written dispute generally pauses collection of the disputed amount until a covered debt collector sends verification. Follow the deadline shown on your notice.

Does disputing with a collector also correct my credit report?

No. If the collection information is inaccurate, dispute the credit-report entry separately with each reporting company that shows it and, when appropriate, with the furnisher.
