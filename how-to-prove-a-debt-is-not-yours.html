How to Show a Collection Debt Is Not Yours

Organize account records and identify a mistaken or fraudulent debt before disputing a collector claim and credit-report entry.

The short answer

A debt you do not recognize may result from a mix-up, a sold account under a new name, or identity theft. Identify which situation fits before choosing evidence and a dispute route.

Request details about the debt

Read the validation notice and ask for the creditor, account and amount if unclear. Compare them with your own statements and reports; a new collector name alone does not prove the account is someone else’s.

Collect relevant evidence

Keep records that show a different account owner, an already-paid balance, an account you never opened or an identity-theft report when applicable. Explain only facts you can support.

Use both routes when necessary

Dispute the debt with the collector and, if it appears on a credit report, separately dispute the inaccurate entry with the bureau and furnisher. Save copies and review each response.

Frequently asked questions

Does this page guarantee a deletion or score increase?

No. The outcome depends on the facts, the reporting and applicable rules.

Should I keep copies of my records?

Yes. Keep notices, correspondence, payment evidence and responses related to the account.
