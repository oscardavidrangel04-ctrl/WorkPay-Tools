Accrued PTO vs Unlimited PTO: What to Compare

Compare accrued PTO and unlimited PTO policies, including approval, tracking and payout questions to review in writing.

The short answer

Accrued PTO gives employees time off that is earned under a stated policy, often by pay period or service time. Unlimited PTO generally means a policy does not set a fixed bank of days, but it still has approval, coverage and performance expectations. The employer policy and applicable law control the actual benefit.

What to check

Ask how requests are approved, whether there are minimum or maximum expectations, how sick time is handled, what happens when employment ends and whether any accrued balance is paid out. Do not assume unlimited means unrestricted or that accrued hours are automatically paid in every circumstance.

Use the right record

For an estimated gross value of a known PTO balance, use the PTO Value Calculator. It does not determine policy eligibility, payout rights or tax treatment.

Official reference

The Department of Labor notes that many benefits are governed by agreement between employer and employee rather than a single federal benefit rule. Read the source.

A PTO bank vs an approval-based policy

An accrued policy adds hours under a stated rule, often with a balance, cap or carryover limit. An unlimited policy generally does not use a fixed earned bank, but still has approval and coverage requirements. Unlimited does not mean every request is approved or that protected leave rules disappear. Review how the policy works in practice.

Worked example: accrued hours and wage value

At four PTO hours earned per pay period for 26 periods, the simple annual accrual is 104 hours before usage or caps. At an assumed $25 hourly wage, those hours have $2,600 in illustrative wage value. That is not a guaranteed termination payout. Use PTO accrual for entered earned hours and PTO value for a wage comparison.

Questions to ask about actual use and separation

Ask about approval lead time, blackout periods, expected use, carryover and what happens when employment ends. Payout treatment depends on the policy and applicable law; do not assume all accrued PTO is payable or all unlimited arrangements avoid a payout. The Department of Labor’s leave overview is a federal starting point, not a substitute for state rules or the employer policy.

Frequently asked questions

Does unlimited PTO mean unlimited approved days?

No. Approval, coverage and other policy conditions can still apply.

Is accrued PTO wage value the same as a required payout?

No. A wage-value estimate does not determine whether a payout is owed.

Can an accrual cap change the annual estimate?

Yes. Caps, usage, carryover and eligibility can change the actual balance.

Which policy is better for me?

Compare actual time-off access, policy terms and your circumstances. The label alone does not settle the comparison.
