Understand the numbers

Three pay concepts worth knowing

Short explanations help you choose the correct calculator and interpret the estimate.

01

How overtime pay works

For many covered, nonexempt workers under the federal FLSA, overtime is at least 1.5 times the regular rate after 40 hours in a workweek. State rules, exemptions and other pay components can change the calculation.

Explore overtime pay →
02

Hourly wage vs. salary

An hourly-to-salary conversion is a planning estimate based on your wage, weekly hours and paid weeks. It helps compare compensation formats but does not determine employment classification.

Convert hourly to salary →
03

Time cards and unpaid breaks

A time-card estimate starts with elapsed time between clock-in and clock-out and then subtracts any break you enter. Payroll systems and employer policies can use additional rounding or timekeeping rules.

Frequently asked questions

About WorkPay Tools

Are WorkPay Tools calculators free?

Yes. The calculators are free to use and do not require an account or registration.

Do these calculators include taxes?

Most tools estimate gross pay before taxes, benefits and other deductions. The page for each calculator explains what is and is not included.

Are my calculator inputs stored?

Core calculations run in your browser. Some tools may use local browser storage for convenience, but no account is required and the calculator pages explain relevant assumptions.

Which calculator should I use?

Use Overtime Pay for overtime earnings, Time Card for clock-in and clock-out hours, Work Hours for weekly schedules, and the Hourly to Salary or Salary to Hourly tools for pay conversions.

Are overtime rules the same in every state?

No. Federal law provides a baseline for many covered nonexempt workers, but state law, exemptions, contracts and employer policies can differ. Use the calculators as educational planning estimates rather than legal determinations.
