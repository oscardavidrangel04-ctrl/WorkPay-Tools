Exempt vs Nonexempt Employee: Basic Overtime Difference

Learn the basic difference between exempt and nonexempt classifications and why job duties and pay rules matter.

The short answer

In a US wage-and-hour context, nonexempt employees are generally entitled to minimum wage and overtime protections under the FLSA unless an exemption applies. Exempt status is not determined by job title alone. Duties, salary basis, salary level and other applicable requirements can matter.

Check duties and applicable exemption requirements

Being salaried does not automatically mean exempt, and being hourly does not automatically answer every classification question. State law can provide additional protections. Employers should evaluate classification under current law; employees with a concern can seek official guidance.

Use the right record

For overtime estimates, use the site’s calculator only when its assumptions match the actual pay arrangement. It cannot determine legal classification or amounts owed.

Official reference

The Department of Labor states that many employees are entitled to overtime at not less than time and one-half after 40 hours in a workweek, subject to applicable exemptions. Read the source.

Pay basis and overtime status are different questions

A salary quote describes how pay is expressed; it does not establish an overtime exemption. Before using an overtime estimate, record the actual duties, pay arrangement and hours worked in each workweek. Ask which exemption, if any, the employer applies.

Use the current Department of Labor guidance for the relevant category and check state requirements. This guide does not determine a worker's classification.

Source: DOL Fact Sheet 17A: exemption tests.

Frequently asked questions

Does being paid a salary automatically make me exempt?

No. Salary alone does not establish exempt status. The requirements depend on the exemption; duties and applicable pay tests can matter.

Does a manager title determine overtime eligibility?

No. Actual job duties and the applicable legal tests matter, not the job title by itself.

Can an overtime calculator decide whether I am nonexempt?

No. A calculator applies entered rates and hours. It cannot determine legal classification or whether particular hours qualify for overtime.
