Exempt vs Nonexempt Employee: Basic Overtime Difference

Learn the basic difference between exempt and nonexempt classifications and why job duties and pay rules matter.

The short answer

In a US wage-and-hour context, nonexempt employees are generally entitled to minimum wage and overtime protections under the FLSA unless an exemption applies. Exempt status is not determined by job title alone. Duties, salary basis, salary level and other applicable requirements can matter.

What to check

Being salaried does not automatically mean exempt, and being hourly does not automatically answer every classification question. State law can provide additional protections. Employers should evaluate classification under current law; employees with a concern can seek official guidance.

Use the right record

For overtime estimates, use the site’s calculator only when its assumptions match the actual pay arrangement. It cannot determine legal classification or amounts owed.

Official reference

The Department of Labor states that many employees are entitled to overtime at not less than time and one-half after 40 hours in a workweek, subject to applicable exemptions. Read the source.
