Can Debt Collectors Call You at Work?

Learn when a debt collector may call at work, limits on contacting your employer, and how to ask that workplace calls stop.

How the rule works

A collector may contact you about a debt, but federal rules limit inconvenient workplace contact and what the collector may tell other people.

Tell the collector when work calls are not allowed

If your employer prohibits personal calls or work is an inconvenient place to talk, tell the collector clearly. Keep a dated record of the call or message. The CFPB says a collector aware that you cannot receive these calls at work must not contact you there.

Can a collector speak to your employer?

A collector may in limited circumstances seek location information, such as where you work or how to reach you. Generally, the collector may not tell your employer that you owe a debt. Ask a person who took the call what was said and keep the details.

If the calls continue

Document dates, numbers, messages and your request. Review the CFPB guidance and consider submitting a complaint or seeking legal assistance if the collector disregards the restriction. A request about work calls does not itself erase a valid debt.

How to ask a collector to stop workplace calls

Make the restriction clear: “My employer does not allow personal collection calls at work. Do not call this workplace number.” If work is an inconvenient place to talk, explain that too. Note the date, caller’s name and number, and how you communicated the restriction.

Keep a record you can use later

  • The workplace number the collector used.
  • When you told it the calls were prohibited or inconvenient.
  • Any later calls, messages or disclosures.
  • What a coworker or employer heard, in their own words.

A written follow-up can help preserve the request. Limiting workplace contact is different from requesting that all contact stop; the guide to stopping collection calls explains that distinction.

Does the rule cover an original creditor calling its own customer?

The federal FDCPA generally concerns third-party debt collectors. Original creditors collecting their own debts are usually outside that law’s coverage, although other federal and state protections may apply. Identify who is calling before deciding which rule applies.

For example, asking a coworker for your contact details and announcing an unpaid debt to that coworker are different conduct. Keep the exact message and consult the third-party contact guide for the limits on disclosure.

Frequently asked questions

Can I tell a collector that work is an inconvenient place to talk?

Yes. Tell the collector clearly and keep a dated record of the restriction.

Can a collector tell my boss I owe money?

Generally, a covered collector cannot disclose your debt to your employer when seeking contact information.

Does stopping workplace calls cancel the debt?

No. Restricting contact at work does not resolve the balance or prevent other lawful collection action.
