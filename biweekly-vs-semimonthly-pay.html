Biweekly vs Semimonthly Pay: What’s the Difference?

Biweekly pay means a paycheck every two weeks, usually 26 per year. Semimonthly pay means two paychecks each month, exactly 24 per year. For a fixed annual salary, the schedule changes individual check size and payday timing—not annual gross salary.

Biweekly pay vs semi monthly paycheck example

For a $60,000 annual salary, a standard biweekly gross paycheck is $60,000 ÷ 26 = $2,307.69. A semimonthly gross paycheck is $60,000 ÷ 24 = $2,500.

The semimonthly check is larger, but there are two fewer checks. Before taxes and deductions, both schedules still total $60,000 over a full year. Calculate a custom amount with the Biweekly Pay Calculator or Semimonthly Pay Calculator.

Why biweekly payroll creates three-paycheck months

A biweekly schedule repeats every 14 days. Twenty-six checks spread across 12 months means most months contain two paydays and two months usually contain three. The exact months depend on the employer’s payroll calendar. A semimonthly schedule stays at two paydays per month, often near the middle and end of the month.

Biweekly vs semimonthly pay for hourly employees

For hourly workers, gross pay depends on recorded hours within the employer’s pay period. Biweekly periods usually contain two seven-day weeks, while semimonthly periods contain a changing number of calendar days. That can make hours—and therefore gross checks—less uniform on a semimonthly schedule.

Overtime eligibility and calculation are separate from pay frequency. A paycheck may cover two weeks or part of two months, but applicable overtime rules can still require hours to be evaluated by workweek.

Which pay schedule is better?

Neither schedule raises a fixed annual salary. Biweekly pay offers a predictable weekday and can align well with two-week budgeting. Semimonthly pay offers exactly two checks per month and slightly larger salary checks. The more useful choice depends on bill timing, hourly tracking, payroll administration and personal budgeting.

Biweekly vs semimonthly pay FAQ

What is the difference between biweekly and semimonthly pay?

Biweekly pay occurs every 14 days and normally produces 26 paychecks per year. Semimonthly pay occurs twice per month and produces 24 paychecks per year.

Which paycheck is larger, biweekly or semimonthly?

For the same annual salary, a semimonthly paycheck is usually larger because the salary is divided across 24 checks instead of 26. Total annual gross salary remains the same.

Is biweekly pay the same as twice a month?

No. Every two weeks creates 26 pay periods in most years, while twice a month creates exactly 24 pay periods.

How do overtime hours fit into these schedules?

Pay frequency does not by itself set an overtime workweek. Employers generally track overtime using the applicable workweek and payroll rules, even when the paycheck covers a longer period.
