Personal Loan vs Credit Card Debt

A personal loan is commonly an installment obligation; credit card debt is generally revolving and may have changing required payments.

Compare rates, fees and the repayment term

Compare APR, fees, term, collateral, payment schedule and the consequences of using a loan to consolidate existing card balances.

Compare equivalent payoff plans

To compare an installment loan with card debt, start with the same amount to repay and a clear payment schedule. A smaller monthly loan payment is not enough evidence of savings if the loan runs for much longer.

A practical offer worksheet

  • Amount available after any deducted fees.
  • APR and whether the rate can change.
  • Number of payments and total scheduled repayment.
  • Any separately paid fees or early-payoff conditions.
  • Amounts left on cards after the loan proceeds are applied.

For illustration, 24 payments of $300 total $7,200 before separate charges. Compare that total with a card-payoff estimate using an explicit monthly payment and no new purchases. A plan that assumes continued card spending is not the same comparison. Keep the original statements until all payments and residual amounts are reconciled.

Reference: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau guidance.

After consolidation, reconcile every old account

A new loan can change who you owe without reducing what you owe on day one. If you borrow $6,000 to pay two cards with balances of $2,000 and $4,000, those cards must actually receive the payments. Until they do, a loan approval alone has not paid them off.

A lower card balance is not extra income

Using the cards again after consolidation adds new borrowing alongside the loan. For your budget, record the new loan payment, any remaining card minimums and any final interest or charges. Check the following statements instead of assuming that a transfer confirmation means every account is at zero.

For a practical reconciliation, list each creditor, the payoff amount used, payment confirmation, remaining balance and next due date. This supports a debt payoff plan that follows the money rather than only the advertised monthly payment.

Personal Loan vs Credit Card Debt FAQ

Does taking a personal loan automatically close my credit cards?

Do not assume so. Confirm each account status with the issuer. Paying a balance and closing an account are separate actions.

Can consolidation leave me owing more?

Yes. Fees, unpaid residual balances and new card spending can add costs. Compare the full repayment plan, not just the new loan payment.
