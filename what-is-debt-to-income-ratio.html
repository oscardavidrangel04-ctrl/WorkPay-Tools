What Is Debt-to-Income Ratio?

Learn the basic debt-to-income ratio formula, why gross monthly income is commonly used, and what it does not measure.

The short answer

Debt-to-income ratio, or DTI, compares monthly debt payments with gross monthly income. A simple calculation is monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income, expressed as a percentage. Lenders can define the inputs differently, so a personal estimate is not a lending decision.

What to check

DTI does not show every part of a household budget. It may not capture variable spending, savings needs, income instability or all bills. A lower ratio is not a guarantee of approval, and a higher ratio does not by itself prove that a payment cannot be managed.

Use the right information

Use current statements to identify recurring debt payments and use a representative gross-income figure. Keep the calculation separate from net pay and from credit utilization; they answer different questions.

Official reference

The CFPB describes DTI as a measure of how much pressure debt payments put on a budget and uses gross monthly income for a basic calculation. Read the source.

Frequently asked questions

What should I use for an account-specific answer?

Use the current account agreement, statement or official plan document. General terms do not override those records.
