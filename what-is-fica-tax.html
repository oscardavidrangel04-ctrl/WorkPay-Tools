What Is FICA Tax on a Paycheck?

FICA is the payroll-tax label for Social Security and Medicare taxes withheld from many employee paychecks. It is separate from federal income tax withholding, even though both can appear on the same pay stub.

What does FICA stand for?

FICA stands for the Federal Insurance Contributions Act. It is the federal employment-tax framework that funds Social Security and Medicare. On a pay stub, it may appear as “FICA,” “Social Security,” “OASDI,” “Medicare,” or similar payroll labels.

Employers generally withhold the employee share from covered wages and also pay an employer share. The Social Security Administration explains that FICA and the self-employment counterpart, SECA, fund Social Security and Medicare programs.

The two taxes inside FICA

ComponentWhat it fundsHow it may appear on a pay stub
Social Security taxSocial Security program fundingSocial Security, OASDI or FICA-SS
Medicare taxMedicare program fundingMedicare, MED or FICA-MED

Payroll systems often list the two components separately. That does not necessarily mean you are being charged two unrelated payroll taxes; both are commonly discussed together as FICA.

FICA tax vs federal income tax withholding

FICA and federal income tax withholding are different paycheck deductions. Federal income tax withholding is affected by Form W-4 information and tax rules. FICA is tied to Social Security and Medicare employment taxes on covered wages.

A change in federal withholding does not automatically change FICA in the same way. Likewise, a traditional pre-tax 401(k) contribution can affect federal income-tax wages without necessarily reducing Social Security and Medicare wages. See do 401(k) contributions reduce taxable income? for that distinction.

How FICA appears on a pay stub

Look for both the current-period amount and the year-to-date amount. The current amount is for that paycheck; the year-to-date figure is the total withheld so far in the calendar year. A pay stub can show Social Security and Medicare separately, along with federal income-tax withholding, state withholding and benefit deductions.

Why the FICA amount can change during the year

The Social Security and Medicare components do not always behave the same way. Social Security tax has a wage base that can change by year, while Medicare tax has different rules. Additional Medicare tax can apply above statutory wage thresholds.

Other changes can come from a different gross-pay amount, bonus wages, a corrected payroll entry or a change in employment status. Do not use one paycheck alone to assume the whole-year result. Current IRS and employer payroll records are the best sources for a personal question.

Employees, employers and self-employed workers

Employees

For many employees, the employer withholds the employee share of Social Security and Medicare taxes from each paycheck and pays an employer share separately. The employer share normally does not appear as an additional deduction from the employee's net pay.

Self-employed workers

Self-employed people generally pay Social Security and Medicare taxes under SECA rather than through employee FICA withholding. That calculation and reporting are different from reading a W-2 paycheck, so this page does not calculate a self-employment tax bill.

FICA Tax FAQ

What is FICA tax?

FICA stands for the Federal Insurance Contributions Act. It refers to employment taxes that fund Social Security and Medicare and are commonly withheld from employee paychecks.

Is FICA the same as federal income tax?

No. FICA funds Social Security and Medicare, while federal income tax withholding is a separate paycheck deduction.

Why does my pay stub list Social Security and Medicare separately?

They are the two components of FICA and are often shown on separate payroll lines.

Do self-employed people pay FICA?

They generally pay Social Security and Medicare taxes under SECA instead of employee FICA withholding.
