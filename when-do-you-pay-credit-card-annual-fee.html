Credit card annual fees

When Do You Pay a Credit Card Annual Fee?

A credit card annual fee is often charged near account opening and again around the account anniversary. You normally pay it through the statement on which it appears; the posting date and payment due date are different. Your issuer, agreement and any first-year waiver determine the exact timing.

What does an annual fee mean on a credit card?

An annual fee is the price of keeping a particular card account, separate from the interest charged on borrowed money. Some cards have no annual fee. Others charge one for their account features or benefits; using those benefits is not what triggers each yearly fee.

For budgeting, a $120 yearly fee averages $10 per month, but that does not mean the issuer bills $10 monthly. Unless the agreement provides installments, plan for the fee as a charge when it posts.

How to read the annual fee in a card offer

Check both the amount and the period it covers. An offer saying “$0 introductory annual fee for the first year, then $95” describes a first-year waiver followed by a recurring fee under the terms. It does not mean the account will remain free in later years.

Compare three items before opening or renewing: the annual fee in the disclosure, any first-year waiver in the offer, and the fee actually posted on your statement. If they do not agree, contact the issuer with the offer details. Keep the payment due date in view while the issuer reviews the charge.

For the separate payment deadline after a fee posts, see how a billing period and due date work.

When is a credit card annual fee charged?

Many cards with an annual fee add it to the first statement after the account is opened, although timing can vary by issuer and offer. The fee can appear even when you have not made a purchase, because it is an account charge rather than a charge for one transaction.

Check the card’s rates-and-fees disclosure before applying and review the first statement after opening. A promotional offer may waive the first year, but the offer terms control.

When does the annual fee renew?

After the first year, many issuers bill the fee on or around the account anniversary or in the related billing cycle. The exact posting date, renewal schedule and whether any fee is waived are account-specific, so use your statement history and card agreement instead of assuming a calendar-year charge.

Annual fee vs APR and late fees

ChargeWhat it isTypical timing
Annual feeAn account charge for having a particular card.Often at opening and then around each account anniversary, subject to the agreement.
Purchase interest (APR)The cost of carrying eligible unpaid purchases or other balances.Depends on the balance, transaction type and account terms.
Late feeA possible charge for failing to meet the required payment deadline.Only when the account’s payment terms are not met.

An annual fee may be charged even if you pay purchases in full and never incur purchase interest. APR matters when borrowing costs apply. Compare each charge separately when evaluating a card.

Can you avoid or get a refund of an annual fee?

Before renewal, ask whether your account qualifies for a waiver, a change to a no-annual-fee card or cancellation. Approval and any refund depend on the issuer and your agreement. A request by itself does not remove a charge or change the payment due date.

Questions to ask before deciding

  • Has the renewal fee posted, and what date starts any refund window?
  • Would a product change remove the fee, and when would it take effect?
  • Would closing or changing the card affect rewards, credits or a welcome offer?
  • What balance and scheduled payments will remain afterward?

Save the issuer's confirmation and check for the actual credit or account change. If you are considering closure, read how closing a card can affect your credit before choosing.

What to review before an anniversary

  1. Check whether the fee has posted or is expected in the next billing cycle.
  2. Check whether a first-year waiver, spending waiver or promotional term applies.
  3. Compare the benefits, rewards or protections you actually used with the fee.
  4. Review the current issuer policy for a waiver request, product change, cancellation or any refund after the fee posts.

Those options are not guaranteed and can differ by issuer. Closing an account can affect available credit and other account features, so evaluate that separately before acting.

Is the annual fee worth paying again?

Review the benefits you actually used before the renewal date. For a simple comparison, suppose a card costs $95 per year and provides $130 of benefits you would otherwise have paid for. That leaves $35 of value before interest or other fees. Unused perks have no value in this calculation.

Compare that result with a no-fee alternative. A one-time welcome offer should not be counted as a recurring benefit in later years. Paying purchase balances in full does not automatically waive the annual fee.

When do you pay the annual fee after it is charged?

The annual fee normally appears as a line item on the card statement and becomes part of the account balance for that cycle. Review the statement’s minimum payment and due date, then check the agreement for how interest and any grace period apply to the fee and other balances.

Annual fee payment example

Suppose a $95 annual fee posts on September 10 and appears on a statement closing September 18, with an October 13 due date. September 10 is the charge date; October 13 is the deadline on that bill. If the statement also contains $400 in purchases and no other activity, its balance would be $495.

Paying only the $95 fee does not clear that statement balance. Check the minimum due and the terms for avoiding interest. For a first-year waiver, confirm when the waiver ends; it does not necessarily remove the renewal fee. See minimum payment vs statement balance for the payment amounts.

Check the actual account terms

Credit card rates, fees, posting rules and promotional terms can vary by issuer and transaction. Read your current statement and agreement for the account-specific answer. Frequently asked questions

Is an annual fee charged monthly?

An annual fee is generally a yearly charge, but the agreement tells you how and when it is billed.

When will my first annual fee appear?

Many cards bill it on the first statement after account opening, but the disclosure and account terms determine the timing. A first-year waiver may apply only when stated in the offer.

Does paying in full waive an annual fee?

Not automatically. Paying in full generally addresses interest, not an account fee.

Can I ask for an annual-fee waiver or change cards?

You can ask the issuer, but a waiver, product change, refund or cancellation option is not guaranteed. Review the current terms of the issuer before making a decision.

Where is the fee shown?

The current statement and card disclosures should show the fee and its amount.

Can an annual fee be charged if I never use the card?

Yes. An annual fee is an account charge and may apply even without purchases. Review the opening offer, any first-year waiver and the renewal terms.
