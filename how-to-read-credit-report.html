How to Read a Credit Report: A Practical Guide

A credit report records account information; it is not the same thing as a credit score.

The short answer

Read personal information, account history and inquiries section by section, then compare entries with your own records.

What to review

Note the report date and bureau, then look for unfamiliar accounts, incorrect balances or inaccurate payment history.

How to read a credit report section by section

Start with identifying information, then review each account, collection, public-record section and inquiry. Compare balances, payment status, dates and ownership with your records. A credit report is not the same thing as a credit score.

Related questions

What should I check first?

Look for accounts you do not recognize, incorrect late-payment status, wrong balances and duplicate collection entries.

Why is a closed account still listed?

Closed accounts can remain on a report for a period of time. Check whether the status and dates are accurate.
