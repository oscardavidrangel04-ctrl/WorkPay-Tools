Credit card APR categories

Purchase APR vs Cash Advance APR

Purchase APR applies to eligible purchases. Cash advance APR applies to transactions the issuer classifies as cash advances. The rate, fee and interest-start date can differ even on the same card.

Purchase APR vs cash advance APR at a glance

FeaturePurchasesCash advances
Applicable ratePurchase APRCash advance APR
Grace periodMay apply when account conditions are metOften no purchase-style grace period
Transaction feeUsually not an advance feeMay include a flat or percentage fee
Interest timingDepends on grace-period statusMay begin on the transaction date

What purchase APR means

Purchase APR is the annual percentage rate used for eligible purchases when interest applies. If the card provides a grace period and its conditions are met, paying the applicable statement balance in full by the due date can avoid interest on purchases.

What cash advance APR means

A balance transfer is different from a cash advance; the purpose, fee and interest timing can all differ.

Cash advance APR applies to cash withdrawals and other transactions classified by the issuer as advances. These transactions may accrue interest immediately and may carry an additional fee. The card agreement defines which transactions qualify.

Why the advertised rate is not the whole cost

Compare the APR, transaction fee and the number of days interest can accrue. A cash advance with a higher rate, an upfront fee and no grace period can cost more than a purchase even when the dollar amount is the same.

The credit card interest calculator can model a simple constant-balance estimate, but it does not add transaction fees or reproduce issuer allocation rules.

Cash advance cost example: fee plus daily interest

Assume a hypothetical $500 advance has a 30% APR and a fee of 5% or $10, whichever is greater. The fee is $25 because 5% of $500 exceeds $10. If the $500 principal remains unpaid for 20 days, a simple estimate using a 365-day year is:

$500 × 0.30 ÷ 365 × 20 = $8.22 in interest. Adding the $25 fee gives $33.22 in estimated borrowing costs, before any ATM fee.

This illustration keeps the principal constant and excludes interest on the fee, compounding, payments and other account activity. It is not an issuer payoff quote. The agreement determines the actual balance method and day count. A purchase of the same amount could have a different result if a purchase grace period applies. For promotional offers, check the difference between 0% APR and deferred interest.

Purchase and Cash Advance APR FAQ

Is cash advance APR always higher?

Not always, but it can be. Compare the exact APRs and fees in the card's current disclosures.

Can buying a cash equivalent count as a cash advance?

Some issuers classify certain cash-like transactions as advances. Check the agreement and transaction coding.

Does paying the statement balance stop cash advance interest?

Payment can reduce the balance, but interest timing and allocation depend on the account terms and applicable law.

Can you withdraw money from a credit card?

Some cards allow ATM or other cash withdrawals as cash advances. This borrows against the card account; it is not a withdrawal from your checking balance or a redemption of cash-back rewards. Citi describes cash advances as a separate transaction category.

  1. Check whether your account permits the withdrawal and how much cash-advance credit is available. Do not assume the entire purchase limit can be withdrawn.
  2. Read the advance APR, transaction fee and any ATM charge before confirming.
  3. Check when interest starts and how you will repay it. A purchase grace period generally does not postpone cash-advance interest.

If the purpose is to move an existing debt, compare the balance-transfer offer instead of assuming an ATM withdrawal receives its promotional terms.

Related questions

Does a low purchase APR apply to cash advances?

Not unless the agreement explicitly says so; rates are commonly separate.

Can a cash advance cost both a fee and interest?

Yes. The transaction fee and interest are separate costs.
