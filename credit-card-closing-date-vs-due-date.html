Credit card billing dates

Credit Card Closing Date vs Due Date

The closing date ends the billing cycle and creates the statement. The due date is the deadline for the payment required on that statement. They describe different events and normally are not the same day.

Closing date vs due date at a glance

Date What happens What to check
Closing or statement date The issuer ends the cycle and calculates the statement balance Purchases and credits that posted during the cycle
Payment due date The required statement payment is due Minimum due, statement balance and issuer cutoff time

How the timeline works

The billing period guide explains which posted transactions are captured when the cycle closes. An early payment can change the next snapshot; see paying before the statement closes.

  1. Transactions post during the billing cycle.
  2. The cycle closes and the issuer generates a statement balance.
  3. The statement lists a minimum payment and payment due date.
  4. New activity after closing generally belongs to the next cycle, even though it can change the current balance.

For example, a statement that closes on May 5 may show a later payment deadline. Use the dates printed on the actual statement; do not assume every issuer uses the same interval.

Why the statement date matters

The closing date separates one statement period from the next. A purchase made near that date may post before or after the cycle closes. Pending activity is not always included until it posts, and issuer displays vary.

That is why the statement balance and current balance can differ after the statement is issued.

How to find your closing date and due date

Open the latest statement or sign in to the card issuer’s website or app. Look for the statement closing date, statement balance, minimum payment due and payment due date. If you are planning a payment around the cycle boundary, confirm the next closing date with the issuer because processing and posting can affect which statement includes an activity.

When is the best time to pay a credit card?

The due date is the deadline for the required payment. Paying before the closing date may lower the balance shown on that statement if the payment posts in time, but it does not replace paying the amount required by the due date. Some people pay earlier to manage cash flow or reduce a reported balance; issuer reporting dates and account terms vary. For timing examples, see whether to pay before the statement closes.

Why the due date matters

For payment cutoffs and receipt rules, see when a credit card payment is late. A missed deadline can also cause a late fee.

The due date controls whether the statement's required payment is on time. Pay at least the required amount by the issuer's cutoff. If the account offers a purchase grace period and its conditions are met, paying the applicable statement balance in full by the due date can avoid purchase interest. Cash advances and other balances may not receive the same treatment.

A payment made before the closing date can change the balance reported on that statement, while a payment by the due date addresses the required payment for that cycle. Those are different decisions; read whether to pay before the statement closes for that distinction.

Official sources: CFPB guidance on credit card grace periods and CFPB credit card resources.

Closing date vs due date example

Suppose a card closes with a $900 statement balance. A $120 purchase posts two days later, so the current balance becomes $1,020. The statement still shows $900 for that completed cycle. The minimum payment and due date remain the amounts and date on that statement unless the issuer provides an adjustment.

Credit Card Date FAQ

What is the difference between a credit card closing date and due date?

The closing date ends a billing cycle and produces a statement. The due date is the deadline for the required payment shown on that statement.

Is the statement date the same as the closing date?

The terms commonly refer to the end of the billing cycle, although account labels and processing times can vary by issuer.

Should I pay on the closing date or due date?

Pay at least the required amount by the due date. Paying earlier may change the balance that is reported, but account treatment varies.

How do I find my credit card closing date?

Check the latest statement or the issuer's website or app. Confirm the current account information because the next cycle's date may vary slightly.

What is the best time to pay a credit card?

Pay at least the required amount by the due date. Paying before the closing date may affect the statement balance if the payment posts in time; the right timing depends on your goal and account terms.
