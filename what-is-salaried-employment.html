Salary basics

What Is Salaried Employment?

Salaried employment generally means a worker receives a predetermined amount of pay on a regular pay-period basis rather than having all base pay determined only by the number of hours worked. Salary is often quoted annually—for example, $60,000 per year—even though the employee receives paychecks throughout the year.

How a salary is converted into pay periods

A $60,000 annual salary can be expressed as approximately $5,000 per month, $2,500 per semimonthly period or $2,307.69 per biweekly period before taxes and deductions. The exact payroll amount can vary with employer practices and deductions.

Use the Salary to Hourly Calculator when you want an hourly equivalent, or the Biweekly Pay Calculator for a two-week estimate.

Salary and hourly equivalent are not the same thing

An annual salary can be converted to an hourly equivalent for comparison, but that does not turn the position into an hourly job. The conversion simply divides the annual amount by assumed work hours. For example, $60,000 ÷ 2,080 hours is about $28.85 per hour.

Salaried does not automatically mean overtime-exempt

Under U.S. federal law, being paid on a salary basis is only one part of certain overtime exemptions. Job duties and other requirements also matter, and state rules can differ. A job title or the word “salary” by itself does not decide the issue.

See the U.S. Department of Labor salary-basis guidance for official federal information.

Base salary vs total compensation

Base salary is only one part of an offer. Bonus opportunities, employer retirement contributions, paid benefits, equity and paid time off can affect the overall economic value of a role. Use the Compensation Package Calculator to compare selected components on an annual basis.

Salaried Employment FAQ

What is a salaried employee?

A salaried employee is generally paid a predetermined salary for a pay period rather than being quoted only as an hourly rate. Specific payroll and legal rules depend on the job and jurisdiction.

Is a salaried employee always exempt from overtime?

No. Salary payment alone does not determine exempt status. Applicable legal tests and job duties matter.

Can you convert salary to an hourly equivalent?

Yes. Divide annual salary by an assumed number of annual work hours, such as 2,080 for 40 hours per week across 52 weeks.

Does a salary include benefits?

Base salary and benefits are separate concepts. Total compensation may include salary plus bonuses and employer-paid benefits.
