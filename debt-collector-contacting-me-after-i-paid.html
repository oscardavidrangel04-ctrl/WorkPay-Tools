Debt Collector Contacting You After You Paid?

What to check when a collector contacts you about a debt you already paid, including receipts, account identity and reporting errors.

The short answer

An unexpected collection call after payment calls for a records check, not an immediate second payment. Confirm that the collector is referring to the same account and amount.

Match the payment to the account

Put the collection notice next to your receipt and agreement. Match the account identifier, creditor, payment recipient, payment date and amount. A matching dollar amount alone is not enough when you have several accounts.

Paid in full, settled, or paid an installment?

Suppose a notice lists $800 and you have a $600 receipt. If a written agreement accepted $600 to settle that specific account and you met its terms, include both the agreement and payment evidence. If $600 was only an installment, the receipt alone does not show that the remaining $200 was forgiven. Ask for a written explanation of any amount still claimed.

If you lost the receipt, ask the company you paid for a payment history or confirmation. The CFPB describes the records to send after a paid-debt collection contact.

Respond with the specific discrepancy

Ask for validation information and tell the collector which payment or amount is missing. Send copies, not irreplaceable originals, and retain the correspondence.

Check the credit report separately

If the reported balance or status is wrong, dispute that exact field with the bureau and furnisher. A paid collection may still appear accurately with an updated balance or status.

Frequently asked questions

Should I pay again to make the calls stop?

First verify the collector and reconcile the account with your payment records. A repeated demand is not proof that you owe the same amount twice.

What if I paid a different collection agency?

Keep the earlier notice, payment receipt and any settlement terms. Identify which company received the money and ask the current collector to explain how that payment appears in its account history.

Does a receipt prove that the whole debt was settled?

A receipt proves a payment. Whether it resolved the full account also depends on the balance and any written settlement agreement. Compare both records.
