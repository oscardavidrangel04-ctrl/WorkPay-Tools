Credit Card Grace Period After Carrying a Balance

Understand what can happen to a credit card grace period after carrying a balance and why new purchases may accrue interest.

The short answer

A purchase grace period can depend on paying the required balance in full by the due date. After carrying a balance, new purchases may begin accruing interest under the account terms even before the next statement is due.

How a grace period can be lost

If the conditions for the purchase grace period are not met, interest can apply to unpaid purchases and potentially to new purchases from their transaction dates.

How a grace period may return

Paying the account in full may restore grace-period treatment, but timing and residual interest can span another billing cycle. Ask the issuer how its agreement handles restoration.

Cash advances are different

Cash advances commonly do not receive the same purchase grace period and can have a separate APR and fee.

Official reference

Review current guidance from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the terms of the specific account.

Frequently asked questions

Does paying the minimum preserve the grace period?

Generally, paying only the minimum does not equal paying the applicable statement balance in full.

Can interest appear after I pay the account off?

Yes. Interest accrued before the payoff can appear on a later statement.

Do all cards offer a grace period?

No. Review the card agreement and statement disclosures.
