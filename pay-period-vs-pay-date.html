Pay Period vs Pay Date: What Is the Difference?

Understand the difference between a pay period and pay date, why they can be different, and how each appears on a pay stub.

The short answer

A pay period is the span of work time for which you are being paid. A pay date is the date the employer issues or dates the payment. They are related but are not interchangeable. A biweekly pay period can end several days before the corresponding pay date because payroll must process time, earnings and deductions.

What to check

Use the pay-period start and end dates to check whether hours, overtime, tips or other earnings belong on that statement. Use the pay date for cash-flow planning and to identify when funds are expected to be issued. Your employer’s pay calendar, not a generic schedule, controls the actual timing.

Use the right record

If a check seems short, compare the covered work dates before assuming the pay date marks the work performed. For a conversion estimate, see the site’s pay-frequency tools; those calculate gross estimates and do not replace an employer pay calendar.

Official reference

The IRS distinguishes the pay period from the pay date when explaining how to review paycheck information. Read the source.
