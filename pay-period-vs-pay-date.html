Pay Period vs Pay Date: What Is the Difference?

Understand the difference between a pay period and pay date, why they can be different, and how each appears on a pay stub.

The short answer

A pay period is the span of work time for which you are being paid. A pay date is the date the employer issues or dates the payment. They are related but are not interchangeable. A biweekly pay period can end several days before the corresponding pay date because payroll must process time, earnings and deductions.

Match work dates to the correct paycheck

Use the pay-period start and end dates to check whether hours, overtime, tips or other earnings belong on that statement. Use the pay date for cash-flow planning and to identify when funds are expected to be issued. Your employer’s pay calendar, not a generic schedule, controls the actual timing.

Use the right record

If a check seems short, compare the covered work dates before assuming the pay date marks the work performed. For a conversion estimate, see the site’s pay-frequency tools; those calculate gross estimates and do not replace an employer pay calendar.

Official reference

The IRS distinguishes the pay period from the pay date when explaining how to review paycheck information. Read the source.

Example: work dates before payday

An illustrative pay period might cover October 1 through October 14, with payment scheduled for October 20. The first two dates identify the work being paid; October 20 identifies the scheduled payday. Work after October 14 normally belongs to a different period under that example calendar.

This is not a required payroll schedule. Compare the employer's calendar, time records and pay stub if a shift seems missing. Bank availability and the scheduled payday are also distinct details to confirm.

Source: IRS Publication 4268: payroll records.

Frequently asked questions

Can the pay date be after the pay period ends?

Yes. Payroll schedules can include time between the end of the work period and the scheduled payment. Use the employer calendar and applicable wage-payment rules for the actual schedule.

Which date tells me what work the check covers?

The pay-period start and end dates identify the covered period. The pay date alone does not identify all the work included.

Does a biweekly schedule mean two paydays every month?

No. Every two weeks and twice a month are different schedules. Check the calendar rather than assuming each month contains exactly two biweekly paydays.
