What Is a Credit Card Grace Period?

Learn what a credit card grace period is, why it can apply differently by balance type, and where to check the card agreement.

The short answer

A grace period is a period during which interest is not charged on a particular balance, if the account meets the agreement’s conditions. A card issuer is not required to offer a grace period for every transaction type. Purchases, balance transfers and cash advances can have different terms.

What to check

The agreement tells you whether a grace period applies and what must be paid by the due date. Paying the full account balance required under the agreement can be important for purchase grace-period eligibility. Carrying a balance or using a different transaction type can change the result.

Use the right information

Read the statement’s interest-charge and payment sections, then use the card agreement to verify the applicable terms. This guide does not determine whether your specific account is accruing interest.

Official reference

The CFPB explains that a grace period can be conditional and may apply to a specific balance rather than every transaction. Read the source.

Frequently asked questions

What should I use for an account-specific answer?

Use the current account agreement, statement or official plan document. General terms do not override those records.
