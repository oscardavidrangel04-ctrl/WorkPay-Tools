What Is a Credit Card Grace Period? Dates and Examples

Learn what a credit card grace period is, why it can apply differently by balance type, and where to check the card agreement.

How the rule works

A grace period is a period during which interest is not charged on a particular balance, if the account meets the agreement’s conditions. A card issuer is not required to offer a grace period for every transaction type. Purchases, balance transfers and cash advances can have different terms.

What to check

The agreement tells you whether a grace period applies and what must be paid by the due date. Paying the full account balance required under the agreement can be important for purchase grace-period eligibility. Carrying a balance or using a different transaction type can change the result.

Use the right information

Read the statement’s interest-charge and payment sections, then use the card agreement to verify the applicable terms. This guide does not determine whether your specific account is accruing interest.

Credit card grace period example: closing date vs due date

Suppose a statement closes on April 1 and the payment is due April 26. The interval between those dates is the statement’s grace-period window, if the account qualifies. Paying the required full purchase balance by April 26 can avoid purchase interest when the card’s conditions are met.

A purchase made after April 1 usually belongs to the next billing cycle. It does not move the April 26 deadline for the earlier statement. Compare closing date vs due date before choosing when to pay.

Does paying the minimum keep the grace period?

Paying the minimum by the deadline can meet the required payment without preserving an interest-free purchase period. The amount needed to avoid interest is different from the minimum due. Check the statement and any special balance or installment-plan instructions.

If you are already carrying a balance

New purchases may start accruing interest from their transaction dates. Restoring a grace period can take more than one statement cycle, depending on the agreement. Use the separate guide to grace periods after carrying a balance for that situation.

Cash advances and promotional balances

To distinguish the annual rate from the dollar charge, read credit card APR vs interest rate.

Cash advances generally start accruing interest immediately. A temporary 0% offer is a promotional rate, not the same as a purchase grace period. Check which transaction types qualify and when the promotion ends.

Frequently asked questions

Is a grace period extra time after the payment due date?

No. For credit cards it generally means the interval between the billing-cycle close and payment due date, subject to eligibility.

Will paying only the minimum avoid purchase interest?

Usually not. Avoiding interest generally requires paying the full amount specified under the purchase grace-period terms.

Do cash advances receive the purchase grace period?

Generally no. Cash advances usually accrue interest from the transaction date.
