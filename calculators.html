Plain-English glossary

Pay and work-hour terms used in these calculators

Understanding the inputs makes the estimates easier to interpret.

Gross pay

Earnings before taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, garnishments and other deductions are taken out.

Hourly rate

The base amount paid for one hour of work. Premiums such as overtime or shift differential may be calculated separately.

Overtime threshold

The number of workweek hours after which an overtime assumption begins. The federal default commonly used by our planning tool is 40 hours, but applicable rules can differ.

Time-and-a-half

A 1.5ÃƒÆ’Ã†â€™ÃƒÂ¢Ã¢â€šÂ¬Ã¢â‚¬Â pay multiplier. For a $20 hourly rate, a simple time-and-a-half rate would be $30 per hour.

Double time

A 2ÃƒÆ’Ã†â€™ÃƒÂ¢Ã¢â€šÂ¬Ã¢â‚¬Â pay multiplier. Whether double time is actually required depends on the rule, agreement or policy that applies.

Shift differential

An additional premium for certain shifts, such as evenings or nights. It may be stated as a dollar amount or percentage depending on the workplace.

PTO

Paid time off can include vacation or other paid leave. The PTO Value Calculator estimates wage value, not whether unused PTO must be paid out.

Workweek

A recurring seven-day period used for many payroll calculations. It is not necessarily the same as a calendar week.

Why actual payroll can differ

FAQ

Calculator library questions

Quick answers about choosing, interpreting and using the tools.

Which calculator should I use?

Use Overtime Pay for time-and-a-half estimates, Time Card for clock-in and clock-out shifts, Work Hours for recurring schedules, and the salary/income calculators when you want to convert earnings between periods.

Are WorkPay Tools calculators free?

Yes. The calculators are free to use and do not require an account or sign-up.

Do these calculators show take-home pay after taxes?

Usually no. Most results are gross-pay estimates before taxes, benefits, retirement contributions and other payroll deductions. Check the individual tool's assumptions before using a result for budgeting.

Which calculator should I use for overtime?

Use the Overtime Pay Calculator for regular and overtime hours with an adjustable threshold and multiplier. Use Double Time when you specifically need a 2ÃƒÆ’Ã†â€™ÃƒÂ¢Ã¢â€šÂ¬Ã¢â‚¬Â rate estimate, and Shift Differential for an added shift premium.

What is the difference between Time Card and Work Hours?

Time Card is designed for clock-in and clock-out times with breaks. Work Hours is better for a recurring schedule described by hours per shift, days worked and unpaid break time.

How do I convert hourly pay to annual salary?

Use Hourly to Salary. A common full-time planning assumption is hourly wage ÃƒÆ’Ã†â€™ÃƒÂ¢Ã¢â€šÂ¬Ã¢â‚¬Â 40 hours ÃƒÆ’Ã†â€™ÃƒÂ¢Ã¢â€šÂ¬Ã¢â‚¬Â 52 weeks, but the calculator lets you use your own weekly hours and paid weeks.

How do I estimate a biweekly paycheck?

Use Biweekly Pay when your pay period covers two weeks. The result is a gross estimate, so actual take-home pay can be lower after taxes and deductions.

Can I use these tools to check a pay stub?

You can use them as a reasonableness check for basic hours, rates and gross-pay math. They are not a payroll audit and may not reproduce employer-specific rules, rounding, taxes or deductions.

Are overtime rules the same in every state?

No. Federal rules provide a baseline for many covered workers, while states can have additional requirements. Employment status, occupation, agreements and exemptions can also change what applies.

Are my calculator inputs stored?

The core calculators are designed to run in your browser. Some convenience features, such as recently used tools or saved inputs, may use local browser storage on your device rather than requiring an account.
