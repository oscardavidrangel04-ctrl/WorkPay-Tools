About & methodology

About WorkPay Tools

WorkPay Tools is a focused collection of browser-based pay and work-hour calculators.

Reviewed August 14, 2026

How calculations are built

Each calculator starts with a narrow question and a visible formula. Worked examples are included so a visitor can independently check the arithmetic. The calculators run in the browser and do not require an account.

How rule-sensitive pages are reviewed

When a page discusses federal overtime, compensable hours or the regular rate, WorkPay Tools links to primary U.S. Department of Labor material. We distinguish the calculator's simple mathematical model from a legal determination for a specific worker.

Editorial principles

We aim to

  • Show formulas and assumptions.
  • Use plain language.
  • Cite primary government sources where appropriate.
  • State important limitations.
  • Update pages when the underlying rules or methodology change.

We do not claim to

  • Replace payroll records.
  • Determine employee exemption status.
  • Provide legal, tax or financial advice.
  • Guarantee a specific paycheck.

Corrections

If you spot a calculation, wording or source issue, use the contact page and include the calculator URL and non-sensitive example inputs.

Who WorkPay Tools is for

WorkPay Tools is designed for people who want a quick, understandable gross-pay or work-hours estimate without creating an account. The calculators are educational planning tools, not payroll systems or legal determinations.

Source hierarchy

When a calculator touches federal wage rules, the preferred references are primary U.S. government sources such as the Department of Labor or IRS. Pure arithmetic converters rely on transparent formulas shown directly on the page.

Update and review policy

Rule-sensitive pages show a visible review date. We revise formulas, assumptions, examples and source links when the underlying guidance or the calculator itself changes. A sitemap last-modified date is updated only when a page is actually revised.

Privacy by design

Most calculator inputs are processed in the browser. The goal is to let visitors test pay scenarios without submitting payroll information to an account or profile.

Calculator methodology

WorkPay Tools starts with transparent arithmetic. A calculator should make clear which values you enter, which formula is applied, what the result represents, and which real-world factors are outside the estimate. Gross-pay tools do not silently present their result as take-home pay, and schedule conversions do not treat biweekly and semimonthly pay as interchangeable.

Four checks before a calculator is treated as complete

  1. Math check: sample inputs are calculated independently and compared with the displayed result.
  2. Assumption check: the page states important assumptions such as weekly hours, pay periods per year, overtime multipliers or whether the result is gross pay.
  3. Source check: when a page discusses a federal wage rule, the supporting source should be a primary government source rather than an unsourced summary.
  4. Search-intent check: the calculator, explanation and examples must answer the same user question instead of adding unrelated payroll topics only for keywords.

How calculator pages are updated

Pages are reviewed when formulas, source links, user-facing assumptions, calculator behavior or major explanatory content changes. Minor design changes do not automatically change the review date. Search-performance data can suggest unclear sections, but it does not override mathematical accuracy or the scope of the tool.

What WorkPay Tools does not do

The site does not invent ratings, reviews or legal conclusions to qualify for search features. Estimates can differ from payroll records because employers may apply taxes, deductions, benefits, bonuses, local rules, different pay calendars or other inputs that a focused calculator does not collect.
