What Is a 401(k) Match? How Employer Matching Works

Learn what an employer 401(k) match is, what plan terms to verify, and why the plan document matters before you compare offers.

The short answer

A 401(k) match is an employer contribution connected to an employee’s 401(k) contributions. Match formulas vary: an employer may match a stated share of employee contributions up to a stated limit, or use a different formula. A match is not guaranteed across all employers or plans.

Read the matching formula and vesting terms

Ask whether you must contribute to receive the match, when contributions are made, whether there is a vesting schedule, and what happens after a job change. The employer’s plan document and summary plan description define those details.

Use the right record

Do not treat an advertised match percentage as an immediate cash increase. It can be subject to contribution limits, plan eligibility and vesting. Compare it alongside salary and other benefits using the Compensation Package Calculator as an estimate only.

Official reference

Investor.gov explains that some employers match a portion of employee 401(k) contributions and that traditional and Roth options can differ. Read the source.

Example: a 50% match up to 6% of eligible pay

Assume $50,000 in eligible annual pay and an employer match of 50% of employee contributions up to 6% of that pay. An employee contribution of $3,000 meets the illustrated 6% cap; the employer match is $1,500. Contributing $2,000 instead produces a $1,000 match under that same formula.

This simplified annual example ignores contribution timing, eligibility changes and plan-specific adjustments. Check how your plan defines eligible pay and calculates each match.

Source: IRS: matching contributions.

Frequently asked questions

Does a 50% match mean the employer contributes half my salary?

No. It normally describes the share of qualifying employee contributions matched, subject to the formula cap. Read both the matching percentage and eligible-pay limit.

Is my employer match always immediately vested?

Not necessarily. Employer contributions can have plan-specific vesting rules. Your own employee contributions are fully vested.

Will contributing above the matching cap increase the match?

Not under a formula that has already reached its matching cap. Additional employee contributions and employer matching are separate calculations.
