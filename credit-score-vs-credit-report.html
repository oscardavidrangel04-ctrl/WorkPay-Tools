Credit Score vs Credit Report: What Is the Difference?

Understand the difference between a credit score and a credit report, plus why you should check the underlying account information.

The short answer

A credit report is a record of information about credit accounts, payment history and certain other data maintained by a credit reporting company. A credit score is a number created from report information using a particular scoring model. The score and report are related, but they are not the same thing.

Credit report vs. credit score

Credit reportCredit score
What it isA file of information about your credit and identity.A numerical estimate generated by a scoring model.
What it can includeAccounts, balances, payment history, inquiries and identifying details.A result based on selected information in a report and a model’s rules.
Who creates itCredit reporting companies maintain bureau reports.A scoring company or lender’s model calculates a score.
What to do with itCheck the underlying entries for errors or unfamiliar accounts.Use it as a snapshot; it does not show which report entry caused a change.

Why can credit scores differ?

There is not just one score for every purpose. A score may differ because a lender uses another scoring model or version, a different credit bureau’s file, or information from a different date. The report is the underlying record; the score is one model’s calculation from available data. A score difference alone does not prove that a report is wrong.

How to check your report and address an error

  1. Get your reports from AnnualCreditReport.com, the federally authorized source.
  2. Compare names, accounts, balances, payment history and inquiry details across reports.
  3. If an entry is inaccurate, dispute the specific information with the credit reporting company and, when appropriate, the company that furnished it. Keep copies of supporting records.

Start with this practical guide to finding and disputing a credit report error. Checking a report is different from purchasing a score; a score is not required to identify a factual reporting error.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a credit report and a credit score?

A credit report is a record of credit and identity information maintained by a credit reporting company. A credit score is a number calculated from report data using a scoring model.

Why are my credit scores different?

Scores can differ because providers may use different scoring models or versions, different bureau reports, and information from different dates.

Where can I check my credit reports?

Use AnnualCreditReport.com, the federally authorized source for free reports, and review each bureau report for accuracy.

Can I dispute a credit score?

A score itself is calculated from data; if the underlying report contains inaccurate information, dispute that information with the reporting company and the company that supplied it.
