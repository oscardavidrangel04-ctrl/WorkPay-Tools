Credit Score vs Credit Report: What Is the Difference?

Understand the difference between a credit score and a credit report, plus why you should check the underlying account information.

The short answer

A credit report is a record of information about credit accounts, payment history and certain other data maintained by a credit reporting company. A credit score is a number created from report information using a particular scoring model. The score and report are related, but they are not the same thing.

What to check

Different lenders can use different reports, scoring models and dates. A score can change when report information changes, but a change does not automatically identify a mistake. Review account details, payment history and personal information if something looks unfamiliar.

Use the right information

Use official channels to obtain your report and dispute inaccurate information with the reporting company and the furnisher when appropriate. Avoid paying anyone who promises a guaranteed score increase or removal of accurate negative information.

Official reference

The CFPB explains that payment history and length of credit history may appear in reports and can be factors used in credit scoring. Read the source.

Frequently asked questions

What should I use for an account-specific answer?

Use the current account agreement, statement or official plan document. General terms do not override those records.
