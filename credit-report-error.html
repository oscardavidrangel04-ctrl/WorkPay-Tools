How to Handle a Credit Report Error

If a report entry looks inaccurate, preserve the report details and compare it with statements or other records.

The short answer

Document the bureau, report date, account details and records supporting the concern before using the applicable dispute process.

What to review

Identify the entry, retain supporting records, use the bureau or furnisher process and keep copies of dates and communications.

How to fix credit report errors

Document the specific error, collect supporting records and dispute it with each bureau displaying it. Also consider contacting the information furnisher. Keep copies of submissions and investigation results.

Related questions

How long does a credit report dispute take?

Timing depends on the dispute and applicable rules. Use the current CFPB and bureau instructions for the expected process.

Can accurate negative information be disputed away?

A dispute process is for information believed to be inaccurate or incomplete, not for removing accurate information solely because it is unfavorable.

Common report errors to check

Compare dates, balances and ownership. See the separate guides for incorrect late-payment marks, collection timelines and bankruptcy reporting.

Paid collection reporting errors

A paid collection that still shows the wrong balance is a specific issue to document. Review paid collection status and removal questions before submitting an evidence-based dispute.

Inaccurate information on a credit report

Identity details, account status, balances, dates and limits can all be inaccurate. Common credit reporting errors include a wrong name on a credit report, an account that belongs to a different person, an incorrect balance, or the same debt listed more than once. State the exact field that is wrong and support it with records instead of using a generic dispute reason.

Credit score errors versus credit report errors

A credit score is calculated from report information under a particular model. First correct inaccurate credit reporting; a correction may or may not change a score, and the result can vary by bureau and model. For a broader overview, see credit score versus credit report.

When a bureau reports inaccurate information

Dispute the inaccurate item with the reporting company that displays it and with the furnisher when appropriate. Keep copies of the report, your evidence and the outcome. Do not assume a similar company name or a small spelling variation proves an account is not yours.
