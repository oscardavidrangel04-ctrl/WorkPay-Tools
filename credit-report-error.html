How to Dispute a Credit Report Error

If a report entry looks inaccurate, preserve the report details and compare it with statements or other records.

The short answer

Dispute a credit report error with each reporting company showing the inaccurate or incomplete item. Send relevant evidence to the company that supplied the information when appropriate, and save a copy of every report, document and response.

What to review

Compare the exact entry with statements, payment confirmations, account letters or identity records. Note which bureau report contains it, the account or field involved, the reported value and why it is inaccurate or incomplete. A clear, item-specific dispute is easier to investigate than a general request to “fix” the report.

Official reference

Start with the CFPB dispute instructions and its list of common report errors.

How to fix credit report errors

Dispute information you believe is inaccurate or incomplete with the credit bureau that displays it. You may also send the same evidence to the company that furnished the information. Be specific: identify the account or personal-information field, the value shown, why it is wrong, and the document that supports your position.

  1. Get and save the report from each nationwide bureau; an error may appear in one report but not another.
  2. Mark each inaccurate or incomplete item and gather records that directly support the correction.
  3. Dispute with every credit reporting company showing the item. The CFPB dispute guide links to the current Equifax, Experian and TransUnion channels.
  4. When appropriate, send the same focused dispute and evidence to the company that supplied the information (the furnisher).
  5. Keep copies, submission dates and delivery or confirmation details; read the investigation result and check the report again.

Use the reporting company’s current instructions for its online or mail process. Include copies rather than irreplaceable originals, and keep proof of what you sent and when.

What documents should I include?

Include a copy of the report section with the item identified, a concise explanation of what is wrong and what correction you request, plus relevant proof such as a statement, payment receipt, account-closure letter, identity-theft report or correspondence. Send only documents that support the specific issue, and retain the originals.

See how to read a credit report before disputing an unfamiliar field. A negative item that is accurate is not automatically removable through a dispute.

How long does an investigation take?

The CFPB says a credit reporting company generally has 30 days to investigate a dispute. Some cases can take up to 45 days, including certain disputes made after obtaining a free annual report or when relevant information is submitted during the investigation. The company generally must notify you of results within five business days after completing its investigation. Check the current CFPB timing guidance for details.

If the problem is not resolved

Read the investigation result, compare the updated report with your evidence, and follow the reporting company’s instructions if you want to add a brief statement to your file. If you still have a problem with credit reporting, you can submit a complaint to the CFPB. Keep the case number and correspondence.

Related questions

How long does a credit report dispute take?

A credit reporting company generally has 30 days to investigate, though certain cases can take up to 45 days. See the current CFPB guidance for exceptions.

Can accurate negative information be disputed away?

A dispute process is for information believed to be inaccurate or incomplete, not for removing accurate information solely because it is unfavorable.

Should I dispute with the bureau or the company that reported the information?

Start with each credit reporting company showing the item. You can also dispute with the furnisher—the company that supplied it—when appropriate. Keep a copy of each submission and the supporting records.

What should I include in a credit report dispute?

Identify the exact account or field, explain what is inaccurate or incomplete, state what correction you are requesting, and include copies of supporting records.

Paid collection reporting errors

A paid collection that still shows the wrong balance is a specific issue to document. Review paid collection status and removal questions before submitting an evidence-based dispute.

Write a clear dispute explanation

Keep the explanation focused on one item at a time: identify the account or field, say what is inaccurate or incomplete, explain the correction you believe is needed, and name the evidence attached. Avoid disputing an entire report with a generic claim when you can describe the precise issue.

Credit score errors versus credit report errors

A credit score is calculated from report information under a particular model. First correct inaccurate credit reporting; a correction may or may not change a score, and the result can vary by bureau and model. For a broader overview, see credit score versus credit report.

When a bureau reports inaccurate information

Dispute the inaccurate item with the reporting company that displays it and with the furnisher when appropriate. Keep copies of the report, your evidence and the outcome. Do not assume a similar company name or a small spelling variation proves an account is not yours.
