Balance Transfer vs Cash Advance

A balance transfer moves existing debt to another account. A cash advance borrows cash or a cash-equivalent against a credit line. They are not interchangeable.

What each transaction does

A balance transfer is commonly used to move an existing card balance, sometimes under a promotional rate. A cash advance provides cash or funds classified as an advance by the issuer. Each has its own available credit and transaction rules.

Compare the costs

A transfer can carry a transfer fee even at 0% promotional APR. A cash advance can carry an advance fee, possible ATM fee and interest beginning immediately. Read the applicable rate and fee tables; the lower advertised APR does not describe every cost.

Avoid confusing the two

Some transactions that look like ordinary spending may be classified as cash equivalents under an agreement. Ask the issuer how it will code a transaction before relying on purchase grace-period treatment. Continue paying an old account until a transfer is confirmed.

Check the actual account terms

Credit card rates, fees, posting rules and promotional terms can vary by issuer and transaction. Read your current statement and agreement for the account-specific answer. Primary reference: consumer guidance from the CFPB or card issuer.

Frequently asked questions

Can a balance transfer have a fee at 0% APR?

Yes. A promotional rate does not eliminate a separate transfer fee.

Do cash advances usually have a grace period?

They commonly begin accruing interest immediately rather than receiving the purchase grace period.

Does a balance transfer give me cash?

Usually it pays an existing debt, but some offers permit a deposit into checking or a transfer check. The specific offer determines whether it is treated as a balance transfer or a cash advance.

Can you pay one credit card with another?

A balance transfer can move an eligible debt to another card, but it is a separate borrowing transaction, not an ordinary purchase payment. Taking a cash advance to pay a bill creates a new advance balance and its own costs. Neither approach cancels the debt.

Example: include the transfer fee in the new balance

With a hypothetical $4,000 transfer and a 3% fee, the fee is $120. If it is added to the receiving card, that card starts with $4,120 owed, assuming no other activity. A 0% introductory APR does not erase the fee. Confirm whether the offer has enough room for both the transfer and fee, and check its repayment deadline.

Does a deposit into checking always mean a cash advance?

No. Some balance-transfer offers permit a direct deposit or transfer check. The offer determines the transaction category, not just where the money arrives. Citi explicitly describes checking-account deposits for some balance transfers. Do not assume that a different bank transfer, card check or ATM withdrawal has those terms.

Keep paying the original card until the payment is confirmed. Check transfer timing and completion before relying on a request that is still pending.
