Credit Card Payment Allocation: How Extra Payments Apply

For covered U.S. credit card accounts, the portion of a payment above the required minimum generally goes first to the balance with the highest APR. The minimum-payment portion can be allocated differently.

How payment allocation works

A card can have separate balances with different interest rates—for example, purchases, a cash advance and a promotional balance. When you pay more than the required minimum, Regulation Z generally requires the issuer to apply the excess first to the balance with the highest annual percentage rate (APR), then to other balances in descending APR order.

This rule concerns the amount above the minimum due. It does not mean your entire payment is necessarily directed to the highest-rate balance: the issuer generally has discretion over how the minimum-payment portion is allocated, subject to other applicable requirements.

Simple example

Suppose the statement has a $40 minimum due, a $500 purchase balance at 24% APR and a $300 transfer balance at 0% APR. If you pay $140, the first $40 satisfies the minimum under your issuer’s allocation method. The $100 above the minimum generally goes to the 24% balance first. The exact account calculation can depend on the rates and balances the issuer uses under the rule and the agreement.

What to check on your statement

  1. Find the minimum due and payment credited for the cycle.
  2. Compare the APRs and balances for purchases, transfers, cash advances and promotional offers.
  3. Check the statement’s payment-allocation disclosure and your card agreement.
  4. Contact the issuer if the amounts do not make sense or a payment appears missing.

Payment allocation is different from deciding whether to pay the minimum or the statement balance. For the minimum itself, see how a credit card minimum payment is determined.

Promotional and deferred-interest balances

Promotional APR and deferred-interest offers can have special allocation rules, especially near the end of a deferred-interest period. Do not assume an extra payment will be assigned to the promotional balance in the way you prefer. Review the offer terms, statement and current regulation; ask the issuer how a payment will be applied before the promotion expires.

Official reference

The CFPB’s current Regulation Z, 12 CFR § 1026.53 describes allocation of payments, including the general rule for amounts paid above the required minimum and special treatment for certain promotional balances. Account terms and the exact balance categories still matter.

Credit card payment allocation FAQ

How is a credit card payment above the minimum allocated?

For covered U.S. credit card accounts, the amount above the required minimum generally goes first to the balance with the highest APR, then to other balances in descending APR order.

Does the highest-APR rule apply to the minimum payment too?

The rule addresses the amount above the required minimum. The issuer generally determines how the required minimum is allocated, subject to other applicable requirements.

Can I choose how my extra payment is allocated?

Check the card agreement and issuer payment options. Special rules can apply to deferred-interest balances near the end of a promotion.

Where can I see how my payment was applied?

Review the statement’s balance categories, APRs, payment credits and agreement. Contact the issuer if the allocation is unclear.
