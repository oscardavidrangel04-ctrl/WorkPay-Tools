How Long Do Closed Accounts Stay on a Credit Report?

A closed account does not vanish immediately. Positive closed accounts can remain for years, while negative information generally has a seven-year reporting limit.

Closed accounts in good standing

Positive payment history may remain after an account is paid and closed. Credit-bureau practice commonly retains closed accounts in good standing for about 10 years, but the precise removal date can vary.

Closed accounts with negative history

Closing an account does not reset or erase earlier late payments. Most negative account information is generally reportable for up to seven years under federal rules.

Why a closed account still matters

A scoring model can consider information in a closed account while it remains on the report. Separately, closing a revolving card can change available credit and utilization now.

Reviewing an inaccurate closed status

Check ownership, balance, payment history, closed date and whether the account should be open or closed. Dispute the particular field you believe is wrong.

Official reference

Check current guidance from Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This page is educational and cannot determine an individual credit score or legal outcome.

Frequently asked questions

When do closed accounts fall off a credit report?

Positive closed accounts are often retained for about 10 years; negative information generally has a seven-year reporting limit.

Does closing an account delete its history?

No. The account can remain on a report after closure.

Does a closed account hurt credit?

It depends on account history and the rest of the credit profile; closing a card can also change utilization.
