Closed account reporting

How Long Do Closed Accounts Stay on a Credit Report?

A closed account does not vanish immediately. Positive closed accounts can remain for years, while negative information generally has a seven-year reporting limit.

Closed accounts in good standing

Positive payment history may remain after an account is paid and closed. Credit-bureau practice commonly retains closed accounts in good standing for about 10 years, but the precise removal date can vary.

Closed accounts with negative history

Closing an account does not reset or erase earlier late payments. Most negative account information is generally reportable for up to seven years under federal rules.

Why a closed account still matters

A scoring model can consider information in a closed account while it remains on the report. Separately, closing a revolving card can change available credit and utilization now.

What does a closed account mean on a credit report?

Closed usually means the account is no longer open for new use. It does not by itself mean the balance is zero, that the debt was forgiven or that all payment history must disappear. Read the balance and payment-status fields separately.

A paid loan can remain as positive history after closure. A closed card with an unpaid balance may still require payments. An account can also have both older positive history and a specific late payment with its own reporting timeline.

Check the relevant date

For an account closed in good standing, the closure date matters to the bureau's retention practice. Negative items follow their applicable reporting rules; do not assume that closing an account restarts every negative item's clock. Experian explains closed-account retention. If a field is wrong, identify that field in a credit-report dispute.

Read the dates as separate fields

  • Date opened: when the original account began, not when it was closed.
  • Date closed: when new use ended; it does not establish that the balance was paid.
  • Last updated: when information was refreshed; a recent update does not make an old account new.
  • Delinquency or estimated removal date: check the entry associated with the negative information instead of using the latest update date.

For example, a loan opened in 2018, paid and closed in 2024, and updated in 2026 still has three different dates. The 2026 update alone does not restart its history. If the report wrongly shows a remaining balance, compare the payoff confirmation with that balance field, not just the word “closed.”

Record the exact field and supporting document before using the credit-report dispute process. For an account sent to a collector, review the separate collection reporting timeline.

Closed accounts with balances, transfers or errors

Should I pay off a closed account on my credit report?

A closed status alone does not cancel a valid debt. Check the current balance, account owner and payment instructions. If an old creditor's entry says transferred, confirm who now handles the account before sending money. Do not treat closure as proof of forgiveness or pay two companies for the same obligation without reconciling the records.

What does closed due to transfer mean?

A transfer can leave historical information under the old creditor and an entry under the new provider. Compare names, account references, balances and dates with the transfer notice. The old entry being closed does not establish that the entire obligation has been repaid. Experian explains the closed-account label.

How to dispute an inaccurate closed account

Describe the specific error: for example, a paid account showing an unpaid balance, an incorrect ownership field or a wrong closure date. Include the record supporting that correction. An accurate closed account is not removable merely because you would prefer a shorter report. Use the credit-report dispute checklist and keep payment obligations separate from the reporting investigation.

Frequently asked questions

When do closed accounts fall off a credit report?

Positive closed accounts are often retained for about 10 years; negative information generally has a seven-year reporting limit.

Does closing an account delete its history?

No. The account can remain on a report after closure.

Does a closed account hurt credit?

It depends on account history and the rest of the credit profile; closing a card can also change utilization.

Does a closed account with a balance mean the debt was forgiven?

No. Closure and balance are separate fields. Verify the current obligation, creditor and payment instructions.

Should I dispute every closed account?

No. Dispute information you believe is inaccurate or incomplete, identifying the specific field and evidence rather than requesting blanket removal.
