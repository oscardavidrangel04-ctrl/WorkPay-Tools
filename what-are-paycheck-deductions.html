What Are Paycheck Deductions?

Learn common paycheck deduction categories, how to compare a stub, and when to ask payroll for a written explanation.

The short answer

Paycheck deductions are amounts removed from gross pay before the employee receives net pay. They can include tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare taxes, insurance premiums, retirement contributions, wage garnishments and other authorized items. The name and tax treatment of a deduction can vary by plan and payroll system.

Compare current amounts with earlier elections

Read current-period and year-to-date columns separately. A deduction can change after an enrollment election, a pay-rate change, a one-time adjustment or a benefit-plan change. Confirm whether an amount is pre-tax or after-tax before comparing it to a prior period.

For benefit-account labels, consult HSA vs FSA and then verify the deduction against your enrollment records.

Common paycheck deduction categories

CategoryWhat it can includeWhere to verify it
TaxesFederal, state or local withholding; Social Security and MedicarePay stub and tax records
BenefitsHealth coverage, dental, vision or other elected benefitsEnrollment confirmation or plan materials
RetirementEmployee plan contributionsElection and plan records
Other itemsGarnishments, union dues or authorized repaymentsApplicable notice or payroll explanation

A label alone does not establish the rule that applies. Check the plan, election or payroll record for the exact amount and timing.

Pre-tax vs. after-tax deductions

Some benefits and contributions can be taken before certain taxes, while others are taken after tax. The result depends on the benefit type and plan rules. Do not assume every deduction lowers the same tax wages. For the federal withholding side of a paycheck, read what tax withholding means; for Social Security and Medicare, see what FICA tax is.

How to check an unfamiliar deduction

  1. Compare the current amount with the prior pay stub and the year-to-date column.
  2. Match it to a benefit election, tax line, payroll notice or other document.
  3. Keep the pay stub and ask payroll for a written explanation if the label or amount remains unclear.

This guide does not determine whether a deduction is lawful. Employee rights and payroll rules can vary by location and circumstance.

Official reference

The IRS describes gross income as pay before taxes and deductions and explains that employee pay can be subject to multiple withholding categories. Read the source.

Reconcile a deduction before disputing it

Write down the label, current amount, year-to-date amount and the first paycheck where the change appeared. Match the entry to an enrollment confirmation, tax election or payroll notice. Ask payroll whether it is recurring, a one-time adjustment or a correction.

An employer-paid benefit shown for information is not necessarily deducted from your wages. Use the actual employee-deduction column when reconciling net pay.

Source: IRS Publication 4268: paycheck records.

Frequently asked questions

Are all paycheck deductions taxes?

No. A paycheck can also include employee benefit premiums, retirement contributions and other deductions. Read the individual labels and supporting records.

Should I subtract the year-to-date deduction from this paycheck?

No. Use the current-period deduction to reconcile this check. Year-to-date totals summarize a longer period.

Does every pre-tax deduction reduce every payroll tax?

No. Tax treatment depends on the deduction and the tax involved. Do not assume federal income-tax wages, Social Security wages and Medicare wages change identically.
