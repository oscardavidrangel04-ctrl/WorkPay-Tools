How to Calculate PTO Accrual

Learn the simple PTO-accrual formula and which policy details to verify.

The basic method

PTO accrual may be stated as hours earned per pay period, eligible hour worked, or service period. Start with the written policy to identify the calculation method, eligibility, caps and carryover. This planning calculation does not determine legal leave rights.

Reconcile an accrual and a leave balance

Under an illustrative policy that earns 0.05 PTO hours per eligible hour worked, 80 eligible hours earn 4 PTO hours: 80 × 0.05 = 4. If the opening balance is 20 hours and 8 hours are used, the closing balance is 20 + 4 − 8 = 16 hours, before other adjustments.

Accrued hours are not the total balance

The four new hours are an addition, not a replacement for previously saved time. A policy that grants a fixed amount per pay period uses a different calculation; do not multiply that fixed grant by hours worked.

  • Confirm whether paid leave counts as eligible time.
  • Check caps, waiting periods and carryover rules.
  • Use the same posting period for earned and used hours.
  • Ask payroll about corrections before changing a recorded balance.

Calculate PTO earned per pay period

If a policy spreads a 120-hour annual amount evenly across 24 eligible pay periods, the rate is 120 ÷ 24 = 5 hours per period. Across 26 eligible periods, the rate is about 4.6154 hours per period. These examples assume a full year of eligibility with no pauses or cap restrictions.

Check biweekly vs semimonthly pay before choosing the number of periods. Use the employer's actual accrual schedule; a payroll calendar alone does not establish the PTO rule.

Why rounding can change the total

Displaying 4.6154 as 4.62 hours is convenient, but posting exactly 4.62 hours in all 26 periods produces 120.12 hours. A payroll system may retain more precision or make an adjustment under its policy. Compare posted accruals with the policy instead of assuming a small difference is extra leave owed.

A per-period rate is not an hourly rate. Use the PTO accrual calculator for a policy that awards PTO per eligible hour worked, and keep both the accrual unit and input unit in hours.

Before applying an accrual formula, compare accrued PTO and unlimited PTO to identify which type of policy you are reading.

Question to check

Which record controls?

Compare the current written PTO policy with the payroll record. Ask payroll to explain any mismatch. A generic accrual formula cannot establish your leave entitlement or resolve a disputed balance.
