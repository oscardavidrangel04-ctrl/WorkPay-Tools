Can Collection Agencies Report to Credit Bureaus?

Understand when a collection account can appear on a credit report and how to check ownership, balance and dates for errors.

The short answer

A collection agency can furnish information about a collection account to credit reporting companies. The key question for a consumer is whether the particular entry is accurate and current.

What to compare on each report

Inspect the agency name, original creditor, balance, status and dates. A creditor account and a collection account can both appear, but their balances and status should not misstate what is owed.

If the entry is inaccurate

Identify the exact error and gather statements, payment records or correspondence. Dispute the information with the reporting company and the furnisher; do not assume that an unfamiliar agency name alone proves an error.

Reporting time and payment are separate

A payment can change the reported balance without automatically deleting accurate history. Read the collection timeline guide for the date that generally governs how long negative information can be reported.

Frequently asked questions

Does this page guarantee a deletion or score increase?

No. The outcome depends on the facts, the reporting and applicable rules.

Should I keep copies of my records?

Yes. Keep notices, correspondence, payment evidence and responses related to the account.
