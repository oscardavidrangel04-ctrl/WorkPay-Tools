How to Find Which Collection Agency Has Your Debt

Use creditor records, validation information and credit reports to identify a collector before sharing information or paying.

The short answer

Start with the collection notice, check your credit reports, and contact the original creditor using details from your own records. Match the account and current collection company across those sources. A report can help identify an agency, but may not show a recent transfer or every debt.

Read the notice and build an account-by-account list

  1. Record the collector’s business name and address.
  2. Identify the creditor currently owed and any original-creditor details provided.
  3. Compare the account identifier, balance and itemization date with your records.
  4. Write down the notice’s dispute deadline before investigating further.

Keep a separate row for each claimed account. If two companies contact you about one account, ask which company currently handles it and how the earlier company’s role ended. A change of collector is not a reason to count one debt twice.

Compare independent records

Check statements from the original creditor and your credit reports, but remember a report may lag a transfer and may not name every collector. Contact the original creditor using a number from your own records, not only the incoming call.

What if the debt is missing from your credit report?

A credit report is one source of information, not a complete list of every amount a company may claim you owe. If no collection entry appears, compare the notice with your original account records and contact the original creditor using contact details you obtained independently.

Ask which company currently handles the account and whether it was assigned or sold. If the name differs from the caller's, request an explanation and written account information before sending money. A familiar balance or creditor name alone does not verify the caller.

The CFPB recommends verifying the debt and collector before sharing sensitive information. Keep the notice and any response so you can follow the applicable dispute process. See what to check when a debt is not yours.

Verify before paying

Confirm the company and payment destination independently. Request written terms and save all messages and receipts. If the debt is not yours or the amount is wrong, dispute the specific issue.

Once you have identified the companies involved, review paying the collection agency vs the original creditor before choosing a payment destination.

Frequently asked questions

Can I find every collection debt on my credit report?

No. A report can help, but it may omit debts or lag a transfer. Compare it with notices and records from the original creditor.

Why does the collector have a different name from my lender?

The creditor may use another company to collect, or the debt may have been sold. Verify the account and the company’s role instead of relying on the name alone.

What if two agencies claim the same account?

Compare the creditor, account identifier, amounts and dates. Ask for written clarification of who currently handles the debt before choosing a payment destination.
