How to Calculate a Salary Increase

Calculate a salary increase using percentage and dollar formulas, with worked examples for annual, monthly, biweekly and hourly pay.

Reviewed October 22, 2026

Salary increase formulas

Raise amount = current salary × (raise percentage ÷ 100)

New salary = current salary + raise amount

Increase percentage = ((new salary − old salary) ÷ old salary) × 100

Worked example: 5% raise on $60,000

  1. Convert 5% to 0.05.
  2. Multiply $60,000 × 0.05 = $3,000.
  3. Add $3,000 to $60,000 for a new salary of $63,000.

Calculate your own raise

Calculate the raise percentage from two salary amounts

If salary rises from $48,000 to $51,600, the difference is $3,600. Divide the increase by the old salary: $3,600 ÷ $48,000 × 100 = 7.5%. Dividing by the new salary answers a different question and gives the wrong raise percentage.

What if the raise starts halfway through the year?

The new annual rate is not necessarily this year's total earnings. In a simplified six-month/six-month example, $24,000 at the old rate plus $25,800 at the new rate totals $49,800. That is $1,800 more than a full year at $48,000. Actual payroll depends on effective dates and proration.

Use the salary increase calculator for the rate comparison, then check the effective date on your payroll notice.

How to calculate an hourly pay increase

Use the same percentage formula with hourly rates. If pay rises from $24 to $25.20 per hour, the increase is $1.20. Divide $1.20 by the old $24 rate: $1.20 ÷ $24 × 100 = 5%.

At an unchanged 40 paid hours per week, that rate change adds $48 in weekly gross regular pay. If hours change, compare rates and hours separately. A smaller paycheck after a rate increase can reflect fewer paid hours; the paycheck total alone does not tell you the raise percentage.

Check the denominator and the pay period

  • Use the old rate as the denominator, not the new rate.
  • Compare annual with annual, hourly with hourly, or matching payroll periods.
  • Use the actual number of paychecks when converting an annual raise. Biweekly and semimonthly are different schedules.

For that last step, compare biweekly vs semimonthly pay before dividing the raise into paycheck amounts.

Gross raise versus take-home raise

A salary increase calculator normally reports gross pay. Your net paycheck change can be smaller because withholding, benefit contributions and other deductions may also change.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a 10% raise?

Multiply the current salary by 0.10 and add that result to the current salary.

How can I calculate the raise when I know both salaries?

Subtract the old salary from the new salary, divide by the old salary and multiply by 100.
