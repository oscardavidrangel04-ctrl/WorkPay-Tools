Gross Pay vs Reported W-2 Wages

Gross pay is total earnings before deductions, while W-2 wage boxes use specific tax definitions. Because Box 1, Box 3 and Box 5 follow different rules, none is automatically identical to year-end gross pay.

Gross pay vs W-2 wages at a glance

AmountGeneral meaning
Gross payTotal earnings before deductions
W-2 Box 1Federal income-tax wages
W-2 Box 3Social Security wages, subject to applicable rules and wage base
W-2 Box 5Medicare wages and tips

Why gross pay may not match Box 1

Eligible pre-tax deductions can reduce federal taxable wages without reducing gross earnings. Traditional 401(k) elective deferrals are a common example. Other benefits and payroll adjustments may also affect reported wages.

Why W-2 boxes differ from each other

Each box uses a different wage definition. Compare Box 1 vs Box 3 and Box 1 vs Box 5 for focused explanations.

What to check

  • Year-to-date gross earnings on the final pay statement
  • Pre-tax deductions and benefit elections
  • Retirement-plan amounts and Box 12 codes
  • Employer payroll explanations
  • The applicable IRS W-2 instructions

If amounts appear incorrect, contact the employer or payroll department rather than changing tax documents based on a general example.

Gross Pay vs W-2 Wages FAQ

Why does gross pay not match W-2 Box 1?

Box 1 uses federal taxable-wage rules, so eligible pre-tax deductions can create a difference.

Which W-2 box is gross pay?

No single box is a universal gross-pay total.

Can Box 5 be higher than Box 1?

Yes. Some amounts excluded from federal wages remain included in Medicare wages.
