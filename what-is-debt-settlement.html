What Is Debt Settlement?

Debt settlement is an effort to resolve a debt for less than the amount claimed as owed, often through a negotiated arrangement.

Risks to review before a settlement

Review fees, creditor participation, credit-report effects, potential collection activity and any tax-related notice with qualified help when needed.

For the reporting dimension, read debt settlement and its credit impact separately from the proposed payment amount.

Verify the proposed agreement and all costs

Compare the current statement, agreement or offer before acting. Focus on the amount owed, APR, fees, required payment, due date and any promotional or service terms that apply to your specific accounts.

A proposed settlement is not a completed agreement

If an account shows $4,000 owed and someone proposes a $2,500 settlement, the $1,500 difference is only a proposed reduction until the creditor or authorized collector agrees. Keep any service fee separate when comparing the amount you would actually pay.

Get the terms for the specific account

  1. Confirm who owns the debt and who can accept the agreement.
  2. Request the accepted amount, due dates and treatment of the remaining balance in writing.
  3. Check whether missing an installment changes the agreement.
  4. Keep payment records and written completion confirmation.

Settlement programs can involve missed payments, additional charges and continued collection activity. A creditor does not have to accept a proposal. Review the CFPB's debt relief warnings before paying for a service. A settled account is not the same as an inaccurate account that should be disputed.

What Is Debt Settlement? Process, Costs and Risks FAQ

Does settlement guarantee that the account will be deleted?

No. Paying an agreed reduced amount and removing credit-report information are separate issues. Review reporting terms without assuming deletion or a particular score increase.

Is a debt management plan the same as settlement?

No. A management plan generally organizes repayment through a credit counselor; settlement seeks acceptance of less than the claimed balance.
