Using the snowball amount

Keep required minimum payments current, then direct the amount shown to the selected smallest balance. When that debt is repaid, roll its payment into the next target.

How to use a debt snowball payment budget

List balances from smallest to largest. Enter the monthly amount available for all debts, then the required minimums on every debt except the current target. A $900 budget minus $550 for other debts leaves $350 for the target. That $350 is its total planned payment, not an extra payment on top of its own minimum.

Budget shortfalls and payoff estimates

Check that the target payment covers its own required minimum. If other minimums consume the budget, zero available does not mean the bills are covered. This calculator does not track multiple accounts, interest or payoff dates. Use credit card payoff for a single-account estimate. The snowball prioritizes the smallest balance; choosing the highest APR instead is a different strategy.

Calculator FAQ

Does the target payment include its minimum?

Yes. The output is the total amount remaining for the target after other debts’ minimums.

What happens when a debt is paid off?

Recalculate using the next target and the remaining debts. The freed payment can stay in your monthly budget.

Does snowball always minimize interest?

No. Paying the highest-rate debt first can reduce interest compared with smallest-balance ordering.

Why is my result zero?

The minimums on other debts equal or exceed your entered budget. Review the shortfall rather than treating zero as a repayment plan.
