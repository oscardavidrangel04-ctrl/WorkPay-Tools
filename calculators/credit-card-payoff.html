How this payoff estimate works

If you are comparing a balance transfer, calculate its upfront transfer fee and allow for the time a transfer may take. Neither is included in this fixed-payment model.

This model uses one purchase APR. If your balance includes advances, compare purchase APR vs cash advance APR because one rate may not describe the entire balance.

Each modeled month adds APR ÷ 12 interest and subtracts the entered payment. Actual cards often use daily or average-daily-balance methods, so the statement result can differ.

What to enter in a credit card debt payoff calculator

Use the current balance, the purchase APR that applies to that balance, and the monthly amount you can consistently pay. Do not use the credit limit as the balance, and do not combine a cash-advance APR with a purchase APR as though they were the same rate.

For multiple cards, list each balance and rate first. A general debt payoff plan can help organize the accounts before deciding whether a snowball or avalanche order fits the situation.

Example: paying off $5,000 at 24% APR

Keep the balance and APR the same when comparing payments. In this calculator's monthly model, a $5,000 starting balance at 24% APR produces the estimates below. Each payment is fixed, interest is added before the monthly payment, and there are no new charges, fees or rate changes. The last payment may be smaller.

Adding $50 to the $200 payment saves 10 modeled months and about $551.20 in interest. To reproduce that comparison, enter $200 as the monthly payment and $50 as the extra payment. These are calculated scenarios, not an issuer payoff quote.

Why balance divided by payment is not enough

Dividing $5,000 by $250 gives 20 months, but ignores interest. The first modeled month adds $100 of interest, so only $150 of that payment reduces principal. The model needs 26 payments instead of 20. At 0% throughout the model, $250 would repay $5,000 in 20 payments; a temporary promotional APR cannot be treated as permanent.

If you are paying only the required amount, review minimum payment vs statement balance. This tool keeps your planned payment fixed; the minimum payment calculator estimates a different amount and does not produce the same repayment schedule.

The CFPB explains why paying more than the minimum can shorten repayment in its credit card guidance.

Credit Card Payoff FAQ

Does this predict my exact payoff date?

No. It is a fixed-payment planning model without fees, new charges, rate changes or multiple APR categories.

What if payment is lower than monthly interest?

The balance will not decline in this model, so increase the payment or review the account terms.

Does this include deferred-interest offers?

No. Deferred-interest promotions require separate treatment under the card agreement.

Can I use this for several credit cards?

Use it for one balance-and-rate scenario at a time. For several cards, first list the balances, APRs and required payments, then choose a repayment order.

Fixed monthly payment versus a declining minimum

The payment you enter remains fixed for every modeled month. A card's minimum payment may decline as the balance falls, so paying only the changing minimum can take longer than this fixed-payment estimate. The final modeled payment can be smaller than the regular amount.

Check promotional offers before using one APR

A zero-percent offer lasting six months is not a zero-percent rate for the entire repayment plan. This calculator does not switch rates when a promotion ends. Deferred-interest offers also require separate treatment.

What happens with a payment barely above interest?

Repayment can take a very long time. The model stops after 1,200 months rather than presenting an unfinished simulation as a successful payoff.
