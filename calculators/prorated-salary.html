Prorated Salary Calculator

Estimate gross salary for part of a pay period using a transparent workday method. Use the schedule and payroll frequency that apply to the period you are modeling.

PRORATED GROSS PAY—

Planning estimate only. Employer policy, applicable law and payroll rounding may use another method.

Prorated salary and pro rata pay formula

Full period salary = annual salary ÷ pay periods per year

Prorated salary = full period salary × (payable workdays ÷ scheduled workdays)

A $60,000 salary across 26 pay periods equals $2,307.69 per full period. Six payable days out of ten produces an estimate of $1,384.62.

When to calculate prorated salary

A partial-period calculation may be relevant when an employee starts or leaves during a pay period or when another unpaid partial-period event applies. This calculator does not decide whether a deduction is permitted; it only performs the selected arithmetic.

Choose the correct day count

Use scheduled working days if payroll prorates by workday. Do not mix calendar days with workdays. Holidays, paid leave and employer-specific rules may change the payable-day count.

Using separate calculations when the salary rate changes

This calculator uses one annual salary rate at a time. If your payroll method requires splitting a period between two rates, model each portion separately with the same full-period day count, then add the estimates.

For illustration, suppose a monthly period has 20 scheduled workdays. Ten payable days use a $48,000 annual rate and ten use a $52,800 rate. With 12 pay periods, the two estimates are ($48,000 ÷ 12) × 10 ÷ 20 = $2,000 and ($52,800 ÷ 12) × 10 ÷ 20 = $2,200. Their total is $4,200 gross under this workday method.

Do not overlap the payable days

Count each payable day once. Confirm the effective date, payroll convention and permitted treatment with payroll before using the estimate to check an actual payment. The tool does not infer employment rules or calculate taxes. Review prorated salary definitions if the employer uses a different convention.

Prorated salary FAQ

How do you calculate prorated salary?

Divide annual salary by annual pay periods, then multiply the full-period amount by the payable share of scheduled workdays.

Can I use this as a prorated semimonthly salary calculator?

Yes. Use 24 pay periods and enter the scheduled and payable workdays for the specific semimonthly period.

What is the difference between pro rata pay and full-period pay?

Pro rata pay covers only the eligible fraction of the period; full-period pay covers the complete scheduled period.

Does this include taxes?

No. The result is estimated gross pay.

Can I calculate a paycheck with two salary rates?

The tool accepts one rate per calculation. You can model the two non-overlapping portions separately under the same applicable workday convention, then add the gross estimates.

Prorating a semimonthly paycheck with different workday counts

At $60,000 a year and 24 pay periods, full-period gross salary is $2,500. If the current period has 11 scheduled workdays and 5 payable workdays, the estimate is $2,500 × 5 ÷ 11 = $1,136.36. Enter the actual scheduled workdays for that period; do not assume every half-month has ten.

Compare the method, not just the result

This tool uses a share of scheduled workdays. An employer using calendar days or an annual daily-rate convention can produce a different amount. Confirm the applicable method before comparing your payroll statement.
