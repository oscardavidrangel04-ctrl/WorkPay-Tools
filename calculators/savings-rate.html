Keep the time period consistent

Compare monthly savings with monthly income, or annual savings with annual income. Mixing periods can make a savings-rate result misleading.

Question to check

Should debt payments count as savings?

That is a budgeting choice. Keep your definition consistent when comparing months.

Savings rate formula and example

Savings rate = savings ÷ income × 100. Saving $600 from $5,000 of gross monthly income gives a 12% gross-income savings rate. Enter a positive income amount; zero income does not produce a defined ratio.

Gross versus take-home income

If take-home income is $4,000 instead, the same $600 equals 15%. Both ratios can be useful, but label the denominator and do not compare them as if they used the same definition. The input in this tool is labeled gross monthly income.

Define what you count as savings

If you include employee retirement contributions in savings, count them once and use a consistent income definition. Keep employer contributions separate unless you also define how they affect the denominator. Transfers between your own savings accounts are not new savings.
