Time card: quick answers for hours, breaks and overtime

How do I calculate hours from clock-in and clock-out?
Subtract the start time from the end time, then subtract unpaid breaks. Add each shift to get the total hours.
How do I calculate a lunch break on a time card?
Subtract only the break time that is unpaid. Whether a break must be paid depends on the rules that apply to the job.
Can this calculate overnight shifts?
Yes. Enter a clock-out time that falls after midnight and the calculator treats it as the next day.
Does a time card prove overtime eligibility?
No. It totals the hours you enter. Overtime eligibility can depend on federal, state and job-specific rules.

Time Card Formula, Breaks and Decimal Hours

For each day: paid hours = elapsed clock time − unpaid break time. Weekly total hours worked are the sum of all completed daily rows. For a pay estimate, net hours × hourly rate = estimated gross pay before taxes and deductions.

Worked example

Example: 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM is 8.5 elapsed hours. Deduct a 30-minute unpaid break to get 8.0 net hours. At a regular hourly rate of $20, net hours × hourly rate gives an estimated $160 in gross pay.

  • Calculate paid shift length from clock-in and clock-out times.

What this calculator assumes

  • Clock-out can occur the following day for an overnight shift.
  • The calculator subtracts only the break minutes you enter.
  • The calculator applies the same hourly rate to all net hours.
  • Weekly overtime is estimated using the threshold and multiplier you enter; legal entitlement can depend on additional rules.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Subtracting a break that should not be deducted for your situation.
  • Using one shift to infer weekly overtime entitlement.
  • Forgetting that an overnight clock-out occurs on the next day.

What is a time card?

A time card or timecard is a record of when work starts and ends. A time-card calculator turns those clock-in and clock-out entries into elapsed time, subtracts the unpaid breaks you enter, and totals the resulting paid hours.

Lunch breaks and clocking out

Enter lunch break or other unpaid break time only when you want those minutes deducted from the hours you are modeling. The calculator uses your start and end times, subtracts the break you enter, and shows the remaining paid time in hours and minutes plus decimal hours.

What is 3.75 hours?

3.75 decimal hours equals 3 hours 45 minutes. Decimal time is useful when payroll systems store a fraction of an hour instead of hours and minutes.

Time Card Calculator FAQ

Can this time card calculator handle overnight shifts?

Yes. If clock-out is earlier than clock-in, the calculator treats clock-out as occurring the next day.

How are breaks handled?

The tool subtracts only the number of unpaid break minutes you enter.

Does the calculator decide whether a break should be paid?

No. Paid work time can vary depending on the situation and the rules that apply.

Does it calculate weekly overtime?

Yes. The weekly time card separates regular and overtime hours using the threshold and multiplier you enter. For more advanced overtime scenarios, use the Overtime Pay Calculator.

Can I calculate pay without entering an hourly rate?

Yes. You can still calculate net hours. Enter an hourly rate only if you want a gross pay estimate.

What is a time card?

A time card records work start and end times. This calculator converts those entries into paid hours after subtracting the unpaid break minutes you enter.

Do you clock out for lunch?

Workplaces can handle meal periods differently. Use the break fields only for time that should be deducted from the hours you are modeling, and check the rules that apply to your situation.

What is 3.75 hours in hours and minutes?

3.75 hours is 3 hours and 45 minutes because 0.75 of an hour equals 45 minutes.
