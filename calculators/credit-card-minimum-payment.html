Credit Card Minimum Payment Calculator

Estimate a minimum payment using a balance percentage and stated floor.

Calculate your estimate

ESTIMATED MINIMUM PAYMENT—

Percentage-and-floor estimate only. It does not separately add interest, fees or past-due amounts. Use your statement for the actual minimum due.

Formula

min(balance, max(payment floor, balance × percentage ÷ 100))

With a 2% rate and a $35 floor, the percentage equals the floor at a $1,750 balance. Below that point the floor applies, unless the remaining balance is less than $35. This is an illustration of the entered rule, not a rule shared by all cards.

Why the estimate can differ

Choose the right formula before entering numbers

  1. Find the minimum-payment rule in your card agreement. Copy its percentage and dollar floor, rather than assuming the defaults are your terms.
  2. Check which balance the percentage applies to. A rule based on a balance before interest is not the same as one using the full statement balance.
  3. If the rule adds interest, fees or past-due amounts separately, use the statement or calculate those components separately. This calculator does not add them.

For example, a hypothetical 2% rule on a $2,000 balance gives $40 before considering a $35 floor. A different hypothetical rule using 1% of $2,000 plus $40 interest gives $60. Do not enter 1% here and treat the $35 result as the amount owed under that second rule.

See how a credit card minimum payment is calculated for more examples. Your statement, not this estimate, sets the payment obligation.

How to calculate a percentage-based minimum

Multiply the card balance by the entered percentage, then compare it with the payment floor. With a $3,000 balance, 2% gives $60; a $35 floor therefore leaves the estimate at $60. With a $500 balance, the percentage gives $10, so the floor applies. The estimate is capped at the remaining balance and a zero balance produces zero.

Minimum due is not the statement balance

This tool models a percentage-and-floor rule, not every issuer formula. Some accounts add interest, fees or past-due amounts. Use the actual statement minimum for your payment obligation. Paying only that amount can leave a revolving balance and interest charges. Compare minimum payment vs statement balance before deciding what to pay.

Calculator FAQ

Does this estimate my payoff date?

No. It estimates one payment under the entered rule. Use the credit card payoff calculator for a timeline.

Can the minimum exceed the remaining balance?

This simplified estimate is capped at the balance. Actual fees and past-due amounts may change the statement amount.

Does the calculator include APR?

No. It does not model formulas that separately add monthly interest.

Will paying the minimum avoid purchase interest?

Not usually when carrying a balance. Grace-period eligibility and full-payment requirements depend on card terms.
