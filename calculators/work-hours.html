Work Hours Calculator Formula and Examples

Weekly net hours = (scheduled shift hours − entered break hours) × days worked per week.

Worked example

Example: five 8.5-hour shifts with a 30-minute entered unpaid break each produce 40 net hours for the week.

Work hours versus a time card

What your weekly hours result means

The weekly total is the paid time implied by your shift length, workdays and unpaid break. It is useful for schedule planning and pay estimates, but actual compensable time can depend on workplace rules.

What this calculator assumes

  • Every modeled workday has the same shift length.

  • The calculator measures time, not legal overtime entitlement.
  • Partial hours can be entered as decimals.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Entering 8.30 to mean 8 hours 30 minutes; use 8.5 instead.
  • Assuming all breaks should be unpaid.
  • Assuming a weekly total alone resolves every overtime question.

How to figure hours worked for payroll

Start with the scheduled shift length, subtract unpaid break time, then multiply the net shift hours by the number of workdays. If payroll needs decimal hours, remember that minutes are fractions of 60: 45 minutes = 0.75 hours, so 3.75 hours means 3 hours 45 minutes.

How to calculate minutes for payroll

Convert minutes to decimal hours by dividing minutes by 60. For example, 15 minutes = 0.25 hours, 30 minutes = 0.50 hours and 45 minutes = 0.75 hours. For exact clock-in and clock-out entries, use the Time Card Calculator.

Clock in and clock out

Clock-in marks the start of the work period you are recording and clock-out marks the end. This page is best when you already know the scheduled shift length; use Time Card when you need to calculate from exact times.

Work Hours Calculator FAQ

How do I calculate weekly work hours?

Subtract the entered break time from one shift, then multiply net shift hours by days worked per week.

How do I enter 8 hours 30 minutes?

Enter 8.5 hours because 30 minutes is half an hour.

Are short rest breaks unpaid?

Not necessarily. This tool only subtracts the break time you choose to enter.

Does 40 hours always mean overtime starts?

Federal overtime commonly uses over 40 hours in a workweek for covered, nonexempt employees, but exemptions and special rules can apply.

Can I use this for a four-day schedule?

Yes. Enter the appropriate shift length and four days per week.

How do I figure hours worked?

Subtract unpaid break time from the scheduled shift length, then multiply the net shift by the number of workdays for a weekly total.

How do I calculate minutes for payroll?

Divide minutes by 60 to convert them to decimal hours. For example, 45 minutes is 0.75 hours.

What does clocking in mean?

Clocking in records the start of a work period. If you need to calculate from exact clock-in and clock-out times, use the Time Card Calculator.
