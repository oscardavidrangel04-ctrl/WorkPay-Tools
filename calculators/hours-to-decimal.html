DECIMAL HOURS—

How to convert minutes to decimal hours

Decimal hours = whole hours + (minutes ÷ 60).

For example, 8 hours 30 minutes = 8 + 30 ÷ 60 = 8.50 hours. Twenty minutes is 0.3333 hours, not 0.20 hours.

Why payroll uses decimal hours

Clock time is written in hours and minutes, but many payroll calculations use decimal hours. The minutes portion is divided by 60 before it is added to the whole hours. For example, 7 hours 30 minutes becomes 7.50 hours, while 7 hours 15 minutes becomes 7.25 hours.

This matters because minutes are not hundredths of an hour. Entering 7:30 as 7.30 hours understates the time worked. A decimal conversion helps keep time-card totals, hourly-pay estimates and spreadsheet calculations consistent.

Hours-to-decimal worked examples

To convert 8 hours 20 minutes, divide 20 by 60. The result is 0.3333, so the total is about 8.33 decimal hours. For 6 hours 45 minutes, 45 ÷ 60 equals 0.75, giving 6.75 hours.

If you already have clock-in, clock-out and break times for several days, the Time Card Calculator is usually faster because it totals the week for you. For a repeating daily schedule, use the Work Hours Calculator.

Using decimal hours for pay estimates

Once time is in decimal format, multiply decimal hours by the hourly rate to estimate gross straight-time earnings. If the weekly total crosses an overtime threshold, do not apply the same rate to every hour. Use the Overtime Pay Calculator to separate regular and premium hours.

For example, 7 hours 45 minutes converts to 7.75 hours. At $20 per hour, 7.75 × $20 equals $155 in gross straight-time pay before taxes or deductions.

Hours to Decimal FAQ

Is 30 minutes 0.30 hours?

No. Thirty minutes is half an hour, so it equals 0.50 decimal hours.

Why do timesheets use decimal hours?

Decimal hours make multiplication by an hourly pay rate straightforward.
