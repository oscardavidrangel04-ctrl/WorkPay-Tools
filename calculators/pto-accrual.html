PTO accrual example

At an accrual rate of 0.05 hours per hour worked, an 80-hour pay period produces 4 accrued PTO hours. Policies can set different accrual rates by tenure, job type or pay period.

Question to check

Does this show PTO available today?

No. It estimates newly accrued time only; approved leave, caps and prior balances are separate policy items.

How to calculate PTO accrual per hour worked

PTO earned = eligible hours worked × PTO hours earned per hour. For a policy granting 104 hours over 2,080 eligible hours, the accrual rate is 104 ÷ 2,080 = 0.05. Enter 0.05, not 5. Working 80 eligible hours then earns 4 PTO hours.

Per-hour accrual versus a fixed pay-period grant

A policy granting 4 hours each biweekly period is not necessarily an hourly policy. A shorter work period might still receive that fixed grant. Use this calculator only when your employer's policy bases accrual on eligible hours.

Accrued PTO is different from your available balance

A starting balance of 12 hours plus 4 earned hours minus 8 used hours leaves 8 hours, before any policy adjustments. This calculator displays only the 4 newly earned hours. It does not subtract leave taken or apply a cap.

What to check in your PTO policy

  • Which worked or paid hours qualify for accrual.
  • When accrual begins and when earned hours become available.
  • Whether tenure changes the rate.
  • Whether a balance cap pauses accrual.
  • How carryover differs from the maximum balance.

After confirming the balance, the PTO value calculator can estimate its gross wage equivalent. That estimate does not establish a cash payout entitlement.
