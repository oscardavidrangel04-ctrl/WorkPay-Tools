Proration example

A $6,000 annual bonus with nine eligible months produces a simple $4,500 estimate. Bonus plans can use start dates, performance measures, payout dates or discretionary conditions instead.

How to calculate a prorated bonus

For this monthly method, multiply the full-year bonus by eligible months divided by 12. A $6,000 bonus with nine eligible months gives $4,500. Six months gives $3,000; zero months gives zero. Fractional months can be entered only when the employer’s counting method supports them.

Monthly proration vs a daily bonus calculation

This tool uses a 12-month denominator. It does not count calendar days, determine start-date eligibility or assess performance targets. If the plan uses eligible days divided by days in a plan year, calculate with that method instead. For salary rather than bonus, use prorated salary. Neither result establishes an entitlement to payment.

Calculator FAQ

What is a prorated bonus?

A portion of a full-period bonus based on eligible service or another plan-defined measure.

Can I enter more than 12 months?

No for this annual monthly method. Use the correct plan period instead.

Does the estimate include taxes?

No. It is a gross planning amount.

Does my employer have to use this formula?

No. The bonus agreement can use different eligibility, performance and proration rules.
