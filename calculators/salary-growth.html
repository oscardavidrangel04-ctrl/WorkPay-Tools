How to use a salary growth projection

Start with your current gross annual salary, then enter the percentage increase you want to model and the number of years. The calculator applies the same percentage increase once per year. This makes it useful for comparing a steady raise pattern, estimating how a promotion path may change pay, or testing several long-term salary scenarios.

The result is a projection, not a guarantee. Real compensation can change because of promotions, job changes, bonuses, market adjustments, reduced hours or periods with no raise. For a single raise rather than a multi-year projection, use the Salary Increase Calculator.

Why salary growth compounds over time

With percentage raises, each increase is applied to the salary after the previous increase. That is why a 3% raise for five years is not the same as simply adding 15% to the starting salary. Compounding makes later raises slightly larger in dollar terms because they are calculated from a higher base.

For example, a $60,000 salary with a 3% annual raise becomes $61,800 after one year, $63,654 after two years and about $69,556 after five years. The annual percentage stays the same, while the dollar increase grows.

Compare salary growth with other compensation changes

Salary growth is only one part of compensation planning. A job with slower base-salary growth may still have a stronger overall package if it includes bonuses, employer retirement contributions, benefits or equity. Use the Compensation Package Calculator to compare those components. If you are comparing employee pay with independent work, the Contractor Salary Calculator can translate an annual income target into a billable hourly rate.

You can also use the Annual Income Calculator to model hourly income and the Salary to Hourly Calculator to convert a projected salary into an hourly equivalent.

Salary growth scenarios to test

  • Conservative: use a lower annual percentage to model modest recurring increases.
  • Target: enter the raise rate you expect based on your own planning assumptions.
  • Promotion scenario: compare a higher growth rate to see how faster salary progression changes the long-term result.
  • No-growth baseline: use 0% to compare future purchasing or budgeting needs against a flat nominal salary.

Run more than one scenario instead of treating a single percentage as certain. The difference between two growth rates becomes more noticeable as the time horizon gets longer.

Salary Growth FAQ

How does compounding work?

Each annual percentage increase applies to the previous year's salary.

Does this include inflation?

No. The calculator shows nominal salary growth.
