Frequently asked questions

Does this calculate net savings after the transfer fee?

No. It compares simple interest on a constant balance. Subtract transfer fees separately and review payment timing and promotional terms.

What does a negative result mean?

It means the transfer APR produces more interest under the entered assumptions. The calculation does not enforce a zero-percent promotional offer.

Interest reduction is not net balance transfer savings

A constant $4,000 balance at 24% versus 0% over 12 months has a simple interest difference of $960. A 3% transfer fee adds $120 of upfront cost, leaving $840 after subtracting only that fee in this simplified comparison. Calculate the fee with the balance transfer fee calculator.

Why the constant-balance model can overstate savings

If you repay principal during the period, interest on the original card can decline. This tool holds the balance constant and does not model payments, compounding or promotional-rate expiration. It cannot predict an exact break-even date or payoff schedule.

A higher transfer APR can produce additional interest

A negative result means the transfer APR costs more under the entered assumptions. It is not a savings recommendation. Use a time horizon that fits the offer, and review post-promotion terms separately.
