How to total monthly bills

Enter housing separately, then add utilities, insurance, debt minimums, subscriptions and other recurring monthly costs in the second field. Housing of $1,800 plus $1,200 in other bills totals $3,000. Do not include housing again in the other-bills amount.

Convert nonmonthly costs before adding them

A $600 annual renewal averages $50 per month. A $40 weekly cost averages about $173.33 per month using 52 payments divided by 12. These averages are not the dates bills are due. Keep a payment calendar or reserve for larger periodic bills. This tool adds two already-monthly amounts; it does not fetch bills or convert their frequency automatically.

Calculator FAQ

Does this total groceries and other variable spending?

Only if you include a chosen monthly estimate in the other-bills field.

Can I enter an annual insurance premium directly?

Convert it to a monthly average first, usually by dividing the annual amount by 12.

Is the result a complete household budget?

No. It is the sum of the entered bills; savings, irregular costs or spending may still be missing.

How do I use it for an emergency fund?

Start with the necessary monthly expenses you intend to cover, then use the emergency fund calculator for a chosen number of months.

Separate the monthly reserve from this month's payment

A $1,200 annual insurance bill needs a $100 monthly reserve if funded evenly across 12 months. In the payment month, the bank withdrawal is still $1,200. Count the reserve in the planning budget or the withdrawal in that month's cash-flow record; adding both to the same expense total would count the cost twice.

List each bill once, with its frequency and due date. Keep transfers to savings separate from payments to the provider. See sinking funds versus emergency funds for the difference between a known future bill and a reserve for unexpected costs.
