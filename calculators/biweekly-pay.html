How to Calculate Biweekly Pay

Biweekly pay is pay received every two weeks. For salaried employees on a standard 26-pay-period schedule, the basic formula is:

Biweekly pay = annual salary ÷ 26 pay periods

For hourly workers, a simple straight-time estimate uses:

Biweekly pay = hourly rate × number of hours worked per week × 2

For example, a $65,000 annual salary divided by 26 equals about $2,500 in gross pay every two weeks. An hourly worker earning $25 for 40 hours per week would estimate $25 × 40 × 2 = $2,000 before taxes and deductions.

How Does Biweekly Pay Work?

A biweekly pay frequency schedules a payday every 14 days. Most calendar years therefore contain 26 biweekly pay periods. This is different from getting paid twice each month, because the dates move through the calendar rather than staying on fixed days of the month.

Some payroll calendars can contain 27 pay periods. That happens occasionally because a calendar year is longer than exactly 52 weeks. If your employer has 27 pay dates, use the pay schedule provided by payroll instead of assuming that every annual salary is divided the same way.

Biweekly vs. Semimonthly Pay

Biweekly and semimonthly pay are not the same. Biweekly employees are paid every two weeks, which usually creates 26 paychecks per year. Semimonthly employees are paid twice per month, which usually creates 24 paychecks per year.

That difference changes the amount of each gross paycheck even when annual income is the same. For a direct comparison, use the Semimonthly Pay Calculator.

Common Biweekly Pay Mistakes

  • Do not assume biweekly means two paychecks in every month. Some months can contain three pay dates.
  • Do not confuse 26 biweekly pay periods with 24 semimonthly pay periods.
  • Do not treat gross pay as take-home pay. Payroll deductions can reduce the amount deposited.
  • Do not use the straight-time hourly formula when overtime changes the paycheck.

Biweekly Pay FAQ

What is biweekly pay?

Biweekly pay means you are paid every two weeks. Most biweekly schedules have 26 pay periods per year.

How many biweekly pay periods are in a year?

Most years have 26 biweekly pay periods. Some payroll calendars can have 27 because paydays repeat every 14 days.

How do I calculate biweekly pay from salary?

Divide your annual salary by 26 to estimate biweekly gross pay on a standard 26-pay-period schedule.

What does biweekly salary mean?

It means the annual salary is paid on an every-two-weeks schedule. The annual salary does not change just because the pay frequency is biweekly.

Is biweekly pay the same as twice a month?

No. Biweekly pay is every 14 days and usually creates 26 pay periods. Semimonthly pay is twice per month and usually creates 24.

Is the result before taxes and deductions?

Yes. The calculator shows estimated gross pay before taxes, benefits, insurance, and other payroll deductions.
