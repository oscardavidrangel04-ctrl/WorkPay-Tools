Step 1

Formula for converting hourly pay to annual salary

Weekly pay = hourly rate × paid hours per week

Annual pay = weekly pay × paid weeks per year

Average monthly pay is annual pay ÷ 12

For a $25 hourly wage, 40 paid hours per week and 52 paid weeks, the calculation is $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000 per year. The result is gross pay before taxes and deductions.

Step 2

Choose paid weeks carefully

Using 52 weeks assumes every week in the year is paid. If you expect unpaid time off, seasonal gaps or unpaid leave, reduce the paid-weeks field.

Step 3

Gross pay is not take-home pay

The calculator estimates gross cash earnings. It does not subtract federal, state or local taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, garnishments or other deductions.

Overtime needs a different model

What the result means

The annual amount is the gross cash pay implied by the hourly rate and schedule you entered. It is an annualized planning estimate, not a guarantee of hours or earnings.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming every year has 52 paid weeks when some time off is unpaid.
  • Using four weeks to estimate every month instead of annual pay ÷ 12.
  • Ignoring overtime or premium pay when it materially changes earnings.
  • Comparing salary offers without separately considering benefits and paid leave.

Part-time and variable schedules

The formula works for part-time jobs. Use your realistic average paid hours per week. If your schedule changes significantly, compare several scenarios instead of treating one annual estimate as exact.

Biweekly vs semimonthly

Biweekly averages divide annual pay by 26. Semimonthly averages divide annual pay by 24. They can look similar but represent different pay-period schedules.

Paycheck context

How annual salary converts to common pay periods

After estimating annual gross pay, these standard divisions help compare pay schedules. Actual payroll can differ because of unpaid time, irregular hours, bonuses or payroll timing.

Weekly

Annual gross pay ÷ 52. This is useful for comparing a steady weekly schedule.

Biweekly

Annual gross pay ÷ 26. Biweekly schedules generally produce 26 pay periods in a year.

Semimonthly

Annual gross pay ÷ 24. Semimonthly schedules generally pay twice per month.

Average monthly

Annual gross pay ÷ 12. This is an average, not a prediction of any specific monthly paycheck.

Why paid weeks matter in hourly-to-salary comparisons

Two jobs with the same hourly rate can produce different annual income if one job provides fewer paid weeks. This calculator keeps weekly hours and paid weeks separate so you can model seasonal work, unpaid time off or shorter work years.

The standard shortcut of hourly rate × 2,080 assumes 40 paid hours every week for 52 weeks. Use your actual schedule when that assumption does not fit.

Hourly pay questions

Hourly to Salary Calculator FAQ

How do I convert hourly pay to annual salary?

Multiply your hourly rate by paid hours per week, then by paid weeks per year. Example: $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000 in estimated annual gross pay.

How much is $20 an hour per year?

At 40 paid hours per week for 52 paid weeks, $20 per hour equals about $41,600 in annual gross pay.

How much is $25 an hour per year?

At 40 paid hours per week for 52 paid weeks, $25 per hour equals about $52,000 in annual gross pay.

How many work hours are in a 40-hour, 52-week year?

Forty paid hours per week across 52 paid weeks equals 2,080 paid hours per year.

Can I use this calculator for part-time work?

Yes. Enter your actual average paid hours per week and realistic paid weeks per year instead of using a 40-hour default.

What if I take unpaid weeks off?

Reduce paid weeks per year. For example, use 50 paid weeks to model two unpaid weeks.

Does the hourly to salary calculator include overtime?

It can. Turn on the weekly overtime option to apply your entered threshold and multiplier to hours above the threshold. Leave it off for a straight-time hourly-to-salary conversion.

Why is monthly salary shown as an average?

The calculator annualizes the schedule and divides by 12. Calendar months have different numbers of days and workweeks, so this is an average.

Is the result gross pay or take-home pay?

The calculator shows estimated gross pay. It does not subtract taxes, benefits, retirement contributions, or other payroll deductions.

How should I compare hourly pay with a salary offer?

Compare annual gross-pay equivalents, then separately consider paid leave, bonuses, benefits, retirement contributions, schedule stability and premium-pay eligibility.

Accuracy & sources

Assumptions behind this pay conversion

The calculation uses simple arithmetic, not a tax or legal rule: hourly rate × paid hours per week × paid weeks per year. It treats the result as estimated gross cash pay and does not add overtime, bonuses, benefits or deductions.

Schedule assumption

Use 52 paid weeks only when that reflects your situation. Reduce the value for unpaid weeks or seasonal gaps.

Overtime assumption

This page intentionally uses one regular hourly rate. For U.S. overtime questions, see the U.S. Department of Labor and use our overtime pay calculator for a separate estimate.

What is excluded

Taxes, payroll deductions, bonuses, commissions, benefits, paid-leave value and employer costs are outside this calculator’s scope.
