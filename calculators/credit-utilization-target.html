Calculate a target credit card balance

Multiply the credit limit by the target percentage divided by 100. A $10,000 limit at 30% gives a $3,000 target balance; at 10%, it gives $1,000. The percentage is your chosen planning target, not a guaranteed scoring threshold.

Target balance vs payment needed

If your current balance is $4,000 and the target is $1,000, the illustrative reduction is $3,000. This page outputs the target balance, not that payment. Use the credit utilization calculator to check the current ratio. New charges, interest or a changed limit can alter the comparison. The CFPB explains that real scores also depend on other report information and the scoring model.

Calculator FAQ

Does a 30% target mean my score will improve?

No. This is a balance calculation, not a score forecast or universal scoring cutoff.

Can I calculate a zero-percent target?

Yes. A 0% target gives a zero balance.

Should I use one card or combined limits?

Use the limit matching the balances you intend to compare. An overall target can hide high utilization on an individual card.

Does this tell me the payment due?

No. Your statement sets the payment obligation. This tool calculates a chosen balance goal.
