Salary Range Penetration Calculator

Calculate where a salary falls between the minimum and maximum of a pay range. This is a mathematical comparison, not a pay recommendation or equity determination.

Calculate range penetration

RANGE PENETRATION—

Salary range penetration formula

(Salary − minimum) ÷ (maximum − minimum) × 100

For a $72,000 salary in a $60,000–$90,000 range, penetration is 40%.

How to interpret the result

0% equals the range minimum, 50% is halfway between minimum and maximum, and 100% equals the maximum. Results below 0% or above 100% indicate salary falls outside the entered range.

Range penetration vs compa-ratio

Range penetration uses both endpoints. Compa-ratio compares salary directly with the midpoint. Do not treat the two percentages as interchangeable.

Worked examples at the endpoints and midpoint

For a $60,000–$90,000 range, a $60,000 salary has 0% penetration, $75,000 has 50%, and $90,000 has 100%. A $54,000 salary gives −20%; $96,000 gives 120%. Keeping outside-range results visible helps identify whether salary is below or above the entered band.

Range penetration is not a salary percentile

A 40% result means the salary is 40% of the distance from minimum to maximum. It does not mean the employee earns more than 40% of workers. Use matching currencies and time periods for all inputs. Changing a band can change penetration without changing pay. A published policy midpoint may also differ from the arithmetic midpoint.

Calculator FAQ

What is a good salary range penetration?

There is no universal target. Job level, policy, skills and other evidence matter; this formula alone does not establish fair pay.

Can range penetration be negative?

Yes. A negative result means salary is below the entered minimum.

Is range penetration the same as compa-ratio?

No. Penetration measures distance within a band; compa-ratio compares salary with a midpoint.

Why must maximum exceed minimum?

The formula divides by the range width. A zero or reversed width is not a valid band.
