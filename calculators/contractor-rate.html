Frequently asked questions

Should I use working hours or billable hours?

Use the annual hours you expect to bill. Administration, unpaid gaps and other non-billable time should not inflate the denominator.

Is this rate a guaranteed after-tax income?

No. The calculator covers the income target and business costs entered, but does not independently calculate taxes, benefits or market demand.

Calculate a contractor rate using billable hours

For an $85,000 income target, $15,000 in annual business costs and 1,500 billable hours, the target is $100,000 ÷ 1,500 = $66.67 per billable hour. At 1,000 billable hours, the same revenue requirement needs $100 per hour.

Working hours are not automatically billable hours

Administration, proposals and unpaid gaps can reduce hours available to invoice. Use the billable hours calculator to estimate a weekly schedule before annualizing it. Match annual costs with annual billable hours.

What this hourly rate does not include

The model adds the costs you enter; it does not independently calculate taxes, benefits, profit margin, late payments or market demand. Treat the income target as a stated planning input, not guaranteed take-home income. Zero billable hours cannot define an hourly rate.
