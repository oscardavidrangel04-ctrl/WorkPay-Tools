How overtime pay is calculated

For this simple hourly-rate model, regular hours are paid at the base hourly rate. Hours above the selected threshold are paid at the base rate multiplied by the overtime multiplier.

Regular pay = regular hours × hourly rate

Overtime rate = hourly rate × overtime multiplier

Overtime pay = overtime hours × overtime rate

Total weekly gross pay = regular pay + overtime pay

Worked example

At $20 per hour for 45 hours, with a 40-hour threshold and a 1.5× multiplier, the first 40 hours equal $800. The 5 overtime hours are paid at $30 per hour, adding $150. Estimated weekly gross pay is $950.

Basic vs. advanced mode

Basic mode uses the familiar hourly-rate model: hours up to the threshold are paid at the base rate and overtime hours are paid at the selected multiplier.

Advanced mode provides a simplified regular-rate planning estimate when you enter eligible additional compensation. It treats base wages for all hours plus the additional compensation as straight-time compensation, calculates an estimated regular rate, then adds the overtime premium. Real payroll calculations can require different treatment depending on the type of payment and applicable law.

Multiple pay rates and the regular rate

When an employee works at more than one hourly rate in a workweek, the regular rate can be based on total includable straight-time compensation divided by total hours worked. The advanced worksheet helps model that weighted-rate concept before applying an overtime premium.

This is not universal payroll logic. Special agreements, exclusions, tipped work, piece rates, commissions and other compensation methods can require different calculations.

2026 qualified overtime tax context

Federal tax law currently provides a deduction for certain qualified overtime compensation for tax years 2025 through 2028. The IRS explains that limits, filing-status rules, income phaseouts and qualification requirements apply. The premium tracker above is intentionally limited to showing the estimated premium component; it does not claim that amount is deductible.

Federal overtime context

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, covered nonexempt employees generally must receive at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. Federal law does not generally require overtime simply because work occurs on Saturday, Sunday, a holiday or a regular day of rest.

A workweek is a fixed, regularly recurring period of seven consecutive 24-hour periods. Hours generally cannot be averaged across two or more workweeks to avoid overtime.

State or local law, occupation-specific rules, exemptions, collective bargaining agreements and employer policies may provide different or additional requirements.

What this calculator assumes

  • You are modeling one workweek.
  • The hourly rate you enter is used as the base rate for this simplified estimate.
  • The default threshold is 40 hours and the default multiplier is 1.5×.
  • Basic mode excludes bonuses and other compensation. Advanced mode can model additional regular-rate compensation, but it remains a simplified estimate.
  • The result is gross pay before taxes and deductions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Averaging hours across two different workweeks.
  • Assuming every employee has the same overtime eligibility.
  • Assuming weekends automatically qualify for overtime under federal law.
  • Treating gross pay as take-home pay.
  • Ignoring state rules that may be more protective than the federal baseline.

Overtime Pay Calculator FAQ

What is time-and-a-half?

Time-and-a-half means 1.5 times the regular hourly rate. At $20 per hour, a 1.5× overtime rate is $30 per hour.

Is overtime always after 40 hours?

The federal FLSA generally uses more than 40 hours in a workweek for covered, nonexempt employees. State laws, exemptions and special rules can differ.

Does working weekends automatically mean overtime?

Not under the general federal rule. Working Saturday, Sunday, a holiday or a regular day of rest does not by itself require overtime pay, although another law, contract or employer policy may apply.

Does overtime start after 8 hours in one day?

The general federal FLSA overtime standard is weekly rather than a universal daily overtime rule. Some states can have additional daily overtime requirements.

Is overtime taxed differently?

This calculator does not estimate taxes. Overtime earnings are included in gross wages, while withholding depends on payroll and tax circumstances.

Does PTO count toward overtime?

Overtime under the federal FLSA is generally based on hours actually worked. Paid time off policies and state rules can vary, so check the rule that applies to your situation.

Can salaried employees receive overtime?

Some salaried employees may be eligible for overtime and others may be exempt. Salary alone does not determine the answer.

Can bonuses or commissions affect overtime?

They can in some situations because certain compensation can affect the regular rate used for overtime. This calculator intentionally uses a simplified hourly-rate model.

Does this calculator include taxes or deductions?

No. It estimates gross pay before taxes, benefits, garnishments and other deductions.

How does the multiple-rate worksheet work?

It totals straight-time wages from the rate/hour pairs, divides those wages by total hours to estimate a weighted hourly rate, and then uses that rate in the advanced weekly model. Actual payroll treatment can differ.

Does the overtime premium tracker calculate the new federal tax deduction?

No. It only isolates the estimated premium portion of overtime pay. IRS eligibility, annual limits, income phaseouts and other requirements must still be evaluated separately.
