Overtime pay: quick answers for common searches

What is time-and-a-half?
Time-and-a-half is 1.5 times the regular hourly rate. A $20 regular rate becomes $30 per overtime hour.
How do you calculate overtime pay?
Multiply overtime hours by the overtime rate, then add regular pay. Example: 40 × $20 + 5 × $30 = $950 gross pay.
Does federal overtime always start after 40 hours?
For covered, nonexempt employees under the FLSA, the general federal rule is overtime after 40 hours in a workweek. Exemptions and state rules can change the result.
Is all overtime pay deductible from federal income tax?
No. The federal deduction applies only to qualified overtime compensation and has eligibility rules and annual limits. Use IRS guidance for tax eligibility.
How do you calculate overtime pay?

For a common U.S. federal overtime example, hours over 40 in one workweek are paid at 1.5× the regular rate for covered, nonexempt employees. At $20/hour for 45 hours, that is $800 regular pay + $150 overtime pay = $950 gross pay.

How to Calculate Overtime Pay

Start by separating regular hours from overtime hours. Then use your regular hourly rate and overtime multiplier to calculate each part of weekly pay.

Regular pay = regular hours × hourly rate

Overtime rate = hourly rate × overtime multiplier

Overtime pay = overtime hours × overtime rate

Total weekly gross pay = regular pay + overtime pay

  1. Find overtime hours: total weekly hours − overtime threshold.
  2. Find the overtime rate: regular hourly rate × overtime multiplier.
  3. Calculate regular pay: regular hours × regular hourly rate.
  4. Calculate overtime pay: overtime hours × overtime rate.
  5. Add both amounts: regular pay + overtime pay = estimated weekly gross pay.

Worked example

At a $20 hourly rate, time and a half is $30 per hour. For a 45-hour week with a 40-hour threshold, regular pay is $800. Five overtime hours at $30 per hour add $150. The estimated weekly gross pay is $950.

Time and a Half Calculator

A time and a half calculator multiplies the regular hourly rate by 1.5. For example, a $20 regular hourly rate becomes a $30 overtime rate.

Time-and-a-half rate = regular hourly rate × 1.5

  1. Find your regular hourly rate.
  2. Multiply that rate by 1.5.
  3. Multiply the time-and-a-half rate by your overtime hours.

Basic vs. advanced overtime calculation

Basic mode uses the familiar hourly-rate model. Hours up to the threshold are paid at the base rate. Overtime hours are paid at the selected multiplier.

Advanced mode provides a simplified regular-rate planning estimate when you enter eligible additional compensation. It treats base wages for all hours, plus eligible extra pay, as straight-time pay. It estimates the regular rate, then adds the overtime premium. Real payroll calculations can require different treatment depending on the type of payment and applicable law.

Weighted Overtime Calculator

If you work at more than one hourly rate in a workweek, the calculator can estimate a weighted regular rate. It adds straight-time pay from the rate/hour pairs and divides that amount by total hours worked. The advanced worksheet then uses that rate to estimate the overtime premium.

Federal overtime rules: 40 hours and time-and-a-half

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), many covered nonexempt employees must receive at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Federal law does not usually require overtime just because you work on a weekend, holiday, or regular day off.

A workweek is a fixed, regularly recurring period of seven consecutive 24-hour periods. Hours generally cannot be averaged across two or more workweeks to avoid overtime.

State or local law, occupation-specific rules, exemptions, collective bargaining agreements and employer policies may provide different or additional requirements.

For planning outside the standard 1.5× example, compare the Double Time Calculator or estimate premiums with the Shift Differential Calculator.

What counts as hours worked for overtime?

Federal overtime is based on hours worked in a workweek, but determining what time counts as work can depend on the situation. The U.S. Department of Labor discusses topics such as waiting time, on-call time, meal periods, sleep time, meetings, training and travel in its hours-worked guidance.

This calculator does not decide whether a specific block of time legally counts as hours worked. First determine the hours that apply to your situation, then enter the weekly total here.

Overtime calculator assumptions and limits

  • The calculator estimates one workweek at a time.
  • The hourly rate you enter is used as the base rate for this simplified estimate.
  • The default threshold is 40 hours and the default multiplier is 1.5×.
  • Basic mode excludes bonuses and other compensation. Advanced mode can model additional regular-rate compensation, but it remains a simplified estimate.
  • The result is gross pay before taxes and deductions.

Common overtime calculation mistakes

  • Averaging hours across two different workweeks.
  • Assuming every employee has the same overtime eligibility.
  • Assuming weekends automatically qualify for overtime under federal law.
  • Confusing gross pay with take-home pay.
  • Ignoring state rules that may be more protective than the federal baseline.

What if I have no overtime hours?

If total hours do not exceed the threshold you enter, the calculator shows zero overtime hours and no overtime premium. The weekly result is then based on straight-time pay only.

The threshold field is adjustable because this tool is a planning calculator. A legal overtime threshold can depend on worker classification, jurisdiction and other rules; the calculator does not determine legal eligibility.

Salaried employees and overtime

Being paid a salary does not by itself decide overtime eligibility. Some salaried workers may be nonexempt and eligible for overtime, while others may meet an exemption. The facts and applicable federal, state or local rules matter; use the official sources below for legal context.

Overtime Pay Calculator FAQ

What is time-and-a-half?

Time-and-a-half means 1.5 times the regular hourly rate. At $20 per hour, a 1.5× overtime rate is $30 per hour.

Is overtime always after 40 hours?

Under the federal FLSA, overtime generally starts after 40 hours in a workweek for covered nonexempt employees. State laws and exemptions can differ.

Does working weekends automatically mean overtime?

Not under the general federal rule. Working on a weekend or holiday does not automatically mean overtime pay. State rules, contracts, or employer policies may differ.

Does overtime start after 8 hours in one day?

Federal overtime rules generally use a weekly standard, not a universal daily rule. Some states have additional daily overtime requirements.

Is overtime taxed differently?

This calculator does not estimate taxes. Overtime earnings are included in gross wages, while withholding depends on payroll and tax circumstances.

Does PTO count toward overtime?

Overtime under the federal FLSA is generally based on hours actually worked. Paid time off policies and state rules can vary, so check the rule that applies to your situation.

Can salaried employees receive overtime?

Some salaried employees may be eligible for overtime and others may be exempt. Salary alone does not determine the answer.

Can bonuses or commissions affect overtime?

Yes, in some cases. Certain compensation can change the regular rate used to calculate overtime. This calculator uses a simplified hourly-rate model.

Does this calculator include taxes or deductions?

No. It estimates gross pay before taxes, benefits, garnishments and other deductions.

How does the multiple-rate worksheet work?

It adds straight-time wages, divides them by total hours, and estimates a weighted hourly rate. The advanced weekly model then uses that rate. Actual payroll treatment can differ.

Does the overtime premium tracker calculate the new federal tax deduction?

No. It only isolates the estimated premium portion of overtime pay. IRS eligibility, annual limits, income phaseouts and other requirements must still be evaluated separately.

How much overtime is 50 hours in a week?

With a 40-hour threshold, 50 hours means 40 regular hours and 10 overtime hours. At $20/hour and a 1.5× multiplier, the estimate is $800 regular pay + $300 overtime pay = $1,100 gross pay.

How do I calculate overtime pay?

Subtract the overtime threshold from total hours to find overtime hours, then multiply those hours by the overtime rate. This calculator also shows the straight-time portion and overtime premium separately.

Does overtime pay more than regular time?

When a premium multiplier applies, yes. For example, time-and-a-half pays 1.5 times the regular hourly rate for the overtime hours included in the calculation.

Do salaried employees get overtime pay?

Some do and some do not. Salary alone does not determine overtime eligibility; exempt or nonexempt status depends on the applicable rules and the worker's circumstances.

Can an employer not pay overtime?

Whether overtime is legally required depends on coverage, exemption status, hours worked and the applicable federal, state or local rules. This calculator estimates the math and does not decide legal entitlement.

What is the difference between exempt and nonexempt?

In general, nonexempt workers can be covered by overtime protections, while exempt workers may fall outside particular overtime requirements if the relevant exemption rules are met. Job title or salary alone is not enough to make that determination.
