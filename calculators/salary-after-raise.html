How to use the result

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculate several annual raises?

No. It applies one nonnegative percentage increase to current salary. Use Salary Growth for a repeated annual raise scenario.

Does the result show take-home pay?

No. It is the new annual gross salary rate before payroll deductions and without adjusting for a midyear effective date.

Calculate new salary after a percentage raise

A $60,000 salary and 5% raise give $60,000 × 1.05 = $63,000 in new annual gross salary. The increase is $3,000 a year, or a $250 monthly average. This is an annualized rate, not a prediction of net pay.

This tool starts with a known percentage

If you need to compare increases or understand the dollar change, use the pay raise calculator. For repeated annual raises, use the salary growth calculator. Two 5% raises compound to 10.25%, not 10%.

Effective dates change calendar-year earnings

A raise beginning halfway through a year does not apply to the whole year's pay. Model earnings before and after the effective date separately. The entered percentage is nonnegative; this tool is not designed for pay cuts.
