ESTIMATED CYCLE INTEREST—

Assumes one constant balance and APR. Actual interest may compound daily and use changing daily balances.

Daily interest estimate

Balance × (APR ÷ 365) × cycle days

The CFPB explains that many issuers calculate interest daily using an average daily balance. Review the actual agreement for the applicable method.

Official source: CFPB: how credit card interest is calculated.

APR and grace periods

Before using the estimate, review how purchase interest can be avoided when an active grace period applies.

Card agreements can set different rates for transactions. Compare purchase APR vs cash advance APR before using one rate for a planning estimate.

APR is an annual rate. If a card provides a grace period and its conditions are met, paying the purchase balance in full by the due date can avoid purchase interest. Cash advances and other categories may work differently.

Credit Card Interest FAQ

Why is this different from my statement?

Your daily balance can change, rates can differ by transaction type, and fees or compounding can apply.

Does the planned payment reduce this cycle’s estimate?

No. It is shown only as a comparison with estimated interest because payment timing matters in a daily-balance calculation.

Credit card interest calculation example

For a constant $3,000 balance at 24% APR over 30 days, the estimate is $3,000 × 0.24 ÷ 365 × 30 = $59.18. A 31-day cycle gives $61.15 under the same assumptions. The cycle length matters even when the balance and APR stay unchanged.

Average daily balance is not always today's balance

If the balance is $3,000 for 15 days and $2,000 for 15 days, the simple average is $2,500. Entering today's $2,000 balance would understate that cycle's average. This tool does not build a daily transaction ledger or model compounding.

Keep different APR categories separate

Estimate purchase and cash-advance balances separately if their APRs differ. The planned-payment field compares payment size with interest; it does not subtract the payment from the modeled balance.

What is a minimum interest charge?

Some card agreements set a minimum interest charge for a cycle when interest applies. This is different from the minimum payment due, which is the amount you must pay by the deadline.

For example, if the calculated interest is $0.40 and the agreement imposes a $1 minimum interest charge in that situation, the charge would be $1 instead of $0.40. Those amounts are hypothetical; check your card's actual terms. A minimum-interest clause does not mean every account owes interest in every cycle.

This calculator does not apply that floor. It estimates interest from the entered balance, APR and days. If your statement differs, check both the balance method and any minimum-interest provision. The CFPB's card-disclosure rules distinguish minimum interest charges from other fees and payment information.
