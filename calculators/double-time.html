Double Time Calculator Formula and Examples

Double-time rate = hourly rate × 2. Double-time pay = double-time hours × double-time rate.

Worked example

Example: $24/hour × 2 = $48 double-time rate. Six double-time hours = $288 gross double-time pay.

Double time versus standard overtime

This page owns 2× premium-pay intent. If you need time-and-a-half, weekly overtime hours or regular-rate context, use the Overtime Pay Calculator.

What your double-time result means

The result separates ordinary earnings from hours paid at a 2× rate. Double time is not automatically required in every job or jurisdiction, so use the total as a planning estimate unless you know a 2× rule applies.

Common ways to use this calculator

  • Value hours paid at twice the base hourly rate.

What this calculator assumes

  • The entered hours are already identified as double-time hours.
  • A 2× multiplier is used.
  • Regular-pay hours outside the entered double-time hours are not included.
  • The tool does not determine entitlement to double time.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming federal law generally requires double-time pay.
  • Confusing 1.5× overtime with 2× double time.
  • Adding only the extra premium instead of the full 2× rate when you want total double-time pay.

Double Time Calculator FAQ

What is double-time pay?

For this calculator, double time means two times the entered base hourly rate.

Does the FLSA require double-time pay?

The FLSA does not impose a general double-time requirement.

Is double time the same as overtime?

No. The common federal overtime rule for covered, nonexempt employees is at least 1.5× the regular rate after 40 hours in a workweek.

Does this include regular pay for other hours?

No. It isolates the entered double-time hours.

Can state rules differ?

Yes. State law or other agreements can provide rules beyond the federal baseline.
