Compensation Package Calculator

Estimate total compensation from salary, bonus, employer retirement contributions, benefits and annualized equity.

Reviewed September 6, 2026

Estimate scope: Results are planning estimates. Taxes, deductions, benefits and employer-specific rules are excluded unless explicitly stated. Methodology.

Estimate total compensation

ESTIMATED TOTAL COMPENSATION—

Use documented or reasonable annual values. This is not a tax valuation.

Total compensation formula

Total compensation = salary + bonus + employer retirement contribution + benefits + annualized equity

Compare offers beyond base salary

Two jobs can have similar salaries but different bonuses, retirement support, insurance benefits or equity. Adding the annualized components can make offer comparisons clearer.

What counts as total compensation?

Total compensation is broader than base salary. Depending on the offer, it can include cash bonus, employer retirement contributions, employer-paid insurance or benefits, and the annualized value of equity or other compensation. This calculator adds the annual values you enter so you can compare packages on a consistent basis.

The result is an estimated gross value, not take-home pay. Some benefits may be difficult to value precisely, and taxes can apply differently to salary, bonus and equity. Use documented employer values when available and keep uncertain estimates conservative.

Total compensation example

Consider an offer with an $80,000 base salary, a $5,000 expected annual bonus, $4,000 in employer retirement contributions, $7,000 in employer-paid benefits and $4,000 of annualized equity value. The estimated total compensation is $100,000.

A second offer with a higher salary can still have a lower total package if it provides fewer employer-paid benefits or no bonus. Looking at the components separately makes the trade-off easier to see.

How to compare two job offers

Use the same time period and valuation approach for both offers. Compare base salary first, then add recurring annual components. Treat one-time signing bonuses separately if you do not expect them to repeat. For equity, use an annualized value rather than adding the full multi-year grant to one year.

Then compare the result with salary growth. The Salary Growth Calculator can project recurring raises, while the Salary Increase Calculator is better for a single raise. If one option is contract work, use the Contractor Salary Calculator for a gross billable-rate target.

Compensation values that need extra care

  • Bonus: use a realistic expected amount rather than the maximum possible payout.
  • Benefits: use the employer-paid annual value when it is known.
  • Retirement: enter the employer contribution, not your own contribution.
  • Equity: annualize multi-year grants so offers are easier to compare.

For salary-only planning, use Annual Income or Hourly to Salary. This page is intended for broader package comparisons.

Compensation Package FAQ

Is total compensation the same as take-home pay?

No. Total compensation is an estimated gross value, not net pay.
