Salary to contractor rate formula

Hourly rate = annual income target ÷ (billable hours per week × billable weeks per year)

A $90,000 target with 30 billable hours a week for 48 weeks requires about $62.50 per billable hour.

Why billable hours matter

Contractors may spend time on administration, proposals, training or unpaid time off. Using billable hours instead of total work hours gives a more useful gross-rate target.

How to calculate a contractor hourly rate from salary

Choose the gross annual income you want your billable work to produce, then estimate how many hours per week you can actually bill and how many weeks per year you expect to bill clients. The calculator divides the annual target by those billable hours to produce a gross hourly target.

This is different from converting an employee salary directly to an hourly wage. Contractors often have non-billable time for proposals, administration, client communication, training and business tasks. They may also have unpaid vacation or gaps between projects. Using realistic billable time helps avoid understating the hourly rate needed to reach an annual income goal.

Contractor rate example

Suppose your gross annual income target is $90,000. If you expect 30 billable hours each week for 48 weeks, that gives 1,440 billable hours for the year. Dividing $90,000 by 1,440 produces a target of about $62.50 per billable hour.

If the same annual target is spread across only 25 billable hours per week for 46 weeks, the required gross rate rises because there are fewer billable hours available. This is why the schedule assumptions matter as much as the annual target.

What this contractor calculator does not include

The result is a gross revenue-rate estimate. It does not add self-employment taxes, income taxes, insurance, equipment, software, unpaid administrative time, benefits, retirement contributions or other business costs. Those factors vary widely, so they should be evaluated separately before using the result as a quote or pricing decision.

For a simple employee-pay conversion, use Salary to Hourly or Hourly to Salary. To compare salary, bonuses and employer-paid benefits, use the Compensation Package Calculator.

Ways to test a contractor income target

  • Lower the billable weeks to allow for vacation, holidays or project gaps.
  • Lower billable hours if part of your week is spent on sales or administration.
  • Compare several annual income targets before deciding on a rate range.
  • Use the Annual Income Calculator when you want to model employee-style hourly earnings instead.

For planning, it is usually more useful to test a few realistic schedules than to assume every working hour can be billed to a client.

Calculator FAQ

How do I calculate contractor hourly rate from an annual target?

Divide the gross annual target by billable hours per week times billable weeks per year.

Are taxes and business costs deducted?

No. The result is a revenue rate before costs, not take-home income.

Why does less billable time increase the rate?

The same annual target must be earned across fewer invoiced hours.

What if billable hours or weeks are zero?

A positive denominator is required. Zero billable time cannot produce an hourly rate for an annual target.
