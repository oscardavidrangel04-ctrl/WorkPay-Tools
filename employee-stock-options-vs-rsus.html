Employee Stock Options vs RSUs: Basic Comparison

Compare employee stock options and restricted stock units at a high level, including vesting and why award documents matter.

The short answer

Stock options generally give an employee the right to buy company stock at a stated exercise price after conditions are met. Restricted stock units (RSUs) generally represent a promise to deliver shares or cash after vesting conditions are satisfied. The plan, grant agreement and company status determine the real terms.

What to check

Both can have value that changes with company performance and market conditions. Options can have no practical value if the share price is below the exercise price, while RSUs can be taxable or subject to withholding when they vest. This is not investment or tax advice.

Use the right record

Review the award type, number of units, vesting schedule, exercise window, expiration, tax treatment and liquidity restrictions. Do not compare headline grant values without those facts.

Official reference

Investor.gov provides education about employer-sponsored retirement and investment considerations; award documents and qualified tax or financial advisers are appropriate sources for individual equity decisions. Read the source.

Compare exercise price, vesting and settlement

An employee stock option gives a right to buy shares at the stated exercise price after applicable conditions. An RSU is generally a promise of shares or cash under award terms; it does not normally require the employee to pay an option-style exercise price. Both can have service or performance conditions. Vesting and delivery are not automatically identical events.

Worked example: option spread is not the whole share value

For 100 vested options with a $10 exercise price and a $15 assumed share value, exercise costs $1,000. The illustrative spread is $500 before taxes, fees or restrictions. For 100 RSUs settled in shares valued at $15, the gross share value is $1,500 before withholding. These awards are not equivalent simply because both mention 100 units.

Keep tax and liquidity assumptions separate

Options can have different tax classifications, and award-specific settlement conditions matter for RSUs. Avoid assuming one tax event or a guaranteed sale price applies to all awards. See IRS stock-option guidance and Publication 525 for tax-treatment details. The vested equity calculator is not an option-profit calculator because it does not deduct exercise cost.

Frequently asked questions

Do stock options and RSUs both require an exercise payment?

Options generally require payment of the exercise price to acquire shares. RSUs do not normally have that option-style purchase requirement.

Can an option be underwater?

Yes. Its exercise price can exceed the current share value.

Does vesting guarantee that private-company shares can be sold?

No. Restrictions and liquidity can prevent or limit a sale.

Does this guide calculate my equity taxes?

No. Tax treatment depends on the award, events and individual circumstances.
