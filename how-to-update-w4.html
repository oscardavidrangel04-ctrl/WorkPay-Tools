How to Update a W-4: When to Review Withholding

Learn when a W-4 review may be useful, what the form controls, and where to use the official IRS withholding estimator.

The short answer

Form W-4 tells an employer how to withhold federal income tax from an employee’s pay. It is not a tax return and it does not set every deduction on a paycheck. Your employer uses the form together with pay information and applicable withholding methods.

Recognize changes that warrant a withholding review

A review can make sense after changes such as starting or ending a job, changes in household status, a dependent, a second job, significant investment income or a change in deductions or credits. The form should be completed from the current IRS instructions, not from a generic social-media rule.

Use the right record

Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator to review a situation, then provide a new completed W-4 to the employer if you choose to change withholding. Keep a copy for your records. This guide is educational and cannot determine your individual tax result.

Official reference

The IRS recommends checking withholding when personal or financial circumstances change and directs employees to Form W-4 and its estimator. Read the source.

Steps for submitting a new W-4

  1. Gather recent pay stubs and the other records requested by the IRS estimator.
  2. Use current IRS instructions or the estimator to review your circumstances.
  3. Complete the form and submit it through your employer's payroll process.
  4. Keep a copy and ask payroll when the update will be reflected.
  5. Review a later pay stub to confirm the change was processed.

The estimator does not send an election to your employer for you.

Source: IRS Tax Withholding Estimator.

Frequently asked questions

Do I send my updated W-4 to the IRS?

Give the completed form to your employer through its payroll process, not to the IRS. Keep a copy of what you submitted.

Does updating my W-4 change every paycheck deduction?

No. Form W-4 concerns federal income-tax withholding. Benefit deductions and state withholding may require separate forms or elections.

Does the IRS estimator automatically update payroll?

No. You still need to submit the completed W-4 to your employer if you decide to change withholding.
