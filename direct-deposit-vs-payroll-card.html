Direct Deposit vs Payroll Card: What to Compare

Compare direct deposit and payroll cards at a high level, including access, fees and the payment choices to ask an employer about.

The short answer

Direct deposit sends wages to a bank or credit-union account selected by the employee. A payroll card is a prepaid card an employer uses to deliver wages. The available methods and protections can depend on federal and state rules, employer practice and the financial provider.

Compare fees and access to your wages

Compare access to cash, account fees, ATM networks, balance checks, replacement-card costs and how to receive statements. Do not assume one method is required solely because it is the default offered by an employer. Keep copies of enrollment and pay records.

Use the right record

If a payroll card or direct-deposit arrangement is unclear, request the fee schedule and payment options in writing. Consumer protections and state rules may differ.

Official reference

The CFPB says an employer cannot require payment on a payroll card and must offer at least one alternative; it does not mean every employer must offer two electronic methods. Read the source.

Payroll EFT vs a pay card: what the label tells you

EFT means electronic funds transfer. A payroll screen may use it for direct deposit, but the label alone does not identify the account receiving your wages. A pay card can also receive wages electronically. Confirm the destination, account provider and fee schedule.

Compare the costs of the activities you actually expect to use: withdrawing cash, transferring money, checking balances and replacing a lost card. A provider name alone does not establish whether those services are free.

Before switching your wage-payment account

Ask payroll when the change takes effect, how to verify the new details and where the next paycheck will go. Keep access to the previous account until you confirm any final deposit. The CFPB explains payroll-card payment choices.

Questions to ask before choosing a payment method

  1. Request every available wage-payment option in writing.
  2. Check the cost of the withdrawals, transfers and balance checks you expect to use.
  3. Ask how to access pay records and what happens if the card is lost.
  4. Confirm when a change of payment method will take effect.

Source: CFPB: payroll card choice.

Frequently asked questions

Can my employer require me to accept a payroll card?

No. The CFPB says an employer cannot require payment on a payroll card and must offer another way to receive wages. Available alternatives and state requirements can vary.

Is a payroll card the same as direct deposit to my bank account?

No. A payroll card receives wages through a prepaid account; direct deposit to your bank or credit union uses the account you select. Compare the account terms and access arrangements.

Which payroll card fees should I check?

Ask about ATM withdrawals, out-of-network access, transfers, balance inquiries, inactivity and replacement cards. Check the actual fee schedule rather than assuming every service is free.
