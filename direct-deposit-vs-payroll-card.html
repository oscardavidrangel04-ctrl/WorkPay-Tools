Direct Deposit vs Payroll Card: What to Compare

Compare direct deposit and payroll cards at a high level, including access, fees and the payment choices to ask an employer about.

The short answer

Direct deposit sends wages to a bank or credit-union account selected by the employee. A payroll card is a prepaid card an employer uses to deliver wages. The available methods and protections can depend on federal and state rules, employer practice and the financial provider.

What to check

Compare access to cash, account fees, ATM networks, balance checks, replacement-card costs and how to receive statements. Do not assume one method is required solely because it is the default offered by an employer. Keep copies of enrollment and pay records.

Use the right record

If a payroll card or direct-deposit arrangement is unclear, request the fee schedule and payment options in writing. Consumer protections and state rules may differ.

Official reference

The CFPB’s financial-empowerment materials state that workers paid electronically must be offered more than one electronic method, such as a payroll card or direct deposit to their own account. Read the source.
