Secured vs Unsecured Credit Card: Basic Difference

Learn the basic difference between secured and unsecured credit cards and the account terms worth comparing.

What the security deposit changes

A secured credit card generally requires a cash deposit that is connected to the credit line. An unsecured credit card generally does not require that security deposit. Both can have fees, interest rates, payment requirements and credit-reporting practices that need to be reviewed before opening an account.

Deposit return, upgrades and credit reporting

A deposit is not the same as a prepaid balance for spending. Check how the deposit is held, when it may be returned, whether the account can graduate to an unsecured product and which credit reporting companies receive account information.

Compare written fees and account terms

Compare the agreement, fees, APR, credit limit and deposit terms in writing. Opening several accounts quickly can affect credit applications; no product choice guarantees a score result.

If the offer lists a yearly charge, check when a credit card annual fee is charged as part of the cost comparison.

The security deposit does not pay the monthly bill

Suppose a secured card requires a $300 deposit and provides a $300 limit. If you charge $90, you have borrowed $90; you have not simply spent $90 out of a prepaid balance. You still need to follow the statement's payment instructions.

Compare the full cost of opening and keeping the account

Record the deposit separately from any annual or setup fees. A deposit may be returned under the agreement's conditions; a fee is a cost, not the same kind of refundable security. Ask when a deposit can be released and whether an upgrade requires a new application.

The FTC's card-type comparison distinguishes secured credit from prepaid and debit products. Before applying, check the minimum-payment rules, reporting practices and your ability to keep the deposit tied up.

Official reference

The CFPB notes that secured cards typically require a deposit equal to the credit limit and may be an option for people who do not qualify for a regular card. Read the source.

Frequently asked questions

Is a secured credit card a prepaid card?

No. A secured card is a credit account backed by a deposit. A prepaid card generally lets you spend loaded funds rather than borrow under a credit-card agreement.

Does a larger deposit guarantee a higher credit score?

No. A deposit is not a score purchase. Reporting, payment behavior, balances and the rest of the credit file matter.
