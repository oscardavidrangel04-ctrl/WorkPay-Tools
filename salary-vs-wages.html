Salary vs Wages: How the Pay Terms Differ

Understand how salary and wages are commonly used, how they relate to pay schedules, and why the offer or payroll record controls.

The short answer

Salary commonly refers to a fixed amount paid for a role over a stated period, often quoted annually. Wages commonly refer to pay based on hours worked or another unit of work. In everyday use, employers may use the terms broadly, so the written offer, pay rate and payroll records are more reliable than the label alone.

What to check

A salaried worker may still have overtime rights depending on classification and applicable law. An hourly wage can produce varying gross pay when hours change. Compare the actual pay frequency, expected hours, overtime rules and benefit eligibility rather than relying only on salary versus wages.
