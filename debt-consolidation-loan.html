What Is a Debt Consolidation Loan?

A debt consolidation loan can replace multiple balances with one installment loan, but it does not erase the underlying cost or payment obligation.

The short answer

Compare APR, origination fees, loan term, total interest, collateral requirements and whether old revolving accounts remain open.

Compare the total repayment, not just the payment

For an illustrative offer of 36 payments of $250, scheduled payments total $9,000. Add a separately paid $200 origination fee to get $9,200 in scheduled outlay. Do not add a financed fee again if it is already included in those payments. These invented figures are a comparison method, not a loan quote.

Before replacing existing debts

  1. Collect current payoff amounts, not only the latest statement balances.
  2. Check whether net loan proceeds cover those payoffs after fees.
  3. Compare APR, payment count and total repayment on the same basis.
  4. Confirm when old accounts are paid and check for residual amounts.

Consolidation replaces debts with a new obligation; a lower payment can come from a longer term rather than lower cost.

Reference: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau guidance.

When reviewing income alongside required payments, use debt-to-income ratio to distinguish that measure from an interest rate or credit score.
