Debt Validation Letter: What It Is and How to Request One

Understand a debt validation notice, how to request verification from a collector, and how this differs from a credit report dispute.

The short answer

A validation notice comes from the collector and identifies the debt it seeks to collect. A debt validation letter commonly means your written dispute or request for verification. Keep these two documents separate so you can identify the claim, your response and the applicable deadline.

What the notice should tell you

Look for the collector and creditor names, account reference, current amount, itemized interest, fees, payments and credits, and the date to dispute by. Save the notice and envelope or electronic message. The CFPB explains the required validation information.

How to write and send a debt validation letter

  1. Match the notice. Include the date, your name, the collector's name and the account reference it provided.
  2. Identify what you dispute. State whether you question the whole debt or a particular amount. Describe a missing payment, unfamiliar account or unclear charge accurately.
  3. Request verification. Ask for clarification of the disputed claim. If the original creditor is unclear, request its name and address.
  4. Use the notice's response instructions. Send the written request to the listed address or an electronic channel the collector accepts. Avoid including unrelated sensitive information.
  5. Keep a record. Save your request, relevant supporting copies, the delivery or submission confirmation and the collector's response.

Make an amount dispute specific

For example, a notice shows $850, while your records show a $250 payment that is absent from its itemization. Identify that payment and its date, attach a copy of the receipt and ask how the collector calculated the balance. Do not assume the remaining amount is correct until you compare any other charges or credits.

Check the validation deadline

Use the dispute-by date on the notice. Under the federal rule, a timely written dispute generally requires the collector to stop collecting the disputed debt until it sends verification or a judgment copy. Special rules apply to duplicate disputes. A request sent later does not automatically have the same effect. See CFPB Regulation F, section 1006.38.

Debt validation letter for medical bills

Compare the provider, dates of service, insurance payments or adjustments, and the amount the collector claims remains due. Ask about a specific mismatch rather than sending a full medical history. Keep originals of your records and provide only relevant copies.

Validation is not a credit-report dispute

A collector's claim and a bureau's account entry are separate records. If the report contains an error, use the credit-report dispute letter guide to identify that error and its evidence. Validation does not automatically delete accurate information or guarantee a score change.

Frequently asked questions

Is the validation notice the same as my dispute letter?

No. The collector provides the validation notice. Your written dispute or verification request is a response that identifies what you question about that debt.

Does a validation request remove a collection from my credit report?

No. Validation concerns the collector’s claim. An inaccurate credit-report entry requires a separate dispute, and neither process automatically deletes accurate information.

Can I send the request electronically?

An electronic request can count as writing when you use a medium through which the collector accepts consumer communications. Follow the notice’s response instructions and save the submission confirmation.
