Debt Validation Letter: What It Is and How to Request One

Understand a debt validation notice, how to request verification from a collector, and how this differs from a credit report dispute.

The short answer

A debt validation notice identifies a debt a collector is trying to collect. If the amount, creditor or account is unfamiliar, use the notice to ask specific questions before deciding what to do.

What the notice should tell you

Look for the collector and creditor names, account information, the current amount, an itemization of interest, fees, payments and credits, and the deadline to dispute. Save the notice and envelope or electronic message.

How to request verification

Identify the debt and explain which amount or detail you dispute. Send a written request to the collector using the address or method on its notice, and keep a copy plus proof of delivery. The CFPB explains that a written dispute within the notice’s 30-day period generally pauses collection of the disputed amount until the collector responds.

Validation is not a credit-report dispute

A request to a collector concerns its attempt to collect the debt. If the same account is reported inaccurately, you can separately dispute the specific error with the credit bureau and furnisher. Neither process automatically removes accurate information.

How to send a debt validation letter

Use the address or response method shown on the collector's notice. Identify the account, state the specific amount or information you dispute, and request verification or original-creditor information as appropriate. Keep a dated copy and proof of delivery. A written dispute sent within the validation period—normally 30 days after you receive the notice—has a different effect from a later request; check the CFPB's current explanation and the deadline on your notice.

Debt validation letter for medical bills

Confirm the provider, dates of service, insurance payments or adjustments, and the amount the collector claims remains due. Do not send original medical records or sensitive information unnecessarily. If the credit report also shows inaccurate medical-debt information, the report dispute is a separate step.

How to write a debt validation letter

Start with your name, the collector’s name, the account reference from its notice and the date. State exactly what you dispute: for example, the account is not yours, a payment is missing or the amount is unclear. Ask for verification or the original creditor’s information as appropriate. Keep the request factual and concise; do not include sensitive details that the collector does not need to identify the account.

Send the request using the address or method on the validation notice, retain a copy and record the delivery date. A letter to the collector is different from a credit-report dispute letter. Review the CFPB validation guidance for the notice contents and dispute period.
