Credit card basics

What Is a Credit Card Minimum Payment?

A credit card minimum payment is the smallest amount your issuer requires by the due date on that statement. Its formula can include a percentage of the balance, interest, fees and past-due amounts. Paying it on time meets that payment requirement, but usually leaves a balance and may not avoid interest.

Quick answer: minimum payment vs. statement balance

Your minimum payment is the required amount due for the billing cycle. Your statement balance is the balance shown when that statement closed. Paying only the minimum may prevent a late payment when made on time, but any remaining balance can continue to accrue interest under the account terms.

See minimum payment vs. statement balance for a side-by-side explanation.

How to find the minimum payment on a credit card

  1. Open the latest issued statement in your issuer's app or website. Confirm the statement date and account.
  2. Find minimum payment due and the matching payment due date in the payment summary. Do not substitute the current balance.
  3. If you already paid after the statement closed, check the account's remaining payment requirement and whether that payment has posted.
  4. Review any scheduled automatic payment before sending another payment. Ask the issuer about conflicting figures or pending payments.

For example, a statement might show a $900 balance, a $35 minimum and a November 12 due date. The $35 is the required minimum for that bill; it is not the amount that clears the $900 balance. These figures illustrate where to look, not an issuer's payment formula.

How is a credit card minimum payment calculated?

There is no single formula for every card. An issuer may base the amount on a percentage of the balance, interest and fees, a fixed floor, a past-due amount, or a combination of these items. The exact calculation is in your card agreement and on your monthly statement.

  1. Find the line labeled “minimum payment due” on the statement.
  2. Check the due date and payment instructions.
  3. Review interest, fees, a past-due balance, or promotional terms that may affect the amount.

Use the actual statement amount rather than estimating from a generic percentage. The credit card minimum payment calculator can illustrate a scenario, but it does not replace issuer terms. If you pay more than the minimum across balances with different APRs, see how the extra payment is generally allocated.

Credit card minimum payment formula: worked examples

Two possible methods are a percentage of the statement balance or a percentage of a defined balance plus interest and fees. A minimum dollar amount may also apply. These are hypothetical formulas for learning, not the terms of a particular issuer.

Two illustrative rules for the same $2,000 starting balance
Assumed ruleCalculationMinimum
3% of balance, with a $35 floorGreater of $2,000 × 3% or $35$60
1% of balance plus $40 interest, with a $35 floorGreater of $20 + $40 or $35$60

Both examples assume no fees or overdue amounts. Under the second rule, $40 covers the assumed interest and $20 reduces the starting balance. The formulas can produce different results when the interest or balance changes. If the full amount owed is smaller than the dollar floor, the agreement generally calls for that smaller payoff amount.

What would the minimum be on a $5,000 balance?

Under the illustrative 3% rule, it would be $150. Under a 1%-plus-interest rule, it would be $50 plus the actual interest and applicable fees, subject to the floor. A $5,000 balance alone does not determine the bill. Use your statement's formula and the minimum payment calculator to explore an estimate.

What happens if you pay only the minimum?

The account may remain in good standing for that cycle if the minimum arrives by the due date. However, the unpaid portion of the statement balance can carry forward. When the account does not qualify for a grace period, interest may apply according to the agreement.

A smaller required payment can make the monthly bill easier to manage, but it can also extend payoff time. Compare the minimum with a higher payment using the credit card payoff calculator.

Does paying the minimum affect your credit?

Meeting the required payment on time helps protect your payment history. It does not necessarily bring down a high balance quickly. The balance that gets reported relative to your limit can still affect credit utilization. Paying interest is not required to build credit.

A fixed payment above the minimum can repay debt faster, assuming no new charges and the same interest rate. Review the repayment warning on your statement, then use the payoff calculator to compare affordable amounts.

Is there an average minimum payment you can use for budgeting?

A generic average will not tell you what your account requires. A percentage-based rule can produce a different bill from a rule that adds interest and fees, even on the same balance. A dollar floor or past-due amount can change the result again.

Build a budget from your statement

  1. Record the actual minimum due and due date from your latest statement.
  2. Compare it with the previous statement and look for changes in balance, interest, fees or overdue amounts.
  3. Choose an affordable payment that meets at least the current requirement. Recheck it each month even if you use a fixed automatic payment.

For example, a fixed $50 payment is enough for a $35 minimum, but falls $15 short if the next statement requires $65. The shortfall matters even though you paid the same amount as last month. Review autopay amount settings before relying on a fixed debit.

Why can the minimum payment change?

A higher minimum does not always mean the issuer changed its formula. It can reflect a higher balance, new interest charges, fees, a missed prior payment, or the end of a promotional arrangement. A lower balance can also change the calculation. Review the current statement before assuming the cause.

Minimum payment, current balance, and due date

TermWhat it tells you
Minimum paymentThe least amount required by the due date for that statement period.
Statement balanceThe balance recorded at the end of the billing cycle.
Current balanceThe balance after activity posted since the statement closed.
Due dateThe deadline for the required payment for that statement.

New activity after the statement date can make the current balance different from the amount due. Read statement balance vs. current balance before choosing a payment amount.

What if you cannot afford the minimum payment?

Contact your issuer before the due date if possible. Explain how much you can pay and ask about hardship assistance, a temporary payment arrangement or a due-date change. Confirm the amount, dates, interest treatment and credit-reporting consequences before relying on an arrangement.

Paying less than the required minimum does not satisfy the bill unless the issuer has agreed to different terms. The CFPB explains what to do when you cannot pay.

Ways to pay more than the minimum

  • Set a fixed amount above the required minimum.
  • Make an additional payment after a large purchase, if it fits your budget.
  • Use credit card autopay for at least the minimum and review the account before each due date.
  • Check payment-allocation rules if you have a promotional balance.

For a broader repayment plan, see how to pay off credit card debt.

Check the disclosure on your statement

U.S. credit card statements generally include payment information intended to show how minimum-only payments can affect payoff time. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that the applicable account terms and disclosures control. If a number on your statement is unclear, the issuer can explain your account-specific calculation.

Credit card minimum payment FAQ

Can I pay more than the minimum payment?

Yes. Check the statement and agreement if you have multiple balances or a promotional rate.

Does the minimum payment stop interest?

Not necessarily. Interest treatment depends on the balance, grace period, transaction type, and card agreement.

Why is my minimum payment due higher this month?

Review the statement for balance, interest, fees, past-due amounts, or promotional-term changes.

Is the current balance the same as the minimum due?

No. The current balance can include transactions posted after the statement closed.

Is there one minimum payment percentage for every Visa or Mastercard?

No. The card issuer and account agreement determine the payment formula; the payment network name alone does not tell you the minimum.

Does a 0% APR offer mean there is no minimum payment?

No. A promotional interest rate does not generally remove the monthly payment requirement. Check your statement for the minimum due and the terms of the promotion.

How much more than the minimum payment should I pay?

There is no single extra amount for everyone. Review your budget and the repayment estimate on your statement. An affordable fixed payment above the minimum can reduce repayment time when the rate stays the same and you add no new charges.

Is the minimum payment a fixed amount every month?

Not necessarily. The amount may change with the balance, interest, fees, overdue amounts and issuer formula. Check each new statement rather than assuming last month’s payment is enough.
