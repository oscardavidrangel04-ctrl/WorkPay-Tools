Credit card basics

What Is a Credit Card Minimum Payment?

The minimum payment is the least amount your issuer requires by the due date. It keeps the account current, but it is usually not the same as paying the statement balance in full.

Quick answer: minimum payment vs. statement balance

Your minimum payment is the required amount due for the billing cycle. Your statement balance is the balance shown when that statement closed. Paying only the minimum may prevent a late payment when made on time, but any remaining balance can continue to accrue interest under the account terms.

See minimum payment vs. statement balance for a side-by-side explanation.

How is a credit card minimum payment calculated?

There is no single formula for every card. An issuer may base the amount on a percentage of the balance, interest and fees, a fixed floor, a past-due amount, or a combination of these items. The exact calculation is in your card agreement and on your monthly statement.

  1. Find the line labeled “minimum payment due” on the statement.
  2. Check the due date and payment instructions.
  3. Review interest, fees, a past-due balance, or promotional terms that may affect the amount.

Use the actual statement amount rather than estimating from a generic percentage. The credit card minimum payment calculator can illustrate a scenario, but it does not replace issuer terms.

What happens if you pay only the minimum?

The account may remain in good standing for that cycle if the minimum arrives by the due date. However, the unpaid portion of the statement balance can carry forward. When the account does not qualify for a grace period, interest may apply according to the agreement.

A smaller required payment can make the monthly bill easier to manage, but it can also extend payoff time. Compare the minimum with a higher payment using the credit card payoff calculator.

Why can the minimum payment change?

A higher minimum does not always mean the issuer changed its formula. It can reflect a higher balance, new interest charges, fees, a missed prior payment, or the end of a promotional arrangement. A lower balance can also change the calculation. Review the current statement before assuming the cause.

Minimum payment, current balance, and due date

TermWhat it tells you
Minimum paymentThe least amount required by the due date for that statement period.
Statement balanceThe balance recorded at the end of the billing cycle.
Current balanceThe balance after activity posted since the statement closed.
Due dateThe deadline for the required payment for that statement.

New activity after the statement date can make the current balance different from the amount due. Read statement balance vs. current balance before choosing a payment amount.

Ways to pay more than the minimum

  • Set a fixed amount above the required minimum.
  • Make an additional payment after a large purchase, if it fits your budget.
  • Use credit card autopay for at least the minimum and review the account before each due date.
  • Check payment-allocation rules if you have a promotional balance.

Check the disclosure on your statement

U.S. credit card statements generally include payment information intended to show how minimum-only payments can affect payoff time. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that the applicable account terms and disclosures control. If a number on your statement is unclear, the issuer can explain your account-specific calculation.

Credit card minimum payment FAQ

Can I pay more than the minimum payment?

Yes. Check the statement and agreement if you have multiple balances or a promotional rate.

Does the minimum payment stop interest?

Not necessarily. Interest treatment depends on the balance, grace period, transaction type, and card agreement.

Why is my minimum payment due higher this month?

Review the statement for balance, interest, fees, past-due amounts, or promotional-term changes.

Is the current balance the same as the minimum due?

No. The current balance can include transactions posted after the statement closed.
