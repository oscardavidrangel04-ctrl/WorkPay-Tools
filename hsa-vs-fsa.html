HSA vs FSA: Basic Differences to Check

Understand the basic differences between an HSA and FSA, including eligibility and ownership questions to verify with official materials.

The short answer

A health savings account (HSA) and flexible spending arrangement (FSA) can both help pay eligible health expenses, but they have different eligibility, contribution and ownership rules. An HSA requires eligibility tied to a qualifying high-deductible health plan and other conditions. An FSA is typically an employer-sponsored arrangement.

Check eligibility and unused-balance rules

Do not assume unused money follows the same rule in each account. Carryover, grace-period and forfeiture rules can vary, and HSA ownership differs from many employer arrangements. Read the plan materials before selecting a contribution amount.

HSA and FSA payroll-tax treatment

Do you pay FICA taxes on HSA contributions?

The contribution method matters. An eligible HSA contribution made through a qualifying Section 125 cafeteria-plan salary reduction generally avoids federal income-tax withholding and employee Social Security and Medicare taxes. A payroll deduction outside that arrangement does not automatically get the same treatment.

A qualifying contribution you make directly to an HSA may be deductible on your federal income-tax return. That deduction does not reverse Social Security or Medicare tax already due on the wages used to fund it. Confirm eligibility and limits before contributing.

Compare the same $1,000 contribution

For an employee whose covered wages remain below the Social Security wage base and who is not subject to Additional Medicare tax, excluding $1,000 through the qualifying payroll arrangement would reduce employee FICA by $76.50: $62 Social Security plus $14.50 Medicare. A direct contribution does not produce that payroll-tax reduction. Federal income-tax savings and state treatment are separate calculations.

A health FSA funded through a qualifying salary-reduction arrangement generally also reduces the wages subject to those taxes, but its unused-fund rules differ from an HSA's. See what FICA covers and confirm the deduction type with payroll. Sources: IRS Publication 15 and Publication 969.

Official reference

The IRS explains the eligibility and rules for health savings accounts and flexible spending arrangements in its current publications. Read the source.

Unused funds and job changes

Unused HSA funds remain in the account, and the HSA stays with you when you change employers. A health FSA generally has use-it-or-lose-it rules, although a plan can offer a permitted carryover or a grace period. Check which provision, if any, your plan offers.

Before enrolling, also check whether other health coverage affects HSA eligibility. A general-purpose health FSA can prevent HSA contributions; limited-purpose arrangements can work differently.

Source: IRS Publication 969: HSA and FSA rules.

Frequently asked questions

Do I lose unused HSA money at the end of the year?

No. Unused HSA money stays in the account. Eligibility to make new contributions is a separate question.

Does every health FSA allow a carryover?

No. Carryover or grace-period provisions depend on the plan and applicable limits. Read your plan deadlines rather than assuming unused funds remain available.

Can I contribute to an HSA while covered by an FSA?

A general-purpose health FSA can make you ineligible to contribute to an HSA. Certain limited-purpose or other permitted arrangements differ; confirm the coverage with the plan administrator.
