Wrong Name on a Credit Report: How to Correct It

A wrong name, middle initial, suffix or other identity detail can be a report error. Compare the whole file before deciding whether it is only a typo or a sign that another person’s information was mixed into your report.

The short answer

Document the name shown, the report date and the bureau that displayed it. Dispute the inaccurate identity information clearly with the reporting company, and retain a copy of your report and supporting identification. If unfamiliar accounts appear too, address those account entries separately.

Check whether the issue is a typo or a mixed file

A misspelled first name or an outdated suffix can be an identity-data error. More serious signs include accounts, addresses or phone numbers that do not belong to you, especially when the other person has a similar name. The CFPB lists wrong names and mixed consumer files among common identity errors to review.

How to dispute a wrong name

Mark the exact identity field that is wrong and explain the correction requested. Include copies—not originals—of documents that support the correction, such as government-issued identification or a document showing your current legal name. Send the dispute using the credit reporting company’s current process and retain dates, copies and confirmation numbers.

When unfamiliar accounts are also present

Do not assume that a wrong name automatically makes every account inaccurate. Review account numbers, creditor names, dates and balances. Dispute each inaccurate item specifically. If you think identity theft caused the issue, use the federal recovery process and follow the reporting company’s directions.

Review the outcome

After the investigation, compare the updated report with your original copy. If the identity field or related account remains wrong, preserve the response and follow the official dispute guidance for next steps. A correction is not a prediction of a particular credit-score result.

Official references

The CFPB lists identity errors, including a wrong name, and explains how to dispute inaccurate credit-report information. This page is educational, not individualized legal or financial advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I dispute a wrong name on my credit report?

Yes. Identify the exact name variation or identity field you believe is wrong, then submit a factual dispute to the reporting company and, where relevant, the company that supplied the information.

Does a different spelling mean an account is not mine?

Not necessarily. Compare account details and your records. A similar name can be a reporting error, but an unfamiliar account may require a separate dispute or identity-theft response.

How long does a credit report dispute take?

A credit reporting company generally must investigate a dispute within 30 days after receiving it, subject to the applicable rules and circumstances.
