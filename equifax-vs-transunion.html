Equifax vs TransUnion: What Is the Difference?

Compare Equifax vs TransUnion, why their credit reports and scores can differ, and what to check when information does not match.

The short answer

Equifax and TransUnion are separate nationwide credit reporting companies. They can receive different information, receive the same information on different dates, and display report details in different formats. A difference does not automatically mean one report is wrong.

Why Equifax and TransUnion reports can differ

A creditor is not required to furnish identical information to every bureau. Reporting schedules, account updates, inquiries and public-record handling can also create timing differences. Compare the account name, balance, status and reported date before drawing a conclusion.

Why credit scores can differ

A score depends on the report data, the scoring model and the date it was calculated. Two scores labeled with different models or generated on different dates are not direct equivalents, even when they use data from the same bureau.

What to do when one report looks wrong

Obtain the reports you are entitled to review, identify the exact item that differs and check it against statements or account records. If information is inaccurate, use the bureau dispute process and keep copies of the report, evidence and response.

Official reference

Review the current guidance from Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This page is educational and does not provide legal advice or predict a credit score.

Frequently asked questions

Is Equifax more accurate than TransUnion?

Neither bureau is universally more accurate. Accuracy depends on the information furnished and maintained in the individual report.

Why is my TransUnion score lower than Equifax?

The underlying report data, score model or calculation date may differ. Compare the reports and score disclosures before attributing the difference to one factor.

Does checking my own report hurt my score?

Requesting your own credit report is generally treated as a soft inquiry and does not reduce a credit score.
