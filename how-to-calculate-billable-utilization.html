How to Calculate Billable Utilization

Understand the relationship between billable hours and total working hours.

The basic method

Billable utilization divides billable hours by total working hours. It is a planning metric; contracts, unpaid work and business costs determine the real economics.

Choose a consistent denominator

Suppose a time record shows 30 billable hours out of 40 available hours. The calculation is 30 ÷ 40 × 100 = 75%. If your reporting policy excludes five hours of leave from availability, the same 30 billable hours divided by 35 available hours gives about 85.7%. These are different definitions, not an increase in work delivered.

Utilization is not profit

At an illustrative billing rate of $100 per hour, 30 billable hours represent $3,000 before discounts, collection issues and costs. The 75% utilization figure alone cannot tell you profit or cash received.

  • Use the same date range for both hour totals.
  • Define how leave, training and administration are treated.
  • Do not calculate a percentage with zero available hours; report it as not applicable.

Simple example

If 30 of 40 weekly work hours are billable, utilization is 75%. Track proposals, administration and client work consistently so the ratio remains meaningful.

Question to check

Is a high rate always better?

Not automatically. Sustainable utilization depends on business needs, pricing and non-billable operations.
