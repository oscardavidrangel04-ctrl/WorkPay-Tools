Why Did My Credit Score Drop? Common Reasons

A credit score can change when report information changes or a model receives a different account snapshot.

Common report changes behind a score drop

Reported balances, payment history, new accounts, inquiries and corrected report information can affect a score.

Compare account data and score dates

Compare report dates, balances, payment status, account openings, limit changes and inquiries. If a recent application appears, review how long hard inquiries stay on a credit report and separate report visibility from score impact. Different services can use different models or update dates.

Compare two equivalent score snapshots first

A score of 720 from one service and 700 from another does not by itself prove a 20-point decline. Record the model, bureau and calculation date for both. Different inputs can produce different numbers even when neither display is wrong.

Check the underlying changes in a consistent order

  1. Compare reported card balances and limits.
  2. Review payment status and any newly reported overdue amount.
  3. Check new accounts, inquiries and account closures.
  4. Look for corrected, missing or unfamiliar entries.

For example, a $600 reported balance uses 20% of a $3,000 limit but 40% of a $1,500 limit. The balance stayed the same while utilization changed. This explains a possible factor, not a predictable number of points. Use the utilization formula and investigate any specific reporting error.

Why a score can drop after a dispute or a lower balance

After a credit-report dispute

A dispute is a request to investigate information, not a score-improvement service. Compare the investigation result with both the earlier and updated report. Ask what actually changed: a balance, status, date, account entry or another field. A score displayed afterward may also reflect unrelated changes reported during the investigation.

Experian's dispute explanation distinguishes the dispute from any scoring effect of updated information. If a correction is still wrong, focus on the supporting evidence rather than the number of points lost.

When your balance decreased

Check whether the lower balance has reached the report used for that score. Then compare other accounts and any limit change. Paying down one card does not mean every input in the credit file improved at the same time.

A 20-point drop and a 100-point drop are not diagnoses. There is no reliable lookup table that identifies the cause from the size of a decline. Use the same model, bureau and dates, and review unfamiliar activity promptly. You do not need to carry interest-bearing debt to investigate a score change.

Why would my FICO score drop?

A FICO score can change when the report data used by the model changes, when balances or account status update, or when a different score version or bureau file is displayed. A point change alone does not identify the cause. Compare the score model, bureau, calculation date and recent report changes.

Related questions

Why did my FICO score drop when nothing changed?

A lender or issuer may have reported new data even if you made no new transaction. The displayed score may also use a different model, bureau or date.

Can checking my own score make it drop?

Checking your own report or score is generally a soft inquiry. A lender application can create a hard inquiry, depending on the transaction.

Why could a score fall even though I made a payment?

The displayed score may use a report snapshot from before the payment, or another account factor may have changed. Compare reporting and calculation dates.

Can I identify the cause from the number of points lost?

Not reliably. A point change alone does not identify one event. Compare the score inputs and report changes instead.

Can a credit score drop for no apparent reason?

The reason may not appear in a short alert. Compare the full reports and score details for reporting updates, timing differences or other account changes.

Does disputing an error guarantee that my score rises?

No. The aim is an accurate report. A scoring result depends on the resulting information, the model and the rest of the file.
