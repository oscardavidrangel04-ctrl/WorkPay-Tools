What Is Tax Withholding From a Paycheck?

Learn what tax withholding means, what may affect it, and when to review your Form W-4 with official IRS resources.

The short answer

Tax withholding is money an employer takes from an employee’s pay and sends to tax authorities. Federal income tax withholding depends in part on earnings and the information the employee provides on Form W-4. Employment taxes can also include Social Security and Medicare taxes. State and local rules may add other withholding.

What to check

Withholding is not the same as final tax owed for the year. A refund or balance due can depend on total income, credits, deductions and payments. A pay stub shows what was withheld for that check, while a Form W-2 reports annual wages and withholding.

Use the right record

Review withholding after a life or income change, such as a second job, marriage, a new dependent or significant non-wage income. Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator and submit a new W-4 to your employer if you decide a change is appropriate.

Official reference

The IRS says employee federal withholding depends on earnings and W-4 information and offers its estimator for a withholding check. Read the source.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check about what is tax withholding from a paycheck?

Start with the written employer document, current pay stub or plan materials. Labels and rules can vary by employer.
