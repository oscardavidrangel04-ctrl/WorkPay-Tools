Pay terminology

What Is Gross Pay?

Gross pay is the amount earned before taxes, benefits and other payroll deductions are subtracted. It can refer to one paycheck or to earnings across a longer period.

Reviewed September 13, 2026

Gross pay formula

For an hourly employee with no premium pay:

Gross pay = hourly rate × hours worked

At $25 per hour for 40 hours, gross weekly pay is $1,000. If overtime applies, calculate straight-time and premium earnings separately. The Weekly Pay Calculator shows that breakdown.

Gross pay for salaried work

A basic per-paycheck estimate divides annual salary by the number of pay periods. A $62,400 salary paid biweekly produces a simple gross estimate of $2,400 per paycheck because $62,400 ÷ 26 = $2,400. Bonuses, commissions, unpaid time and payroll timing can change the actual figure.

What may be included

  • Regular wages or salary for the period
  • Overtime or other premium pay
  • Bonuses, commissions or shift differentials when applicable
  • Other taxable earnings reported through payroll

The exact treatment depends on the employer, pay statement and purpose of the calculation.

Gross pay vs net pay

Gross pay is the starting earnings amount. Net pay is what remains after taxes and deductions. A calculator that reports gross pay does not predict the amount deposited into a bank account.

TermMeaning
Gross payEarnings before deductions
Net payAmount after deductions
Annual incomeIncome measured over one year under the definition being used

Gross pay FAQ

Is gross pay before or after taxes?

Before taxes and other payroll deductions.

How do I calculate hourly gross pay?

Multiply rate by straight-time hours, then add overtime, bonuses or other included earnings.

Is it the same as annual income?

Gross annual pay may be part of annual income, but annual income can include other sources depending on the definition.

From gross pay to taxable income

Payroll gross pay is not automatically the same as federal taxable wages, adjusted gross income or taxable income. See adjusted gross income vs taxable income for the federal return sequence.

Check the same pay period

Compare earnings and deductions for the same paycheck, not a year-to-date total with a single deposit. Keep employer-paid benefits separate from cash earnings and check the statement if reimbursements or split deposits make the bank amount different.
