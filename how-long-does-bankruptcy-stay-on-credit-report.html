How Long Does Bankruptcy Stay on a Credit Report?

A bankruptcy can generally be reported for up to 10 years. Chapter 13 entries are often removed after seven years under bureau practices, while Chapter 7 may remain for 10 years.

Chapter 7 vs Chapter 13 on a credit report

Federal law allows bankruptcy information to remain for up to 10 years. Consumer guidance commonly describes Chapter 7 as 10 years and Chapter 13 as seven years because bureaus often remove Chapter 13 earlier. Do not assume that a universal seven-year limit applies to every Chapter 13 report; check the report and current bureau policy.

Which date starts the period?

Review the filing or order date shown in the public-record section and compare it with court records. Individual accounts included in bankruptcy can have separate reporting timelines based on their own history.

Bankruptcy filing versus its account entries

The public-record bankruptcy entry and each creditor account are distinct report items. A discharge does not require every related account to vanish. Check balances, status and dates separately.

If the reporting is wrong

Gather the report and court documents, identify the exact field that is inaccurate, and dispute it with the bureau and furnishing company when applicable. Do not pay anyone who promises to erase an accurate bankruptcy entry.

Official reference

Check current guidance from Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. This page is educational and cannot determine an individual credit score or legal outcome.

Frequently asked questions

How long does Chapter 7 bankruptcy stay on a credit report?

It may appear for up to 10 years under federal reporting rules.

How long does Chapter 13 bankruptcy stay on a credit report?

Federal law allows up to 10 years, though bureaus often remove Chapter 13 entries after seven years.

Do included debts disappear with the bankruptcy entry?

No. Each account and the bankruptcy public record are separate items with their own reporting details.
