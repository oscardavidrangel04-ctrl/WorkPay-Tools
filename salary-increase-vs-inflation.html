Salary Increase vs. Inflation

Learn the difference between a nominal pay raise and a real increase in purchasing power, with a simple comparison formula.

Reviewed September 13, 2026

Nominal raise and real purchasing power

A nominal raise is the percentage printed in a pay offer. A real raise adjusts that change for inflation.

Approximate real change = salary raise % − inflation %

For a more precise comparison: ((1 + raise rate) ÷ (1 + inflation rate) − 1) × 100.

Example: 5% raise with 3% inflation

The quick estimate is about 2%. The compound formula gives approximately 1.94%. This does not mean every personal expense rose by exactly 3%; individual budgets can differ.

What else changes compensation?

Health benefits, employer retirement contributions, bonuses, paid time off and schedule changes can matter alongside salary. Use the Compensation Package Calculator for a broader comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Does a raise above inflation guarantee more take-home pay?

Not necessarily. Taxes, deductions, benefits and personal expenses can change the result.

What is a nominal salary increase?

It is the stated percentage increase before adjusting for inflation.
