Salary vs Hourly Pay: What’s the Difference?

Salary and hourly pay describe different ways of quoting compensation. A salary is usually stated as an annual amount, while hourly pay is based on a rate for each hour worked. The useful comparison is not just the label—it is the effective pay for the schedule, benefits and rules that apply to the job.

How to compare salary and hourly pay

Start by converting both offers to the same time basis. For a standard example, 40 hours per week for 52 weeks equals 2,080 annual hours. A $52,000 salary divided by 2,080 is $25 per hour. In the other direction, $25 per hour × 40 × 52 is $52,000 per year.

Then adjust the assumptions if the job has a different schedule. A role averaging 45 hours per week should not be compared with a 40-hour role as though the time commitment were identical.

What else matters besides the base rate?

Compensation may include bonuses, employer retirement contributions, paid time off, health benefits or other employer-paid items. A higher base salary can still be a weaker package if another offer provides meaningful additional compensation or requires fewer hours.

Salary does not automatically mean overtime-exempt

Being paid a salary is not, by itself, the same as being exempt from overtime requirements. Federal rules use specific tests, and job titles alone do not determine exempt status. State law may also provide different or additional protections.

For official federal guidance, see the U.S. Department of Labor salary-basis fact sheet.

When each pay structure can be useful

Hourly pay can make it easier to see exactly how schedule changes affect gross earnings. Salary can make budgeting easier when pay is consistent across pay periods. Neither structure guarantees a better job; the practical comparison is the amount of pay, expected hours, overtime treatment, benefits and stability of the role.

Salary vs Hourly FAQ

Is salary better than hourly pay?

Neither is automatically better. The better fit depends on the pay level, schedule, overtime rules, benefits, predictability and flexibility of the specific job.

How do I convert salary to hourly pay?

Divide annual salary by the annual work hours you want to assume. A common example is 40 hours per week × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours.

How do I convert hourly pay to salary?

Multiply the hourly rate by weekly hours and then by working weeks per year. For example, $25 × 40 × 52 = $52,000.

Does being salaried automatically mean no overtime?

No. Pay method alone does not determine overtime status. Federal rules look at specific requirements, and state rules can differ.
