Credit Utilization Ratio: Definition and Formula

Learn the credit utilization ratio definition and formula, how balances and limits affect it, and when a calculator is useful for the arithmetic.

Credit utilization ratio definition

Credit utilization compares a revolving balance with the available credit limit. For example, a $300 balance on a $1,000 limit represents 30% utilization on that account. A person can also look at total balances and total limits across accounts, but credit scoring models and lenders may use information differently.

Credit utilization ratio formula

What to check

Utilization can change when a statement closes, when a payment posts or when a credit limit changes. It is not a fee or a required target. Avoid treating a single percentage as a guarantee of approval or a particular credit score. The account agreement and credit report provide the facts for your accounts.

Use the right information

Review the balance, credit limit, statement date and payment status separately. A lower balance is not the same as a higher limit, and a balance transfer can shift utilization between accounts.

Official reference

The CFPB explains that using too much of a credit limit may hurt a credit score and cautions consumers not to get too close to the limit. Read the source.

Frequently asked questions

What is a credit utilization ratio calculator?

It divides a balance by a credit limit and multiplies by 100. Use it to check the arithmetic; it does not estimate a credit score.

What should I use for an account-specific answer?

Use the current account agreement, statement or official plan document. General terms do not override those records.

Credit utilization definition and ratio

Credit utilization is the reported revolving balance divided by the corresponding credit limit, expressed as a percentage. Overall utilization combines balances and limits, while per-card utilization measures one account. Reporting dates can make the report ratio different from today account balance.

Related questions

What is the credit utilization ratio formula?

Reported revolving balance divided by credit limit, multiplied by 100.

Is 30% a required cutoff?

No universal cutoff guarantees a score result. Lower reported utilization can be associated with lower revolving debt use, but models and files differ.
