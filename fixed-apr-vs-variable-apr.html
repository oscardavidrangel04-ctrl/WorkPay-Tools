Fixed APR vs Variable APR

A fixed APR generally remains the same until a permitted change; a variable APR can move with an index or other stated reference.

Fixed, variable and introductory APR compared

A fixed APR does not automatically move with an index. A variable APR can change as the contract's reference index changes. Neither label tells you the full borrowing cost or whether an introductory offer will expire.

Rate structure and promotional period answer different questions
Term in an offerQuestion it answersWhat to check next
Fixed APRDoes the rate track an index automatically?How long it is fixed and what changes the agreement permits.
Variable APRHow can the rate move with its reference index?Index, margin, adjustment dates, floor and cap.
Introductory APRWhat rate applies during a limited period?End date and the regular rate afterward.

For example, a 0% introductory card offer can change to a variable APR after the promotion. The initial 0% rate does not make it a permanently fixed-rate account. CFPB guidance distinguishes fixed and variable APRs.

Compare the rate structure, not just the starting APR

This guide compares fixed and variable rate structures. For card-specific disclosures and balance considerations, use fixed vs variable credit card APR.

  • Fixed: the APR does not automatically track an index. Check the agreement for permitted changes.
  • Variable: identify the stated index and margin, then check when changes take effect.

Index plus margin: a simple example

If an agreement uses an 8% index plus a 12 percentage-point margin, the resulting APR is 20%. If that index becomes 7%, the same calculation gives 19%. This illustration assumes no floor, cap or other adjustment; the agreement controls the actual rate.

A rate comparison is not a total-cost estimate. Fees, balances and payment timing also matter.

Questions to ask before choosing a rate structure

  1. When can the rate change? Check whether the quoted rate lasts for the whole agreement or only an introductory period.
  2. What controls the change? For a variable rate, identify the index, margin, adjustment dates and any floor or cap.
  3. How does a change affect the payment? Ask whether the required payment changes, the payoff time changes, or both.
  4. What costs sit outside the rate? Compare fees and repayment terms on the same borrowing amount and schedule.

A lower starting variable rate is not a forecast of a lower lifetime cost. A fixed rate offers a more predictable rate structure, but the meaning of “fixed” depends on the product and contract. For the credit-card distinction, see the CFPB explanation of fixed and variable APRs.

Keep the interest rate and APR distinct when comparing loans: an APR can reflect certain finance charges as well as interest. The index-plus-margin illustration above describes the stated rate mechanism, not a universal method for calculating every loan's disclosed APR.
