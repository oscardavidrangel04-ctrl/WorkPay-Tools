Credit Card APR vs Interest Rate: Are They the Same?

For credit cards, APR generally means the annual interest rate. It is a rate, not the dollar amount of interest charged on your statement.

Credit card APR vs interest charges: the key distinction

TermMeaning
Interest rate / APRThe annualized rate for a particular balance type.
Interest chargeThe dollar cost calculated using the balance, rate and timing.
Account feeA separate charge, such as an annual or late fee.

Unlike many loan APR comparisons, a credit card’s advertised APR generally does not roll annual fees or transaction fees into a single all-in rate. Check the fee schedule as well as the APR when comparing cards.

A simplified daily interest example

Assume a constant $1,000 balance, a 24% APR, a 365-day divisor and 30 interest-bearing days. A simplified estimate is $1,000 × 0.24 ÷ 365 × 30, or about $19.73. This is an illustrative estimate, not an issuer’s bill: daily balances, compounding, different divisors and rounding can change the result.

The credit card interest calculator helps explore an estimate. Use your statement’s calculation method to check an actual charge.

Why one card can have several APRs

Purchase APR

This rate applies to purchases when interest is charged. An eligible purchase grace period may let you avoid interest by paying the required balance in full on time.

Cash advance and balance transfer APRs

These balance types can have different rates and separate fees. A cash advance generally does not receive the purchase grace period. Compare purchase APR vs cash advance APR.

Promotional and variable APRs

A promotional rate expires under its offer terms. A variable rate can change with its index. Check the current rate, ending date and applicable balance type rather than using an old advertisement.

Frequently asked questions

Are credit card APR and interest rate usually the same?

Yes. A credit card APR generally expresses its interest rate on an annual basis.

Does 24% APR mean I pay 24% interest every month?

No. It is an annualized rate; a monthly interest charge depends on daily balances, timing and the account’s calculation method.

Does APR include a credit card annual fee?

Generally no. Check the fee schedule separately from the APR.
